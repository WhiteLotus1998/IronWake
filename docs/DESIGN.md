# IRONWAKE — Design Document

Working title: **Ironwake**. A turn-based tactics game in the Fire Emblem: Three Houses lineage — grid battles, a small cast you grow, class paths, permadeath with a rewind — but its own game, not a clone. No Fire Emblem names, places, characters, or text anywhere in the repo.

This document is the source of truth, and it belongs to both partners (Chat and Code — see CLAUDE.md). Code that disagrees with it is wrong; if the document is wrong, fix the document in the same PR and record why in `docs/DECISIONS/`. Sections 1–12 are the floor. Section 13 is where the game gets interesting, and it is meant to be rewritten as we learn what's fun.

## 1. Identity

**Premise.** Ironwake is a crumbling border keep at the seam of three rival regions. An armistice is fraying. The keep trains *wardens* — and this year, because the crown stopped paying, the three regions' cadets were dumped into one under-funded cohort. The player is the new captain of that cohort. The cast's frictions and friendships are the story; the fraying armistice is the campaign.

**Pillars, in priority order.**
1. *Every turn is a decision.* If a random legal move wins as often as a thoughtful one, the map is broken.
2. *Numbers are honest.* The combat forecast shows exactly what the resolver will do. No hidden modifiers.
3. *Growth you feel.* Units level, certify into classes, and change what they can do on the map.
4. *Loss has weight, rewind has cost.* Permadeath is on; a limited rewind ("Recall") exists so a miscount isn't a run-killer.
5. *Small cast, big personalities.* 10 recruits + captain for the whole campaign. Depth over breadth.

**Out of scope for v1.** Monastery/calendar, romance, cooking, fishing, voice, animation, any graphics beyond the console. Rendering front-end (Godot 4 .NET, DECISIONS/0008) comes after the core is done and the campaign is fun in the console.

## 2. Architecture (non-negotiable)

- **Headless core.** `Ironwake.Core` contains all rules. It has no dependency on Console, System.Random, file IO, or time. Everything it needs is injected (`IRng`, content passed in).
- **Immutable state.** `BattleState` is an immutable record. The only way to change it is `Resolver.Apply(state, command) -> (BattleState next, IReadOnlyList<GameEvent> events)`. Events are the log; the CLI renders from events, never from diffing state.
- **Determinism.** Same seed + same command sequence = same state, byte for byte. RNG draws happen in a fixed, documented order. This is tested.
- **Rewind is free.** Because states are immutable, Recall is "pop the history stack." Replays are "re-apply the command list." Every bug report is a seed + command list.
- **Content is data.** Classes, weapons, terrain, units, and maps are JSON/text files under `/content`, validated on load with clear error messages. No stats in code.

## 3. Units and stats

Nine stats, integers: **HP, Str, Mag, Dex, Spd, Lck, Def, Res, Cha**. Cha exists from day one. Its jobs are the lean of the Table's sixth round, both spikes and both content: the captain's Cha scales Commander's Word (13.2, issue 85), and a recruit's Cha sets the rate rapport accrues (13.1, issue 16). Battalions, the job this section first named, keep their place in section 12 with their shape open. Each unit has base stats, per-stat growth rates (%), a level (1–30), EXP (0–99), a class, an inventory (5 slots), and a set of abilities.

Classes define: movement type (Infantry, Cavalry, Flying, Armored), Mov, stat modifiers, usable weapon types, class growth modifiers, and one mastery ability (Phase 3). Movement types matter for terrain cost and for effective damage. **Certification (issue 72):** a class may name what it asks of a unit certifying into it, a minimum level, a minimum rank per named weapon type and a minimum per named stat, read against the unit's own stats, not its current class's. A unit that meets them certifies, with no roll; one that does not is refused with every failed requirement named (`needs lance D, has E`). Certifying changes the class and nothing else, so stats follow the new class's modifiers and mastery points and mastered abilities stay. The price, a seal from the campaign's purse, is the between-map screen's (issue 74); a class with a certification trial may be earned by passing it instead (issue 252, section 13.6). The shipped ladder (DECISIONS/0058, provisional) is climbed from the cadet: the cadet asks nothing; pikeman, reaver, bowman, adept and chaplain ask level 3, and outrider, skyrider and bulwark level 4; a class whose weapon a cadet trains asks a rank in it (pikeman lance D, reaver axe D, outrider sword D, skyrider lance D and spd 8), and one it cannot train asks a stat (bowman dex 8, adept mag 6, chaplain mag 4 and res 4, bulwark def 5). The between-map screen's `classes [unit]` prints the ladder and what a unit still lacks for each class.

Level up at 100 EXP. Each stat rolls independently against its growth (unit growth + class modifier). Level cap 30 for v1. Growth rolls are keyed on (unit, new level, stat) and nothing else (issue 31), so a recruit's trajectory is fixed at the campaign seed: no Recall changes what a recruit gets at level 7, and a recruit's level-ups are identical whether or not another recruit is deployed, which gate 4's ablation needs. Keying on map or turn as well would reopen laundering by waiting a turn and break the gate. A single starved stat on one recruit is a character, not a defect; the Sim shows the spread before anyone argues for a change.

## 4. Terrain

Square grid, 4-connected movement. No zone of control. Units may pass through allies, never through enemies, and may not end on an occupied tile.

| Tile | Glyph | Infantry | Cavalry | Flying | Armored | Avoid | Def/Res | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plain | `.` | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | |
| Road | `=` | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | reserved for later cav bonus |
| Forest | `^` | 2 | 3 | 1 | 2 | +20 | +1 Def | |
| Hill | `n` | 2 | 3 | 1 | 3 | +10 | +1 Def | |
| Mountain | `M` | 3 | — | 1 | — | +30 | +2 Def | |
| Water | `~` | — | — | 1 | — | 0 | 0 | |
| Fort | `F` | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | +15 | +2 Def, +2 Res | heals 20% max HP at start of owner's phase |
| Wall | `#` | — | — | — | — | — | — | blocks everything, including flyers |
| Gate/Throne | `T` | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | +30 | +3 Def, +3 Res | seize target; heals 20% |

`—` means impassable. Terrain avoid/def do not apply to flyers except on Fort/Throne.

**Reach.** A unit's reachable tiles are every tile whose cheapest entry cost, summed along a 4-connected path, is at most its Mov; the budget is strict, so a tile costing more than what remains is not entered. Tiles an ally stands on may be crossed but not ended on; tiles an enemy stands on are never entered. The unit's own tile is always a destination, since Move is optional (section 7), and it must be a tile the unit's movement type can enter: a query from one it cannot, a footman on a wall or a rider in water, is refused with an error naming the tile, the terrain, and the movement type, never answered, because the loader will not place a unit there and a unit there anyway is a corrupt state (issue 42). Which of two equal-cost paths a unit walks is part of the rules, because renderers show it: tiles settle in (cost, then row-major) order, and a tile's path changes only when a strictly cheaper one is found. Read as a rule about paths: a tile's path arrives through the row-major-first of its neighbours whose cost plus the tile's entry cost equals the tile's cost, so the path to every reachable tile is derivable from the map alone. The order in which a tile's neighbours are enumerated does no work and is not part of the contract (issue 45). `Movement.Reach` is the one function that answers this; the CLI, the AI, and every renderer ask it rather than counting for themselves (DECISIONS/0012).

## 5. Combat

3H-inspired, but these are Ironwake's formulas. All division is integer floor.

```
Burden        = max(0, WeaponWeight - Str)
AttackSpeed   = Spd - Burden
Doubles       if AttackSpeed >= TargetAttackSpeed + 4

Atk           = Str + Mt   (physical)   |  Mag + Mt   (magic)
Effective     = Mt * 3 before adding Str/Mag, when weapon tag matches target movement type
Damage        = max(0, Atk - (Def | Res) - TerrainDefBonus)
Crit damage   = Damage * 3

Hit           = WeaponHit + Dex + Lck / 2
Avoid (phys)  = 2 * AttackSpeed + Lck / 2 + TerrainAvoid
Avoid (magic) = (Spd + Lck) / 2 + TerrainAvoid
HitChance     = clamp(Hit - Avoid, 0, 100)

Crit          = WeaponCrit + (Dex + Lck) / 2
CritAvoid     = Lck + modifiers
CritChance    = clamp(Crit - CritAvoid, 0, 100)
```

Only `CritChance` is clamped. `CritAvoid` is not: a modifier (rivalry in 13.1 is the first) may push it below zero, and a crit of 3 against a crit avoid of -5 is 8 percent. A defensive clamp on `CritAvoid` would silently erase every such cost against the low-crit weapons that make up maps 1 and 2, so a test falsifies it.

**Why burden and avoid read this way (issue 158, DECISIONS/0028).** Until the keep, burden was weight less `Str / 5` and speed counted once in avoid; an iron weapon then cost a Str 5 to 9 unit 4 to 7 speed, avoid on plain was 1 to 7 for everyone, and raw hits sat at 93 to 100 both ways, so the forecast was arithmetic and doubling followed weapon weight. Burden against the whole of Str gives speed back its job, speed counting twice makes Spd the skirmisher's stat, and the iron tier's hit is 15 lower in content (Iron Sword 75, Iron Lance 70, Iron Axe 65, Iron Bow 70), which together put the player's raw hit on open ground at about 60 to 80 and the enemy's at 50 to 70, with forest and fort visibly below. The fort's avoid is 15, not 30, for the same reason: at 30 under these formulas a boss on a fort was a lottery (Design Table, twenty-fourth round). The Sim's `--hitband` prints the raw-hit histogram of both sides, per defender terrain, beside the doubling rate and gates 1 and 4, under both roll schemes, as a standing number.

**Rolls.** Hit uses the average of two rolls (0–99) by default: a strike lands when `floor((A + B) / 2) < HitChance`, so the landing probability is the share of the 10000 ordered pairs whose sum is below `2 * HitChance` (a raw 50 lands 50.5 percent of the time). Under one roll it lands when `A < HitChance`. Crit uses one roll, `C < CritChance`, drawn only when the hit landed. Every roll is derived from the campaign seed and a key, never from a stream position (issue 31): `IRng.Roll(key)` is the engine's only source of chance, and the key tuples are the contract that replaced draw order. A combat roll keys on `(turn, phase, attacker, target, strikeIndex, roll)` with `roll` one of `HitA`, `HitB`, `Crit`; the counter's strikes key with the defender as the attacker, and `strikeIndex` counts that striker's own strikes in the combat from 0. A growth roll keys on `(unit, newLevel, stat)` and nothing else (section 3). The production `IRng` hashes the key's text with the seed (FNV-1a and a 64-bit finalizer, written out in `KeyedRng`, DECISIONS/0013), so the same seed and key give the same roll in every process; the two pinned rolls in `KeyedRngTests` make a change to the hash a visible decision, since it changes every seed's game.

**Display.** The forecast shows the *resolved* probability of whatever roll scheme is in use, rounded to the nearest integer, never the raw hit stat. Under two-roll averaging a raw 75 lands 87.75% of the time and displays as 88; a raw 30 lands 18.3% and displays as 18. Forecast, resolver, and AI scorer all call one hit-probability function; the roll scheme is a switch inside it. One roll versus two was settled by the hit A/B on the cast (Design Table, ninth round; DECISIONS/0023): two-roll averaging, because under it who you brought explains more of the outcome and the dice less (gate 4's median drop as a share of the baseline's wins, 95 against 77 percent on Old Mill Road and 82 against 59 on Saltmarsh Ford, paired over 200 seeds), and fights are shorter. The terrain reason for one roll did not survive the arithmetic: two-roll averaging makes forest's +20 worth less than printed only above raw 85 (11 points at 96 to 76) and more below it (19 at 86 to 66, 33 at 68 to 48), and the cast's median matchup is raw 86. `--scheme one` stays in the Sim and the CLI as the Fun Gate's first dial if the journals on maps 1 to 3 say the turns read as arithmetic rather than risk (issue 15). The CLI prints the forecast line before every attack it resolves, the enemy phase's included (issue 152), so the odds behind every hit are on the transcript and a reader can tell a misplay from bad luck; the enemy's line is asked of the core on the board the resolver is about to read, the same query the player's `forecast` command uses. The other half of the killbox is `threat <unit> [from <x,y>]` (issue 217): for a unit on its tile or one it can still move to, every enemy that could strike it on the coming enemy phase, each with the weapon, tile and forecast the planner would use were it to choose that unit (the planner's own options restricted to that target, on the board the exposure sum prices with the player phase ended), then the total if every strike lands. The lines are independent, but the total is not (issue 253): it is the worst case over assignments of enemies to distinct strike tiles, one enemy per tile, so two enemies whose only way in is the same tile count once, the harder hitter. Each line reads the phase-start board; an earlier enemy's move or kill is not played out, so the list is what can come, not what will.

**Sequence.** Attacker strikes; defender counters if the attacker is within the defender's weapon range and the defender has a usable weapon; then whoever doubles strikes again. Combat ends early on death. Each of those turns is a *round*: one strike, or two for gauntlets (issue 70), each rolling hit and crit on its own, with a death ending the combat mid-round; so a doubling gauntlet user lands four strikes and a doubling sword user two. The forecast shows every strike (`x2`, `x4`) and section 8 prices them the same way. Brave weapons are not built.

**Weapon types.** Sword, Lance, Axe, Bow, Reason (black magic, targets Res), Faith (white magic: heal and a few attacks). Gauntlets (issue 70): physical, range 1 only, targets Def, and spend one use per combat rather than per strike; Iron Gauntlets (Mt 2, Hit 85, Crit 0, Wt 2, 40 uses) and Steel Gauntlets (Mt 4, Hit 80, Crit 0, Wt 5, 30 uses) ship, no crit because four strikes fish for it; two strikes out-damage one iron-sword strike only while the target's Def is under the wielder's Str less one, so they lose to armour. The reaver wields them beside the axe and Keziah carries a pair (DECISIONS/0048). There is **no weapon triangle**; Phase 3 adds Breaker abilities (e.g. Lancebreaker: +20 hit/avoid vs lances) as the counterplay layer. Weapons have Mt, Hit, Crit, Wt, Range (min–max), Durability, and optional effective tags.

**Abilities (issue 66).** An ability is data in `content/abilities.json`: an id, a name, one line of text, and one effect from a closed set, never a handler per ability, so the forecast, the resolver and both planners price it through the functions above without knowing it by name. The set today is a *passive* flat stat delta, added after the class modifiers (max HP included), and an *on-combat* modifier of hit, avoid, crit and crit avoid against opponents matching an optional weapon type and movement type, and only while the holder strikes or counters with an optional weapon type of its own (`wielding`, issue 245), added inside the hit and crit chances before their clamps. *Canto (issue 71)* is the after-action kind, with no numbers; range extension joins the set with the issue that needs it. A class may list `abilities` that every unit in it holds while in the class and loses on leaving it, which is how the outrider carries Canto. A unit's `abilities`, a class's `abilities` and a class's `mastery` must name entries of the file. **Breakers (issue 69)** are seven such entries, one per weapon type (Fistbreaker for gauntlets, issue 70), +20 hit and +20 avoid against it, on the strike and the counter alike; no unit starts with one, and three are class masteries. **Mastery (issue 69):** a class that names a `mastery` also names `masteryPoints`, and a player unit earns one point per combat it fights in its current class, as attacker or defender, alive at the end, and one point per healing spell it casts on an ally in that class and the resolver accepts (issue 245; a consumable earns nothing); at the requirement the ability joins the unit's own `abilities` with a `masteryEarned` event, so it is kept after a class change, and points are kept per class. Deterministic, no roll; enemies earn nothing. Each of the nine starter classes masters one ability (DECISIONS/0047): cadet Fundamentals (+1 Str, Spd, Def), pikeman Horsebane (+20 hit, +10 crit against cavalry), reaver Bloodrush (+15 crit, -10 avoid, while wielding an axe), bowman Deadeye (+10 hit, +10 crit), adept Deep Study (+2 Mag), chaplain Grace (+10 avoid, +20 crit avoid), outrider Swordbreaker, skyrider Bowbreaker, bulwark Reasonbreaker; the requirement is 12 points for every class, a heal counting as a combat (issue 245), so a front-liner masters its class on its second map.

**Ranges.** Melee 1; Bows 2 (2–3 with Phase 3 abilities); Reason/Faith 1–2. A thrown melee weapon reaches 1–2 and is enemy-only in v1: no cast member carries one, so the player's melee stays at 1 and a throw is something to read on the other side (the Toll Axe, issue 131; the Tollgate's door warden carries the Toll Spear, a 1-2 lance, so no tile outside that keep strikes it unanswered, issue 181). A unit with only a 2-range weapon cannot counter at 1, and vice versa.

**Durability.** Physical weapons have uses and break at 0 (broken weapons stay in inventory, usable at −5 Mt / −10 hit as a "broken" fallback so units are never helpless; repair comes with the between-map screen). Every strike a unit makes spends one use, landed or not, so a swing costs the same whether it hits (issue 9). Spells have uses per battle that fully refresh each map; a spell at 0 is not cast again this battle, and a unit whose only weapon is a spent spell is unarmed until the next map, since the broken fallback is for steel, not for words (DECISIONS/0018). A healer whose attacking spell is spent is conditional rather than idle: the healing spell still casts wherever an ally in range is hurt, which is why the cast rule in section 9 asks for one attacking spell and not for two of anything.

**Combat arts (issue 68).** An art is an ability whose effect kind is `art`: a weapon type, the rank it needs (section 6), an extra durability `cost` of at least one use, and deltas to Mt, Hit, Crit, Wt and optionally range (never negative). A unit knows the arts its `abilities` list. It declares one with the attack command, before the roll (`attack <unit> <target> [slot] [art <id>]`), and the weapon strikes as the art makes it: the deltas are added to the weapon, Mt and Wt floored at zero and the far end of the range extended, so the forecast, the resolver, burden, doubling and the attacker's own avoid against the counter all read it through the functions above. A Wt delta can therefore cost a fast unit its double, and a heavier weapon lowers its avoid against the counter. The art's cost is spent once per declared attack, on top of one use per strike, whether the strike lands or not: an art free on a miss would be strictly better than the plain attack whenever it is legal, and then it is not a choice. The forecast prints the art's numbers and the most uses the attack can spend. An art is refused when the unit does not know it, the weapon is another type, the unit's rank is below the art's, the weapon is broken, or the weapon has fewer uses left than the art's cost and the first strike (provisional reading of "a weapon at 0 uses cannot pay it", on #68's PR). Only an attack declares an art, so a counter never carries one: a counter the player did not choose must not spend a weapon the player is saving. The enemy planner declares none until content gives an enemy an art. `abilities.json` ships no art yet; which arts exist and their numbers are the Table's.

**Items.** A unit's five slots hold weapons, spells, and consumables; the equipped weapon is the first slot holding a usable weapon that is not a healing spell, and an Attack may name another usable slot, which moves to the front so the counter uses it too; choosing costs nothing extra (issue 99). Consumables live in `items.json` (`id`, `name`, `heals`, `uses`); the first is the Field Dressing (heals 10, 3 uses), an original name, since the content standards forbid franchise names. Section 7's Item action uses one slot: a consumable heals its user and needs no target; a healing spell heals one ally within its range. Either spends a use and ends the action; a consumable at 0 leaves the inventory. Healing a unit already at full HP is refused, so no script burns a use on nothing, and the legal-command list offers an item use only where something would heal.

**Healing.** Faith heal = Mag / 2 + 5 + spell base. Healer gains 11 EXP per heal, +5 if the target was below 50%.

## 6. EXP

```
On any strike landed:  Exp = clamp(10 + 2 * (EnemyLevel - UnitLevel), 1, 30)
On a kill (added):     Exp = clamp(20 + 3 * (EnemyLevel - UnitLevel), 5, 70)
Bosses:                kill bonus +20
```

A unit earns EXP once per combat, computed from the best outcome in that combat: the strike line when any of its strikes landed, plus the kill line when the other unit died, plus the boss bonus; nothing landed earns nothing. Only player units earn EXP; enemies are templates that do not outlive the map, and their numbers never change mid-battle behind a forecast the player already read (issue 8, DECISIONS/0017). Level up at 100, carrying the remainder; a combat is worth at most 120, so it can cross two levels and the engine handles that. At level 30 nothing is awarded and no event is emitted; a level-up that reaches 30 discards the remainder. Growth rolls are the section 3 keys, `(unit, new level, stat)`, tested against the effective growth clamped to 0..100, the same growth `AtLevel` scales by, so a rolled level-up equals the deterministic scaling in expectation. An HP gain raises current HP by the same amount. Two events carry it, `ExpGained` and `LeveledUp` (which stats rose), after the combat and before any death. Healer EXP (section 5) is a formula without a caller until issue 9's heal action.

**Weapon skill ranks (issue 67, provisional numbers).** A unit carries rank points per weapon type; its rank is the highest threshold reached: E 0, D 30, C 80, B 160, A 280, S 450. Every weapon names the rank it needs (`rank` in `weapons.json`, required), and a unit equips only weapons at or below its rank in the type. A player unit that struck with its weapon in a combat earns 3 points in that type, 5 if the combat killed, once per combat like EXP; a heal earns 3 in Faith; a unit that made no strike earns nothing. Enemy templates declare `ranks` in content (letters, each at its threshold), nothing scales them with `enemy_level`, and a template carrying a weapon above its declared rank is a content error. No roll touches a rank. Every shipped weapon needs E until the Table sets the ladder.

## 7. Turn structure

Player phase → Enemy phase → (Ally phase if any) → turn counter increments. On a unit's turn: **Move** (optional), then one of **Attack / Item / Wait**. Cavalry get **Canto** (issue 71): after an Attack, Item or Wait, a unit with Canto moves again on its Mov minus the path cost its Move spent, through the section 4 reach from where it stands, and is then done. Its own tile is a legal Canto, so declining is a command the transcript shows; an untaken Canto lapses when its phase ends. The wake check runs after every command (section 8), the Move included, so a Canto cannot ride out of a proximity wake its own Move stop already fired; what it can escape is a wake it has not yet caused: its strike's noise wakes the group on the Attack, and the Canto then rides away before that group's phase. The enemy AI does not take one.

**Win conditions** (one per map): Rout, Seize (captain on `T`), Defeat Boss, Survive N turns, Escape (the captain leaves through an exit tile; issue 269).
**Loss conditions:** captain dies; or a map-specific protect-target dies (the `protect:` header, section 10).

**Outcome.** `BattleState.Outcome` is computed from the board after every command, never stored, and read in this order: captain dead (lost), protected recruit dead or left behind (lost), the win condition met (won), the turn past `turn_limit` (won for Survive, lost for everything else), else ongoing. Seize is the captain standing on a throne tile; Escape is the captain having exited (below); Rout is no enemy alive; Defeat Boss is no boss alive. A decided battle refuses every command but Recall, so a loss is the moment Recall is for.

**Exit** (issue 269; DECISIONS/0056). On an Escape map a player unit standing on an exit tile may take `exit` as its action, in place of Attack, Item or Wait, after its Move or without one. It is not taken after another action and no Canto follows it, so a unit strikes or leaves, never both. The unit leaves the board for the state's escaped list: nothing on the board can target it, be woken by it, or count it, and Recall undoes an exit like any other command. The captain's exit wins the battle, and every player unit still on the board at that moment is left behind, which counts as fallen in the outcome, the battle summary and the campaign (the answer death gets). A protected recruit left behind loses the battle, read where its death is. Enemies never exit. Healing terrain (section 4) heals the units of the side whose phase begins, the terrain's percent of max HP with integer floor, capped at max (issue 7).

**Recall.** 3 charges per map on Normal. Rewinds to any previous player-phase state in the current map's history; Recall is the player's action, so the enemy phase's own states are never a target, and a Recall into one is refused without spending a charge, naming the nearest player-phase states (issue 190, DECISIONS/0032). Charges do not refresh mid-map. **The Recall browser (issue 75)** prices a rewind before it is spent: `recall list` names every state a Recall may return to with its turn, the command that made it and what a rewind there undoes, the kills, EXP, levels and enemy HP given back and the units, HP and not-yet-arrived reinforcements returned, all read by one function (`RecallCost.Of`) from the difference between that state and the present; `recall n` prints the same line as it rewinds, then that the rolls do not change. The list is the confirmation: nothing is spent by reading it, and a spent charge does not come back.

**Recall restores the rolls.** The same attack after a Recall gets the same rolls; a Recall lets you choose differently, never reroll. Rolls are keyed (issue 31, section 5), so this holds at the roll level: the same strike on the same turn draws the same numbers whatever was resolved before it, and a resolver test shows it. It becomes a fact of the game once `BattleState` (issue 6) carries only the seed and no stream position; that issue's acceptance holds the three Recall tests.

## 8. Enemy AI

Every enemy belongs to a *group* with one behavior:

- `Aggressive` — moves toward and attacks the best target every turn.
- `Hold` — never moves; attacks anything in range.
- `Guard` — behaves as Hold until the group *wakes*; then the whole group becomes Aggressive. This is the main pacing tool for maps. A group wakes on any of: **proximity** (after any player command, a player unit stands within the wake radius of any member), **noise** (any combat involving a tile within the wake radius plus 2 of any member), or **death** (any member dies, at any distance). The wake radius is one global constant, 4 tiles, Manhattan distance, walls not considered; it lives in content, never in code and never in a map file, and it is identical on every map so the player learns it once and counting tiles is a skill, not a discovery. The check runs after every player command on where units stand, never on tiles passed through, so the player-facing rule is "do not stop close," not "do not get close": a unit with the movement for it may run past a sleeping group and end beyond the radius, which is a real play, countable in advance, and Cavalry's first job that is not arriving early. The dash is free only if nothing fights on the way through; the noise radius is what prices the greedy version. Waking emits an event naming the group and the cause. While any group is asleep, both board views (the opening screen and `map`) print the rule under the unit rows as one line built from the radii, `asleep: wakes if a unit ends within 4 tiles of a member, a combat happens within 6, or a member dies`, and the protocol's state carries `wakeRadius` and `noiseRadius`, so a rule the map depends on is on the screen and not only here (issue 260). The three causes are one predicate in code (`WakeCheck`), read by the resolver after every command and by the exposure sum of section 11 on the board a plan certainly produces, so the veto and the rules cannot drift apart (issue 128). There are no trigger rectangles. The radius is `wakeRadius` in `content/rules.json` (issue 10), the file for rule constants that belong to no map; the loader refuses a negative one. The check runs inside the resolver after every accepted command on either phase, so a woken group is a fact of the board that a Recall restores with the rest: on the enemy phase only noise and a death can newly fire, since neither player units nor sleeping members move, and a group woken mid enemy phase acts as Aggressive from the next member in id order. One event per group per command, naming the loudest cause in the order death, noise, proximity.
- `Boss` — Hold, plus never leaves its tile.

A boss is any enemy on a `B` line, and its behavior is `Boss` unless the line says `behavior:guard` (issue 259, DECISIONS/0055): a guard boss sleeps as Hold on its own tile, wakes by the three causes like any Guard group, and once awake acts as Aggressive, so it may leave its tile and may end on a throne. It is still the boss for Defeat Boss, the kill bonus and the `!` glyph, and the console names it `boss, asleep` or `boss, awake`. A Seize boss that can never act before the captain arrives is scenery; a guard boss is how a Seize map gives its boss a turn.

The AI is a planner, never a mutator: `EnemyAi.Plan(state, content)` returns the enemy phase's command list, ending in `EndPhase` unless the battle was decided on the way, and the caller applies it through the same resolver the planner used on its working copy, so the two agree by construction. A unit's plan is at most a Move then an Attack or a Wait. Hold, Boss, and a sleeping Guard consider only their own tile; a woken Guard is Aggressive.

Target scoring, deterministic (ties broken by lowest unit id):
```
score = 100 if the attack can kill
      + expectedDamageDealt * hitChance/100
      - expectedDamageTaken * counterHitChance/100 * 0.5
      + 10 if the target cannot counter
      + 5  if the target is a healer
```
`expectedDamage` on both lines includes the crit expectation, `Damage * (1 + 2 * CritChance / 100)`, times the strikes that side makes (2 when it doubles), capped at the HP it could remove, using the same functions the forecast uses. The whole score is a double, since the section 5 convention of integer floor would make `2 * CritChance / 100` zero for every crit under 50 and delete the term in the band it exists for. "Can kill" reads deterministic damage times strikes against remaining HP, never the expectation, so an attack lethal only on a crit is never priced as a kill (issue 10, Chat's two hazards, each held by a test). The enemy prices what the player sees, so a modifier that lowers a unit's crit avoid (13.1) makes that unit a better target in the enemy's own arithmetic rather than a cost the enemy is blind to. "Cannot counter" is the target having no weapon that reaches back at that distance; a healer is a unit carrying a healing spell its class can use.

Tile choice: among tiles that allow the best-scoring attack, prefer highest terrain avoid, then fewest player units that can reach the tile next phase, then (cost from the mover, row-major) so the answer is unique. The score is taken per (target, tile), since the mover's terrain changes the counter it takes, and a tie in score goes to the lower target id before any tile key. "Can reach the tile" means the tile is in that player unit's reach set as section 4 defines it, computed once per plan on the board before the mover moves, which is what `ironwake reach` prints, so the count can be checked by hand.

**Weapon choice.** An enemy's attack options range over every weapon it can strike with, in inventory order: each (tile, weapon, target) is scored by the formula above on the unit with that weapon in front, and the winning option's Attack names its slot under section 5's Items rule (issue 99), so the weapon it last swung is the one it counters with. An Attack with the equipped weapon names no slot. An option equal to the best on the whole order does not replace it, so of two weapons scoring the same the earlier slot strikes. Hold and Boss still consider only their own tile; the approach rule reads the equipped weapon (issue 174). The exposure sum of section 11 ranges over the same weapons: each enemy is counted at the worst of every weapon it can strike with, from every tile that weapon reaches the unit from, so the heuristic never stands at range 2 of a boss it believes has no reach there; the counter to the unit's own attack is the weapon the target holds in front, the one it last swung (issue 131). The bandit leader carries the Steel Axe in front and the enemy-only Toll Axe (1-2) behind it: adjacent to a cadet the Steel Axe scores higher, so the throw is what he does at range 2, and after a throw he counters at 1 and 2 until he swings steel again.

**Approach.** The tile rule above is scoped to tiles that allow an attack, so it says nothing about a turn on which an Aggressive unit can attack nobody, and "moves toward" is not an algorithm. The approach is a rule for the same reason the path tie-break is (DECISIONS/0012): a debug prediction written from this document and the implementation must arrive at the same tile, so the choice cannot be an accident of the code (Design Table, fifth round). When no reachable tile allows any attack:

- **Target:** the player unit whose nearest *attack tile* (a tile the mover can end on, from which its equipped weapon reaches that unit) has the lowest path cost from the mover's own tile, computed with section 4's costs and occupancy and no Mov budget. Ties by lowest unit id, section 8's existing tie-break. A unit with no path to any attack tile is not a target; a mover with no target waits where it stands.
- **Destination:** among the mover's reachable tiles (section 4, own tile included), the one with the lowest remaining path cost to any attack tile on the chosen target. Ties by highest terrain avoid, then fewest player units whose reach set contains the tile, then (cost from the mover's tile, then row-major), DECISIONS/0012's contract as the final key, so the answer is unique. Path cost throughout, never Manhattan, so terrain means the same thing to the approach as to everything else.
- **Order:** enemy units act in ascending unit id, each on the board as the one before it left it. The remaining-cost query is `Movement.DistancesTo`, one multi-source Dijkstra on the reversed graph over the same costs and occupancy as `Reach`, so nothing re-counts; an attack tile is one the mover's movement type can enter, occupied by nobody but the mover, within its equipped weapon's range of the target.

On Old Mill Road as placed, this sends the brigand at 6,5 to 3,6 on enemy phase 1: 3,6, 4,7 and 5,8 all sit at remaining cost 2 to an attack tile on the recruit at 2,8, all Plain, all inside both player units' reach, all at cost 4 from 6,5, and row-major takes y=6. Issue 10 holds that case as a table-driven test named for this rule; a determinism test (the phase replays for the same seed) does not protect it, because a hash-ordered pick among the three replays too.

## 9. Content plan for v1

- **Cast.** Captain (fixed sword unit, Cha-heavy) + 10 recruits, 3–4 from each region, covering every weapon type and movement type at least once. Each recruit has a one-line personality, a home region, and two people they get on with (support hooks for Phase 3). Names are original. A Reason or Faith recruit carries at least two castable spells, at least one of them an attacking spell: a spent spell does not equip, and a healing spell is legal only when an ally in range is hurt, so two healing spells leave the unit with Move and Wait on a healthy turn (issue 113; the loader refuses the entry).
- **Campaign.** 8 maps, escalating: 2 tutorials-in-disguise (small maps, one new idea each), 4 mid maps introducing Guard groups, reinforcements, forts, and a Seize, 2 finale maps. Maps are 12×10 to 20×16. The shipped order (issue 288) is Old Mill Road, Saltmarsh Ford, the Tollgate, Harrow Weir, the raid on Ironwake, Sallow Grange, Brackwater Cut, and the keep; map 7 (#81) slots in before the keep, which is the finale #83 builds on. The keep and its raid are read from `content/keep`, every other map from `content/maps`.
- **Map authoring constraint.** Armored's job is blocking, and with no zone of control that job is binary: a one-tile corridor with a body in it is an absolute wall, a two-tile one is nothing. Maps that want the wall must draw the corridor. Archers need a back line to protect, which means forests behind a wall of two or a corridor, not a third body.
- **Difficulty.** Normal only in v1. Hard is a data change (enemy stat multipliers, fewer Recall charges) — leave the hook. The hook is in (issue 76): a `difficulties` block in `rules.json`, each entry a per-stat `statPercent` (omitted stats 100, rounded to nearest, HP at least 1), an `enemyLevelOffset` on the map's `enemy_level` (held to 1..30), and an optional `recall` count replacing the map's. `normal` must be declared and must be the identity, so Normal is data and not a code path. A map is put under a difficulty once, by `MapDefinition.Under`, and every enemy, spawns included, comes through `EnemyUnit`; nothing else asks which difficulty is in play. It is chosen once per campaign (the campaign record, issue 74) and never per map. The Sim measures one with `--full <map> --difficulty <id>`. Only `normal` ships; Hard's numbers wait on maps 1 to 3 and the Table.
- **Between maps.** Minimal: repair, shop with fixed stock, level/class screen. Text only. Built in issue 74 (DECISIONS/0051, numbers provisional): `content/campaign.json` lists the maps in campaign order, each with the `reward` its win pays into the purse and the `stock` sold on the screen before it (fixed, unlimited, a plan the player can make two maps ahead), beside the `startingPurse` and the `certificationPrice`; a weapon or item sells only with a `price`, and the loader refuses a stock entry without one. A campaign is a `CampaignRecord`: the roster in roster order as the last map left each unit (level, EXP, ranks, mastery points, weapon uses), the fallen, the purse, the next map's index, the seed, the difficulty (chosen once) and the bench. The screen before every map, the first included, buys at full uses into a free slot; repairs a weapon to full at `max(1, price / durability)` a missing use, refusing a spell (it refreshes) and a weapon without a price (the Toll weapons); certifies through issue 72's check, paying the seal from the purse, or, for a class `campaign.json`'s optional `trials` names (class id to a map under `content/trials`), through that class's trial in place of the seal (issue 252, DECISIONS/0057): `trial <unit> <class>` plays it on the screen after the same check, one attempt per unit and class per camp, a pass certifying with the level, EXP, stats and ranks its fights left and the unit's own inventory, abilities and mastery points, a failure (a fall in the trial included) changing nothing but the attempt, the trial on the campaign seed plus the map count plus the next map's index and under no difficulty; and benches, which here is a deployment choice and not gate 4's ablation: the benched unit leaves the roster passed to the battle, so the next recruit in roster order fills its bare slot, while the captain, the `protect:` recruit and a recruit the map places by name cannot be benched. Every refusal names the price and the balance, or the requirement. After a won map, a deployed unit missing from the board has fallen and leaves the roster for good, and a named slot whose recruit has fallen stays empty; survivors keep what the battle left them, spells refreshed, and the consumable uses a `supplies` cap held back are returned, since the cap is what a unit brings into the battle and the rest stays in the wagon. A lost map ends the campaign; Recall is the answer to a loss. Each map's battle runs on the campaign seed plus the map's index, since combat keys name no map and one seed would give the same strike on the same turn the same roll on every map. The console is `ironwake campaign [--seed N] [--script f] [--strict] [--difficulty id]`: the screen's commands, `march`, then every `play` command and `leave` once the battle is decided; the record is a protocol shape (`record`, `docs/PROTOCOL.md`), so a campaign is a file.

## 10. Map file format

A `.map` file is a header of `key: value` lines, then the grid, then a `units:` block, then an optional `events:` block. Glyphs per section 4. Coordinates are `x,y` from the top-left, 0-based. Header keys: `name`, `size` (`WxH`), `win` (`rout`, `seize`, `defeat_boss`, `survive`, `escape`), `turn_limit` (required; every map has one, gate 1 counts against it), `recall` (default 3), `enemy_level` (default 1), `exit` (Escape maps only: the exit tiles as `x,y` separated by spaces, at least as many as the map has player slots, so every deployed unit has one to stand on), `protect` (optional: the id of a `recruit:<id>` slot on this map whose death loses the map, section 7), the optional `cheap_shots` below, and the optional `retreat: on`, which turns on enemy retreat for the map (13.10, issue 33; DECISIONS/0037). The optional `rivalry: <arm>` turns on Rapport and Rivalry for the map (13.1, issue 16; DECISIONS/0038), the arm an id under `rivalry.arms` in `rules.json`. The optional `supplies: N` (1 to 99) caps every consumable stack (an entry of `items.json`) a deployed player unit carries at N uses when the map starts (issue 160; DECISIONS/0039). It is a cap and never a set, so a stack already under it keeps its own and a map can take supplies away but never hand out more than a unit carried; weapons, spells and enemies are untouched. The optional `announce: on` (issue 78; DECISIONS/0052) makes the console list the map's events in player words (`turn 3, enemy phase: a rider arrives at 7,0 (aggressive). A unit standing on 7,0 stops it.`, every spawn line carrying the held-tile rule; issue 256) before the first command and on `map` while any is still to fire, so a reinforcement turn is counted in advance rather than learned when it lands; a map without it keeps its events unannounced (the Tollgate's rider, DECISIONS/0045), and the header is refused on a map with no `events:` block. The optional `keepsakes: on` turns on Carry the fallen for the map (13.8, experiment): a player unit that falls leaves its weapon on its tile for an ally to `recover`, and an enemy that ends a move on it carries it off (issue 295). The optional `dusk: N` (1 to 128) makes the map a dusk map (13.7, experiment): each side's sight is N tiles on turn 1 and one less each turn, never under 1, and neither side strikes what it cannot see. The optional `certification: <class> <item> [<item> ...]` makes the map a certification trial (13.6, issue 73; DECISIONS/0049): the map has exactly one player slot, the captain's, and whoever fills it (`play --candidate <id>`, the captain by default) plays in that class carrying exactly those weapons and items at full uses, its own level, stats, ranks and mastery kept; the console prints the trial, the map's events, and on the battle's end whether the class was earned. A loadout weapon the class cannot wield is refused. The `difficulty: <id>` header is written only for a map played under a difficulty (section 9), whose `enemy_level` and `recall` already include it, so a battle state reads back under the same one; a map under `content/maps` that declares it is refused. A `P` line is `captain`, `recruit:<id>` (this recruit stands here), or bare `recruit` (a deployment slot the roster fills in order). An `E` line needs `group:` and `behavior:` (`aggressive`, `hold`, `guard`); a `B` line is a boss, and its behavior is `boss` (the default when omitted) or `guard` (section 8; issue 259), anything else refused. Example:

```
name: Old Mill Road
size: 12x10
win: rout
turn_limit: 12
recall: 3
enemy_level: 1
supplies: 1

............
..^^....n...
..^^..F.nn..
============
..~~........
..~~.....#..
.........#..
..........^^
............
............

units:
P captain 1,8
P recruit:wren 2,8
E soldier 9,1 group:mill behavior:guard
E archer 10,2 group:mill behavior:guard
E brigand 6,5 group:road behavior:aggressive
E mill_bandit 10,1 group:mill behavior:guard
E archer 8,7 group:road behavior:aggressive
```

The example is `content/maps/old_mill_road.map`, where the mill bandit is an ordinary member of the mill group and wakes with it (DECISIONS/0031) and a road archer trails the brigand so the pair reach the party on the same enemy phase (DECISIONS/0034); a `B` line reads `B bandit_leader 10,0 group:fort behavior:boss`, as on Saltmarsh Ford.

Enemy generic units are templates from `/content/units/enemies.json` scaled to the map's `enemy_level`: a template below it is raised to it (deterministically, on the effective growth of section 3 clamped per stat to 0..100, DECISIONS/0005 as amended by issue 84), and a template already at or above it keeps its own level, so a level-3 boss on a level-1 map stays level 3. Guard groups carry no trigger attribute; they wake by the section 8 rule. An optional header `cheap_shots: allowed` (maps 4 and up only) declares that the map waives gate 3 on purpose; the Sim reports the waiver rather than skipping the gate quietly.

**Map events** (issue 32, DECISIONS/0036). An optional `events:` block follows the units, one event per line as `<name> <trigger> <action>`:

```
events:
sluice enter 3,1 terrain 4,1 =
reinforce turn 3 enemy spawn brigand 9,0 group:east behavior:aggressive
alarm turn 4 player flag alarm
```

Triggers: `turn N player|enemy` fires at the start of that phase, after healing terrain heals. It needs 1 <= N <= `turn_limit`, and `turn 1 player` is refused because the battle opens in it. `enter x,y` fires when a player unit ends a Move on the tile. Passing through does not fire it, and neither does starting there or an enemy stopping there. Actions: `terrain x,y <glyph>` changes the tile's terrain, and every rule reads the new terrain from then on. `spawn <template> x,y group:<g> behavior:<b>` places an enemy from a template on a grid-edge tile, scaled like any placement, never a boss. `flag <name>` sets a flag on the battle for a later win condition to read. Names are unique on the map. Each event fires at most once per battle, in file order when several share a moment, and emits an event naming it. **A held tile blocks an event and spends it.** A spawn whose tile holds any unit, or whose template cannot stand on that tile's current terrain, does not arrive. A terrain change that would leave its occupant on ground its movement type cannot enter does not happen. So standing on a reinforcement tile is a real play, and one you can count in advance. A spawned unit is named `template-n`, with the count continuing from the map's placements of that template through the spawn events before it in file order. The file fixes the name, not the order in which events fire, so ids are stable across replays. The fired list, the flags, and the changed terrain are all part of the battle state, so a Recall restores them with the board. The resolver checks enter triggers after a Move and turn triggers after an EndPhase, both before the Guard wake check, so a spawned Guard group can wake on the command that spawned it. The sample is `docs/samples/sluice_gate.map`, which sits outside `content/maps` so that none of the gated maps moves under a pending Fun Gate entry.

The parser validates beyond the grammar (DECISIONS/0011): positions inside the grid, one unit per tile, exactly one captain, player slots on ground infantry can stand on, enemies on ground their class can enter, a throne for `seize`, a `B` line for `defeat_boss`, and no attribute other than `group` and `behavior`, and every event line as above. Errors name the file and line.

The game writes this format as well as reads it: experiment 13.5 edits the finale's `.map` between maps. The writer is canonical (fixed header order, defaults written out), so writing a parsed map gives the same text back and every file under `content/maps/` is held to that form by a test. The console view (grid with units drawn over the terrain, plus a legend) is a separate rendering for reading, since a grid with letters on it has lost the terrain under them; the legend names the terrain under each unit instead. Player units are drawn as uppercase letters in placement order, enemies lowercase, bosses `!`; on an Escape map every exit tile no unit stands on is drawn `>`, and one line under the unit rows names the exits and the rule, `exits (>): 19,3 ... (a unit on one may exit as its action; the captain's exit wins and leaves the rest behind)`, from the map's `exit:` list (issue 267), so the objective is on the screen; a reach `*` covers an exit it reaches. No starter terrain draws with `!`, `*` or `>`, and a test holds it. Any letter a terrain draws with (`F`, `M`, `T`, `n` in the starter content) is skipped on both sides, so a unit is never drawn as a tile; the skip is by the content's whole glyph set, not the map's, so a slot keeps its letter from map to map (issue 41).

## 11. Quality bar — what "not janky" means when no human is watching

The Sim harness (`Ironwake.Sim`) exists so quality is measured, not felt. Every campaign map must pass these gates before it is marked `tuned` in STATE.md:

1. **Beatable.** The heuristic AI player wins ≥ 60% of 200 seeded runs within the turn limit. The heuristic player is the Sim's own planner over section 8's score and attack tiles, per unit in id order (issue 12): the best-scoring attack from the best tile with the tile keys mirrored (exposure counted over enemy reach sets), else a heal below half HP, else the approach (section 8's rule toward the nearest enemy on Rout and Defeat Boss, toward the throne on Seize and the nearest exit on Escape, through the nearest enemy when the objective has no open path, hold on Survive). On Escape a unit that can reach an exit this turn moves to the cheapest one and exits ahead of any attack, and the captain plans last and exits only once no other player unit that has not acted can reach an exit this turn (issue 269). It knows nothing of the wake rule. A unit owed a Canto takes it before any other unit acts (issue 262): to the tile in its Canto reach with the lowest no-crit exposure sum below, which is the veto's refusal for the units it covers, then the crit key for them, then staying, then the tile keys; a recruit never Cantos onto a Seize throne, and a unit on an Escape exit Cantos only between exits (it has acted, so it exits on its next phase). The veto is arithmetic, not categorical (Design Table, seventh round), and it covers every unit whose death loses the map and nobody else (fourteenth round, issue 141): the captain on every map, plus the recruit the `protect:` header names, since section 7's outcome order treats the two deaths alike and a veto is a rule where the death is a loss condition and a policy anywhere else. It is one predicate at every site of the planner (the attack refusal, the safest heal tile, the approach), because a unit that refuses lethal attacks but approaches blind walks onto a lethal tile by another route. Gate 1 counts a protected death as a loss, never as an exclusion: excluding it would make the win rate a rate over the games where the loss condition did not fire, and a map losing 95 percent to its own objective could pass on the other 5. Read with the captain in place of the veto's unit: the exposure query returns two sums for a plan over the whole cycle, the worst-case counter on the attack plus every enemy whose reach-plus-range covers the tile it ends on, once with no crit landing and once with every strike a crit, at the forecast's own numbers. The sum counts the board the enemy phase will find as far as this command certainly determines it (twelfth and thirteenth rounds; DECISIONS/0024 to 0026): an enemy that is certainly dead is not on it, a group this command certainly wakes is awake on it, and a strike that certainly misses is not thrown on it. Certainly dead is exact and constant-free (issue 117): the raw hit at 100 (the resolved probability exactly one under either scheme; a raw 99 prints as 100 under two rolls and still counts) and the first strike's damage at least the target's current HP, so the target contributes neither its counter nor its enemy-phase term, while a probable kill counts in full. Certainly woken is section 8's three causes through the one wake predicate the resolver uses, on the board the plan produces (issue 128): the mover on its tile, a certainly dead target removed, the fight's two tiles noisy when the plan attacks; a group the command does not wake is counted from where it stands. Certainly missing is a raw hit of 0 after the clamp, contributing nothing as a counter or as an enemy-phase term (issue 126), while raw 1 counts in full. Everything else is a worst case over the board at the moment of the command, and the veto is not a guarantee over the cycle: a group a later command wakes, and the death of the captain's own screen inside the cycle, which opens a path the sum priced as blocked (DECISIONS/0025), are the enemy's choices and the cycle's, not this command's certainty; gate 1's captain-loss count is how that regime is seen. A plan is refused when the no-crit sum reaches the captain's current HP; among plans that pass, one a crit cannot kill on is preferred ahead of the tile keys, so the crit term orders and never forbids. The gate also prints the median and p90 winning turn beside the limit, the instrument issue 13 reads limits from, and the losses by cause in section 7's order (timeout, captain, protected recruit; counts, zeros printed) with the mean quiet tail of the timeouts, the turns from the last combat to the limit (issue 114): a tail near zero is a fight that ran out of clock, a tail near the limit is a board that stopped, and the row says which without a threshold. Beside the tail, over the same timeouts, the row prints the median of each game's highest kill probability the veto refused, with the count it is over, as `refused kill p50 0.9994 over 16` (four decimals, floored rather than rounded, so nothing under one prints as 1.0000: raw 98 and 99 under two rolls print apart from the certainty line, and a doubled raw 99 that kills on either strike, one minus a hundred-millionth, prints 0.9999; issue 153), or a dash when no timeout had a refusal (issue 125; the eleventh and twelfth rounds). The probability is the named attack's own chance to kill over the strikes the attacker lives to make, at the forecast's numbers under the game's scheme: the first strike, and the second when it doubles, with the paths through the second strike weighted by the chance the attacker survives the counter thrown between them, at the defender's own hit and crit (issue 147; a defender that cannot strike back, or whose raw hit is 0, weighs them at one). Nothing new is computed beyond that survival. A value near one is a stall the veto caused by refusing a near-certain kill, which is an instrument reading and not a content finding; a low value is a board the player judged too risky. Printed, never classified, never gated on, and the count is printed so one game reads as one game. On an Escape map the row also prints, over the won games, the median number of recruits that came out and the count of wins with recruits deployed and none out, as `survivors p50 1 of 4, captain alone 0`, or `survivors -` when nothing was won (issue 263). An Escape win is the captain leaving and a recruit left behind has fallen (section 7, issue 269), so a win rate alone cannot tell a party that got out from a captain who walked away from everyone, and Escape maps are tuned on this count (Design Table, forty-second round). Printed, never gated, until the Table writes a threshold.
2. **Decisions matter.** The random-legal-move player wins ≤ 5% of 200 runs.
3. **No cheap shots.** Running the enemy phase from deployment positions with no player moves kills zero player units. Maps 4 and up may waive this deliberately with `cheap_shots: allowed` in the map header (section 10); the waiver is declared in the file so the Table can see which maps opted in, never exempted in a test.
4. **No dead weight.** Ablation, not kills: kills are a proxy that fails healers and corridor-blockers. For each deployed recruit, replay the same 200 seeds with that recruit benched and compare outcomes paired by seed against the baseline. Benching leaves the recruit's placement empty, named or bare, and never shifts another recruit into it, because a substitution is not an ablation (`BattleState.From` takes the bench; a unit whose death loses the map cannot be benched, the captain or the `protect:` recruit, since its empty placement loses every seed on turn 1 and the paired drop would measure the loss condition; issue 141). The threshold is relative: a recruit is dead weight when their paired drop is under half the median drop across the cast. (Threshold provisional, from the Table; tune on the first map that fails it.) A body does not always matter (issue 105, the Tollgate on the synthetic roster: benching any recruit raised the win rate), so the relative rule is guarded by a map-level verdict read first: the cast fails as a whole unless the median drop is above zero by twice the standard error of the row supplying it (on an even count, the larger of the two straddling rows' errors; issue 115), with one of two lines, since the failures are different findings: below minus that margin, the cast is not earning its deployment because benching the median recruit raises the win rate; otherwise the median recruit changes no outcomes, a ceiling. On either line the per-recruit rows print as data and no recruit is labelled, since the relative threshold has no zero point there. A verdict that read the bare sign flipped between FAILED and ok on the third decimal of a median of two noise numbers (the Tollgate, -0.003 under two rolls and +0.015 under one), and a gate that reads the sign of noise teaches re-running until green. This verdict is the gate-side instrument for the authoring rule agreed in the seventh round: about one contested place worth standing per two deployed units, or deploy fewer. The per-recruit rule applies only when the cast passes. The drop is a sample statistic, so the gate reports it with a standard error and fails a recruit only when `drop + 2 * SE < 0.5 * median`; a gate that flips a borderline recruit between runs gets ignored within a week. At 200 seeds the margin is about the size of the threshold, so the gate catches recruits whose drop is near zero and nothing subtler, which is what dead weight means; the seed count is a Sim flag when more precision is wanted. The pairing is only real once rolls and growth are keyed (issue 31): under a sequential stream, benching one unit reshuffles every later roll and every other recruit's growth, and the two arms decorrelate at the first differing command. With keyed rolls both arms draw the same numbers wherever they share a situation, and every recruit's level-ups are identical in the baseline and in every bench, so the gate measures contribution rather than a growth lottery the bench itself reshuffled. Keying does not reduce the run count (the baseline is gate 1's seed set); it reduces the variance. The output prints three action mixes (attacks made, damage dealt, heals cast, enemy attacks absorbed) next to the drop, over the same set of units on both sides of the bench: the recruit's own baseline mix, the rest of the cast's baseline mix, and the rest of the cast's mix with the recruit benched. The gate measures how well the heuristic player uses the unit, not the unit: a failure on a healer indicts the heuristic first, and a healer with a small drop whose absence makes the others absorb a third more is visible in the row. Each row also prints the benched arm's losses by cause in section 7's order (`benched losses 20 timeout 167 captain 0 protected`) and the refused-kill median over that arm's timeouts with its count, as gate 1 does (issue 125): a large drop beside many captain deaths, or beside a refused kill near one, is the veto's doing and not the recruit's, and the row says so itself. On an Escape map each row also prints the benched arm's survivors as gate 1 does (`benched survivors p50 0 of 3, captain alone 156`; issue 263), so a drop bought by the captain leaving the rest behind reads as that. The captain's baseline mix prints as a last row, unjudged, since he is never benched, and the protected recruit's beside it on a `protect:` map, out of the recruit count and the median for the same reason; on later maps a captain whose attack count is zero is the veto making him a piece to position rather than swing, read off the instrument.
5. **Forecast honesty.** For 10,000 random combats, forecast damage/hit/crit equals resolver behavior (statistically for hit/crit, exactly for damage). Two streams feed one tally: 10,000 combats between the content's units on random terrain resolved directly, and every attack of gate 6's random board stream against the forecast asked before it, so the count covers the formulas and the resolver's use of them; hits and crits must sit within three standard errors of the summed displayed probabilities.
6. **Determinism.** 100 random seeds × 100 random commands replay byte-identical.
7. **Speed.** A full AI-vs-AI map resolves in under 1 second.
8. **Crash-free.** 10,000 random legal command sequences across all maps throw nothing.

Gates 5–8 run on every PR (`Ironwake.Sim --smoke`). Gates 1–4 run when maps change. `--hitband <map>|--all [--seeds N]` prints section 5's hit-band table (issue 158): for each arm under each roll scheme, both sides' raw-hit histogram in ten-wide buckets over the heuristic's games, read from the forecast of every attack before the resolver applies it, the share of striking sides that double, and gates 1 and 4 on that arm, then the same buckets per terrain the struck unit stood on (the attacker's strike under the target's tile, the counter under the attacker's). A measurement only; it changes nothing that ships.

`--full` also prints the **free prefix** (issue 47, DECISIONS/0040), printed and wired to no exit code until the Table writes gate 9: for N in 2, 4 and 6, the random legal player plays turns 1 to N and the heuristic from N + 1, paired by seed against gate 1's baseline with gate 4's drop and standard error; a length is free when its drop is at most twice its standard error, and the free prefix is the largest length with every shorter one free too. Beside it, gate 1's median slack and the limit less the p90 winning turn; at the first length that is not free, one re-run with the limit raised by that length against the unraised baseline (still not free is positional; free is at most tempo). Then the turn-state counters over the baseline's games, each player phase read at its start: **live** when a player unit can reach a tile from which a weapon it carries strikes an enemy, or an enemy can (a holding enemy from its own tile only); **quiet** when not live and a player unit can end within the wake radius of a sleeping Guard group; **dead** otherwise; a sleeping group's members count on neither side. The wake tax is the reach set's overlap with sleeping radii, own tile included, from the player unit with the largest fraction. Medians over games of the dead and quiet counts, the leading dead run, first contact, and first quiet at or above the tax floor (`--taxfloor`, provisional 0.25), then one row per turn with the games in each state and the median tax.

Full 8-map campaign. Between-map screen. The presentation protocol (issue 25, `docs/PROTOCOL.md`) and a thin Godot renderer on it: draw, click, forecast, Recall browse, and an event log printing the CLI's text, no art pass, gated by a headless run whose log matches the CLI transcript byte for byte (twenty-second round, DECISIONS/0046).

## 13. Fun — direction and experiments

The gates in section 11 catch jank. They cannot tell us whether a turn was *worth taking*. This section is the creative direction and a starting list of things to try. Both partners add to it; killed ideas get one line in `docs/DECISIONS/` so we don't circle.

### What we're chasing
- **The tense turn.** The turn where you can see two good moves and one of them is wrong. If a map has none of those, it's a spreadsheet.
- **The story the map tells by itself.** Guard groups that wake up at the wrong time, a fort you didn't take, a rival who remembers. Emergent drama from rules, not cutscenes.
- **Growth with a face.** A recruit who was useless on map 1 and carries map 5 should feel like *your* doing, and the class they ended up in should be a choice you can point to.
- **Rewind you argue with yourself about.** Recall should be something you're slightly ashamed to use.

### Starting experiments (build on `experiment/<name>`, play, keep or kill)

Agreed order on the Table (2026-09-14): map events first, as Phase 2 infrastructure rather than a spike; enemy retreat second; Rapport and Rivalry third. Commander's Word runs at its number, issue 85, after map 8: its keep condition is only readable on a party that spreads (sixth round), so the first round's condition (after maps 1 to 3 and the hit A/B) is superseded, not merely met.

1. **Rapport and Rivalry.** Adjacent allies build rapport (the support base). But recruits from *different* regions start with rivalry, and a rival adjacent gives +10 crit and -5 hit — they show off. Rapport eventually overwrites rivalry. Cohort friction as a mechanic, not a cutscene. This is the only thing in v1 that makes adjacency mean anything (no zone of control, supports and battalions are Phase 3). Watch for: +10 crit at triple damage is worth about +20% expected damage against -5 hit, which is a formation bonus the player will farm, not friction. The spike's question is whether the player clusters rivals on purpose. Lean for the spike, the second arm: a rival adjacent also drops the unit's own crit avoid by 10. Against the starter content the cost is symmetric in probability, both sides move about ten points, which is a better defence of it than the flavour; it needs `CritAvoid` unclamped (section 5). The hole in the second arm: the bonus applies to every attack the pair makes and the cost only to attacks the pair receives, and a clean player phase kills the enemies that would have swung back, so the cost tends to zero as skill rises while the bonus stays whole. The spike therefore also logs exposure: of the player-phase actions taken while rival-adjacent, the fraction where that unit is attacked on the following enemy phase. If exposure comes back low, the third arm ties bonus and cost to one condition: the rivalry bonus applies only on counters, so the unit shows off when someone swings at it and nothing can be collected from the back line. All three arms are data changes. Cha's job here (Table, sixth round, the lean): a recruit's Cha sets the rate rapport accrues while adjacent, printed on the unit screen as the rate itself, never as a bare Cha, so pillar 2 holds. Before supports (issue 77) rapport's only effect is to overwrite rivalry, so Cha's sign follows the arm: under the bonus as written a high-Cha recruit loses the show-off bonus soonest, under the symmetric cost that is relief, under the counter-only arm it ends a conditional bonus. The tier numbers are set with issue 77, and the spike's journal says whether a high-Cha recruit read as a friend or as a unit losing its edge. The spike is built behind the map header above (DECISIONS/0038): a recruit is a deployed player unit other than the captain, the arms, the rate table and the overwrite threshold are rows of `rules.json`, and rapport accrues at the end of a player phase only when an awake enemy could strike one of the pair next phase, the strike set the exposure line and the retreat refuge also read (DECISIONS/0042), so the cure is bought under fire. Kept on the symmetric arm with the threshold at 16 (DECISIONS/0043, amended the same day): three exposed enemy phases buy the cure, which is too dear to farm on purpose and is paid where geometry forces the pair together, and the rapport line prints a rival pair's total against it.
2. **Commander's Word.** Once per map the captain calls one order, scaled by Cha: *Hold* (+15 avoid to all allies this enemy phase), *Press* (+1 Mov this phase), *Rally* (heal 15% all). A gambit before battalions exist, and Cha's first job (Table, sixth round; issue 85). Calling the order is the captain's action for the turn, so it costs the captain's swing. Two arms, both content, and arm B is the shape both partners expect to keep. Arm A, as written: magnitude scales with the captain's Cha and the order reaches every ally. Arm B: magnitude fixed, and the order reaches allies within a radius of the captain in tiles set by Cha (`Cha / 4` to start, so the first radius is the wake rule's 4 and the player counts it the same way); a multiplier is honest only after the order is spent, since the forecast shows the resolved number but the player cannot price the order before committing, while a radius is countable at the moment it is a decision, and it prices the charge, since the captain is the loss condition and must stand near the line to hold it. Arm B's failure is Cha going idle once the radius covers the party, so the spike logs the binding fraction (recruits reached over recruits alive, per order, per map) and the captain's exposure on the enemy phase after the order, which separates an order that made the player commit from one that cost them (issue 85). Runs at its number: after issue 66 (Hold is a conditional modifier section 8's scorer must see) and after map 8 (issue 83), because the three sample maps deploy their units packed within Manhattan 2 of the captain and a binding fraction read there is 1 on every order, which says nothing; maps 4 to 8 deploy six to eleven units on grids up to 20x16, where the fraction can bind and the drift can be read on the maps it drifts on. The first round's condition, after maps 1 to 3 and the hit A/B, is superseded by that schedule (Table, sixth round); the hit A/B has run long before, so the order's avoid numbers are still read against one roll scheme. If the journals ever say bosses are walls, a fourth order that stuns is where to look, never an equipment layer.
3. **Recall scars.** Killed 2026-09-14 (DECISIONS/0010). It taxed the recruit's future instead of the charge, and its incentive was to let the recruit die. Replaced by the "Recall restores the rolls" rule in section 7.
4. **Named rivals who level.** A crown cohort — six named enemies — appears on maps 2, 4, 6, and 8 with levels tracking the player's average, carrying grudges: a rival whose ally you killed targets that unit first. Data-driven recurring antagonists.
5. **Ironwake itself.** The finale is the defense of the keep. Between maps the player spends repair points on the keep's `.map` — rebuild a wall, dig a ditch (water), raise a fort — editing the finale's terrain. Your base is a level you've been building all game. *Spiked on issue 82 (experiment/ironwake-keep):* `campaign.json`'s optional `keep` names the keep's map under `content/keep` and a fixed menu of edits, each a terrain, a price and the tiles it may go on, so no spend can close a breach; `Keep.Apply` changes one tile, the edited keep is written by `MapFormat.Write` and parsed back, and `ironwake-sim --keep [<edit> <x,y>]...` runs gates 1, 2 and 4 on it (`--write <path>` saves it for `play`). Kept provisionally (DECISIONS/0059). The menu is wall and ditch; the fort edit is cut (#287). The keep's van strikes the breaches on enemy phase 2 and its waves arrive on enemy phases 2 to 6, the last the heaviest, so the final enemy phase is the one the player fears. *Attacked twice (#288, DECISIONS/0060):* `keep.raid` names a raid map under `content/keep`, fought after Harrow Weir on the bare keep's grid with a smaller force spawning only on the finale's spawn tiles, a Rout whose waves all land by enemy phase 2 so no early rout skips one; the keep itself is the campaign's last map. The menu opens on the screen after the raid is won and never before (`keep` lists it, `build <edit> <x,y>` buys one placement); the campaign record carries the edits bought, in order (`keep` in the protocol's campaign shape), and the finale is the content's keep with them made, rebuilt after the screen so an edit bought on it stands. Each placement prints what it does in rules terms from the terrain and the map, never authored text: the terrain's name, which movement types cannot stand on it, any avoid, def, res or heal, and how a foot-passable gap closed on both sides along its row or column narrows (`wall 10,8: Wall; no unit can stand on it; the gap 10,7 to 10,8 narrows from 2 tiles to 1 (10,7)`).
6. **Certification as a puzzle.** Instead of a roll, certifying into a class is a one-turn micro-map: "kill the dummy in one turn with this loadout." Pass and the class is yours. Tiny puzzle maps are cheap to author and genuinely satisfying. *Spiked on issue 73 (DECISIONS/0049):* the `certification: <class> <item> ...` header of section 10 makes a map a trial, `play --candidate <id>` fields the unit, and two samples under `docs/samples/certification/` are played; a trial stands in for the seal after the class's requirements (thirty-seventh round; built in issue 252, DECISIONS/0057), and the two trials ship under `content/trials/`: the Bulwark's corridor, and the Outrider's rebuilt with a hexer holding the gate, struck only from the forest in front with Canto left to seize; #73 closes on Chat's cold play of the rebuild.
7. **Dusk maps.** Vision radius shrinks by one tile each turn. Not fog of war — a countdown. Pressure without RNG. *Spiked on experiment/dusk (2026-09-26):* the `dusk: N` header of section 10 turns it on per map. Each side's sight is N tiles (counted as ranges are) on turn 1 and one less each turn, never under 1; a side sees a tile when any of its units is within sight of it. Neither side strikes a unit its side cannot see, so on a night of sight 1 an archer needs a friend beside its target; counters are unaffected. The console draws an enemy no player unit sees as `?` with no row, name, numbers, forecast or `threat` line, and each command it takes, a move or a wait, prints as `enemy: something in the dark acts` (#301), so the silence never tells a wait from a move. Sight is a function of the turn, so Recall rewinds it. Played on Brackwater Cut (dusk 7, seed 7).
8. **Carry the fallen.** Permadeath stays, but an ally who reaches a fallen recruit's tile recovers their weapon, and the weapon keeps their name. Loss with a keepsake. *Spiked on experiment/carry-the-fallen (2026-09-26):* the `keepsakes: on` header of section 10 turns it on per map; a player unit that falls leaves its equipped weapon (else its first weapon) on its tile under its id, `recover <unit>` from that tile is the unit's action in place of Attack, Item or Wait (a free slot needed, no Canto after), the stack carries the fallen's name for the rest of the campaign (`Iron Lance (Teodor's)`), and a keepsake nobody recovers is gone when the battle ends. Played on Brackwater Cut and the raid: the rule was either unreachable or free, never a decision (forty-fifth round). *Second arm, the carrier (issue 295):* a tile holds a stack, oldest first, and `recover` takes the newest, one per action. An enemy that ends a move (or a retreat) on a stack takes all of it, one `takes Iron Lance (Teodor's)` line each, and carries it past the five-slot cap, which counts ordinary stacks only. A carrier strikes with a keepsake whenever it can wield one, overriding the planner's score, and one it cannot wield it only carries; the planner reads a tile as the unit would stand on it after taking the stack there, so the take and the strike can come in the same phase. The forecast line names a keepsake struck with (`forecast brigand-2 -> ottilie with Iron Lance (Teodor's): ...`), `threat` names it in its weapon, and an enemy's unit row reads `carries ...`. Whoever dies carrying keepsakes drops all of them on its tile (a player unit its own weapon too), so none is ever dropped silently, and when the battle ends every keepsake nobody recovered gets one line: `... was left at 12,3` or `... went with brigand-2`. Recall restores the stacks and the carriers with the board. Judged on maps where killing is optional; if in both partners' plays nobody spends a move or an action on a carrier they could have ignored, 13.8 is killed.
9. **Map events.** A minimal trigger system: on turn N or when a tile is entered, open a gate, collapse a bridge, spawn reinforcements from an edge. The single most reusable tool for making maps tell stories. Not an experiment: it is the authoring tool maps 1 to 8 are written with, so it is Phase 2 infrastructure (issue 32) and maps 1 to 3 are authored with it rather than rewritten after it.
10. **Enemy retreat and regroup.** Enemies below 30% HP with an escape route fall back to the nearest fort and heal. Enemies that act like they want to live make players commit. Constraint: an enemy retreats only if it can reach a fort or a safe tile *this turn*, and never retreats twice; otherwise it stands and fights. No multi-turn flights, no chases, nothing that eats the turn limit. Built as a spike behind the per-map `retreat: on` header (issue 33, DECISIONS/0037). A `Retreat` command, one predicate (`RetreatRule`) that the planner and the resolver share. It applies to Aggressive effective behavior only, strictly below 30 percent. The refuge is a healing tile in reach that no player unit can strike next phase (the strike set after any move, issue 204), with the least player reach first; a unit already on healing terrain fights from it. A `Retreated` mark is never cleared. A refugee strictly below 50 percent holds its refuge as Hold, striking only from its tile, and is Aggressive again at half (issue 215); the forecast prints the refuge a strike would send its target to (`wingrider-2 would fall back to 2,2 at 5 hp`) on the board as it stands. Keep or kill is open and waits on the third-pass sample (`docs/samples/river_refuge_hold.map`) played from both chairs; a kill removes the header, the command and the predicate.
11. **The two-weapon boss.** A boss carrying a heavy and a light weapon picks between them by section 8's score, so against a cadet fast enough to double the heavy weapon's counter it swings the light one, and the Attack names the slot so the player sees the choice; the forecast shows which weapon it would take against the unit in view. Candidate for after #158's keep or kill (Table, twenty-first round), not before: the bandit leader's Hatchet leaves its inventory with #174 so that the planner line lands content-neutral, and a boss choosing its weapon now would blur the one counter both Saltmarsh entries called the map's only dice. The drop is not a rejection of this.
