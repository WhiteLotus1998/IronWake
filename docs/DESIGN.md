# IRONWAKE — Design Document

Working title: **Ironwake**. A turn-based tactics game in the Fire Emblem: Three Houses lineage — grid battles, a small cast you grow, class paths, permadeath with a rewind — but its own game, not a clone. No Fire Emblem names, places, characters, or text anywhere in the repo.

This document is the source of truth, and it belongs to both partners (Chat and Code — see CLAUDE.md). Code that disagrees with it is wrong; if the document is wrong, fix the document in the same PR and record why in `docs/DECISIONS/`. Sections 1–12 are the floor. Section 13 is where the game gets interesting, and it is meant to be rewritten as we learn what's fun.

## 1. Identity

**Premise.** Ironwake is a crumbling border keep at the seam of three rival regions, Aldmere, Sallow and Kestrow. An armistice is fraying. Under its terms each region owes the keep a levy of fighters, and the crown pays to hold them. This year the crown stopped paying, the old garrison drifted off, and the regions sent whoever they could spare, late, short, or not at all. The player is the captain the crown appointed to a company that hasn't arrived. The campaign is the captain gathering it, one fighter at a time, and holding it together while the armistice comes apart. The cast's frictions and friendships are the story. The fraying armistice is the campaign. (A levy company, not a class: Lotus's "not a school", round 190; how the company arrives is section 14. The captain is a man or a woman, the player's pick (#648); the premise is worded so either reads true.)

**Pillars, in priority order.**
1. *Every turn is a decision.* If a random legal move wins as often as a thoughtful one, the map is broken.
2. *Numbers are honest.* The combat forecast shows exactly what the resolver will do. No hidden modifiers.
3. *Growth you feel.* Units level, are promoted into classes, and change what they can do on the map.
4. *Loss has weight, rewind has cost.* Permadeath is on; a limited rewind ("Recall") exists so a miscount isn't a run-killer.
5. *Small cast, big personalities.* 10 recruits + captain for the whole campaign. Depth over breadth.

**Out of scope for v1.** Monastery/calendar, cooking, fishing, voice, animation, any graphics beyond the console. Rendering front-end (Godot 4 .NET, DECISIONS/0008) comes after the core is done and the campaign is fun in the console. Romance was on this list until Lotus lifted it (round 192, DECISIONS/0126); it lives in supports (#77).

## 2. Architecture (non-negotiable)

- **Headless core.** `Ironwake.Core` contains all rules. It has no dependency on Console, System.Random, file IO, or time. Everything it needs is injected (`IRng`, content passed in).
- **Immutable state.** `BattleState` is an immutable record. The only way to change it is `Resolver.Apply(state, command) -> (BattleState next, IReadOnlyList<GameEvent> events)`. Events are the log; the CLI renders from events, never from diffing state.
- **Determinism.** Same seed + same command sequence = same state, byte for byte. RNG draws happen in a fixed, documented order. This is tested.
- **Rewind is free.** Because states are immutable, Recall is "pop the history stack." Replays are "re-apply the command list." Every bug report is a seed + command list.
- **Content is data.** Classes, weapons, terrain, units, and maps are JSON/text files under `/content`, validated on load with clear error messages. No stats in code.

## 3. Units and stats

Nine stats, integers: **HP, Str, Mag, Dex, Spd, Lck, Def, Res, Cha**. Cha exists from day one. Its jobs are the lean of the Table's sixth round, both spikes and both content: the captain's Cha scales Commander's Word (13.2, issue 85), and a recruit's Cha sets the rate rapport accrues (13.1, issue 16). Battalions, the job this section first named, keep their place in section 12 with their shape open. Each unit has base stats, per-stat growth rates (%), a level (1–30), EXP (0–99), a class, an inventory (5 slots), and a set of abilities.

Classes define: movement type (Infantry, Cavalry, Flying, Armored), Mov, stat modifiers, usable weapon types, class growth modifiers, and one mastery ability (Phase 3). Movement types matter for terrain cost and for effective damage. **Certification (issue 72):** a class may name what it asks of a unit certifying into it, a minimum level, a minimum rank per named weapon type and a minimum per named stat, read against the unit's own stats, not its current class's. A unit that meets them certifies, with no roll; one that does not is refused with every failed requirement named (`needs lance D, has E`). Certifying changes the class and nothing else, so stats follow the new class's modifiers and mastery points and mastered abilities stay. The price, a seal from the campaign's purse, is the between-map screen's (issue 74); a class with a certification trial may be earned by passing it instead (issue 252, section 13.6). The shipped ladder (DECISIONS/0058, provisional) is climbed from the cadet: the cadet asks nothing; pikeman, reaver, bowman, adept and chaplain ask level 3, and outrider, skyrider and bulwark level 4; a class whose weapon a cadet trains asks a rank in it (pikeman lance D, reaver axe D, outrider sword D, skyrider lance D and spd 8), and one it cannot train asks a stat (bowman dex 8, adept mag 6, chaplain mag 4 and res 4, bulwark def 5). The between-map screen's `classes [unit]` prints the ladder and what a unit still lacks for each class.

**The second tier (issue 704, DECISIONS/0157).** Each of the eight first-tier classes has exactly one advanced form, named in `classes.json` by `advances: <base>`: Halberdier (Pikeman), Berserker (Reaver), Marksman (Bowman), Scholar (Adept), Warden (Chaplain), Lancer (Outrider), Sky Captain (Skyrider), Sentinel (Bulwark). Only a unit in the base class may take the step (`needs to be a <Base> first`), at level 7 and rank C in the base's main weapon (rounds 224 to 226, DECISIONS/0161; provisional), for the advanced seal (`advancedCertificationPrice`, 1000). The form keeps every weapon type of its base, its growths are the base's (the file may not name its own), its stat modifiers rise, and one step is the most: a form above a form is refused on load. Each form adds one verb: a weapon type, a range, +1 Mov, a movement type or a terrain rule, plus a new mastery. Slice 1 ships the weapon adds (Halberdier and Lancer the axe, Warden the sword, Sentinel the bow) and the step itself. Slice 2 (DECISIONS/0158) ships the rest as class abilities, held while in the form: the Marksman's Long Draw (a bow reaches one tile further, 2-3), the Warden's Far Mending (a healing spell reaches one tile further: Salve 1-2, Beacon 1-3), the Scholar's Faith for strike spells only (`strikeOnly`; it enters at Faith D through `grants`, since an Adept never trained it and Radiance is D), the Berserker's Blood Price (an axe kill heals 5, never above max HP), and the Sky Captain's Mov 7. Every form names a mastery at 12 points (Set Haft, Hard to Kill, Still Breath, Old Texts, Steadfast, Couched Lance, Wind Rider, Iron Footing). Dusk's unseen reach reads only the classes an enemy template is in, so a player's form does not widen it. `classes <unit>` prints each form's adds on its line. Slice 3 (DECISIONS/0159) ships one enemy template per form, its base template's numbers in the form (Veteran, Marauder, Longbowman, Lector, Deacon, Heavy Rider, Wing Captain, Sentry; steel or a D spell, rank D declared); the Wing Captain takes dusk's unseen reach to 9. **The campaign's curve (slice 4, DECISIONS/0161).** A `campaign.json` map may name `enemyLevel`, the level the campaign fights it at in place of the file's, and `swap`, an object of `x,y` tiles to enemy templates fielded there instead (group, behaviour and boss flag kept); the standalone map, `--smoke` and the EXP formula are unchanged, and a difficulty's offset is added on top. The curve over maps 1 to 9 is 1, 1, 2, 3, 2, 6, 7, 6, 8: the Table's 1,1,2,3,4,5,7,8,8 with Harrow Weir's point bent back to its file, Brackwater Cut's stepped 8 to 7 to 6 on `--curve`'s `carried` read (round 234, DECISIONS/0178), since a tuned map's point comes down when its campaign gate 1 falls under 60, and the raid (never tuned) one higher to buy back the levels, so the top unit still reaches 7 at the camp before Brackwater. The templates enter as one Veteran on Sallow Grange, two Heavy Riders on Brackwater Cut's chase and a Veteran, a Marauder and a Longbowman in the keep's van. `--curve [--map <id>]` prints gate 1 per campaign map as the file reads, as the campaign fights it with the file's party, and with that party raised by the curve's levels. Cadets climb three steps (Levy, a class, its form). An advanced form wears its base's art until it has its own.

**Unique classes, the other door (issue 706, DECISIONS/0166; provisional shapes).** A unique class is an advanced form of one cast member's base, offered to that member alone beside the standard form at the second promotion, at the same level, rank and seal. `classes.json` names it with `advances`, `unique: <member>`, `unlockedBy: <quest>` (a quest of that member, whose win opens it) and `loses: <measure>`, the measure on which it loses to the standard form; the loader refuses a unique class that is hidden, on the captain's ladder, without a standard form beside it, or second for one member above one base, and `unlockedBy` or `loses` on any other class. Passing either door records it on the unit (`doors`, base to form, in the save), and the other door above that base is refused for good (`<id> took the other door above <Base>; <Form> is closed`), whatever the unit later certifies through. `classes` lists a unique class only for its member (or with no unit named), marked `[<Name>'s other door; loses <measure>]`. A test holds each loss (`Sidegrades.Measure`). **Maud's Field Surgeon** (slice 1, unlocked by `maud_1`, The Lazar House): Faith, heal-only (`healOnly`, so Radiance and every strike spell are refused), Steady Hands (a `mending` effect: a heal restores double and reaches 1 tile, whatever its spell or Far Mending says) and Ward Rounds (a Canto owed only after a spell heals an ally, `canto` with `after: heal`), mastery Triage (+2 Spd, +1 Res), modifiers Mag +2, Res +2, Spd +1; it loses `reach` to the Warden (Salve 1 against 2, Beacon 1 against 3). **Rook's Scout** (slice 2, DECISIONS/0167, unlocked by `rook_1`, her quest 1, not yet authored: `unlockedBy` may name a quest id of the member's shape the campaign does not carry yet, and the door stays shut until it does): a flier, Mov 7, lance, High Watch (a `sight` effect: at dusk she sees 2 tiles further than the company's sight, her side sees what she sees, and the dusk line says so, `; Rook sees 3`) and Headcount (a `headcount` effect: `threat` prices a sleeping group with a member within 6 tiles of her, from the tile asked when the threat is hers, member by member as an awake enemy is, with `if woken and all land`, never in the total; protocol `countedBy`, `priced`, `ifWokenAllLand`), mastery Lookout (+1 Spd, +2 Lck), modifiers Spd +3, Res +2. A unique class alone may name `growthModifiers`, added to its base's: the Scout's Str growth is 10 under the Sky Captain's. It loses `damage` (Str plus its strongest weapon's Mt) to the Sky Captain by the 1 Str the Sky Captain's modifiers add.

**The captain's ladder (issue 705, DECISIONS/0160).** The captain does not take the eight. At the first promotion, level 3 for the plain seal, the captain picks one of three classes flagged `captain` in `classes.json`: **Vanguard** (sword only, HP and Def up; DECISIONS/0165 moved its lance to the Champion; a durability-only lever was read and killed as a hoarder, DECISIONS/0174; its verb is Opening, DECISIONS/0177), **Marshal** (sword and Lore, Cha up) or **Ranger** (sword and bow, Mov 5, Move Again as its mastery). Each has one advanced form at level 10 and sword C: **Champion** adds the lance and the axe, **Commander** rides (cavalry, Mov 6) and adds the lance, **Pathfinder** crosses forest and hill at cost 1. The rules: only the captain, the cast's first, is promoted into a captain's class (`only the captain takes <X>`), the captain into no other (`<X> is not on the captain's ladder`), and once on it only up, into the held class's own form (`the captain's ladder is one-way`). The loader refuses a captain's class on any unit but the captain and a form whose flag differs from its base's. Origin and class are independent. The camp row ends `promotes at 3: Marshal, Ranger or Vanguard` until the captain takes one, then `ladder: <class>, then <form> at 10`. Slice 1 ships the doors and the data. Slice 2 (DECISIONS/0162) ships the verbs as three effect kinds: the Vanguard masters Shoulder to Shoulder (`beside`: +1 Def and +1 Res while a living ally stands orthogonally beside), the Marshal Command Presence (`aura`: each ally within 2 tiles, never the holder, fights at +10 Acc; one aura held twice counts once), the Pathfinder carries Trail Sense (`footing`: forest and hill cost 1 to enter, never opening impassable ground). The forms master Breakthrough (Champion: +5 Acc, +15 Crit with an axe), Field Command (Commander: an aura of +10 Evade within 2) and Light Step (Pathfinder: +2 Spd, +2 Lck); 12 points each. All read on the board where units stand, so the forecast, `threat`, both planners and the resolver read one number. The Vanguard's verb (issue 772, round 236, DECISIONS/0177, provisional) is the class ability **Opening** (`opening`): when the Vanguard attacks and any of its strikes hits an enemy that lives through the combat, a hit for 0 included, that enemy is *open* until the phase ends, and every strike on it by an ally reads its Def and Res lower by the ability's numbers, never below 0. The Vanguard's own strikes never read it, a counter never opens, a miss opens nothing, and a second opening refreshes without stacking. The board row, the card (`open: allies of <captain> strike at Def -N, Res -N until player phase ends`), an ally's forecast (`open: ... in this forecast`) and the event `unitOpened` print it; the mark is board state, so Recall restores it. The Champion does not inherit it. It is offence, so round 155 stands. Slice 3 is the Sim's bar, as round 232 rewrote it (DECISIONS/0174, provisional): per map a floor only, no class more than 10 under the unpromoted captain unless its captain absorbs under half his damage (then a hand play of that map decides), and gate 4's captain verdict; across the campaign, each class's mean gate 1 over the campaign's maps the ladder reads (Starting Alone and Sallow Grange left out) within 10 of the others at 200 seeds, 5 at 400 seeds before anything ships as tuned; `--ladder` prints both checks and the captain's EXP share per class; origin by class runs through `--smoke` (DECISIONS/0165). A form's modifiers replace its base's, so no form's modifier may read under its base's (a test holds it). Slice 4 (DECISIONS/0164) spends each class's modifiers on stats the captain uses (no Mag on the ladder; the Lore classes keep their Mag growth); the remaining tier 1 gap is in the classes' lines, not their numbers. If 13.2 is kept, each class strengthens one order (Vanguard Press, Marshal Rally, Ranger Fall back).

Level up at 100 EXP. Each stat rolls independently against its growth (unit growth + class modifier). Level cap 30 for v1. Growth rolls are keyed on (unit, new level, stat) and nothing else (issue 31), so a recruit's trajectory is fixed at the campaign seed: no Recall changes what a recruit gets at level 7, and a recruit's level-ups are identical whether or not another recruit is deployed, which gate 4's ablation needs. Keying on map or turn as well would reopen laundering by waiting a turn and break the gate. A single starved stat on one recruit is a character, not a defect; the Sim shows the spread before anyone argues for a change.

## 4. Terrain

Square grid, 4-connected movement. No zone of control. Units may pass through allies, never through enemies, and may not end on an occupied tile.

| Tile | Glyph | Infantry | Cavalry | Flying | Armored | Avoid | Def/Res | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plain | `.` | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | |
| Road | `=` | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | reserved for later cav bonus |
| Forest | `^` | 2 | 3 | 1 | 2 | +20 | +1 Def | |
| Hill | `n` | 2 | 3 | 1 | 3 | +10 | +1 Def | |
| Mountain | `M` | 3 | — | 1 | — | +30 | +2 Def | |
| Water | `~` | — | — | 1 | — | 0 | 0 | |
| Fort | `F` | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | +15 | +2 Def, +2 Res | heals 20% max HP at start of owner's phase |
| Wall | `#` | — | — | — | — | — | — | blocks everything, including flyers |
| Gate/Throne | `T` | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | +30 | +3 Def, +3 Res | seize target; heals 20% |
| Fire | `%` | 2 | 3 | 1 | 2 | 0 | 0 | burns 20% max HP at start of owner's phase, never below 1; only on a `wildfire: on` map in practice (13.15, experiment) |
| Planks | `+` | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | wears to Split planks when walked off (13.25, experiment; samples only) |
| Split planks | `:` | 1 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 0 | 0 | wears to Water when walked off (13.25) |

`—` means impassable. Terrain avoid/def do not apply to flyers except on Fort/Throne.

**Reach.** A unit's reachable tiles are every tile whose cheapest entry cost, summed along a 4-connected path, is at most its Mov; the budget is strict, so a tile costing more than what remains is not entered. Tiles an ally stands on may be crossed but not ended on; tiles an enemy stands on are never entered. The unit's own tile is always a destination, since Move is optional (section 7), and it must be a tile the unit's movement type can enter: a query from one it cannot, a footman on a wall or a rider in water, is refused with an error naming the tile, the terrain, and the movement type, never answered, because the loader will not place a unit there and a unit there anyway is a corrupt state (issue 42). Which of two equal-cost paths a unit walks is part of the rules, because renderers show it: tiles settle in (cost, then row-major) order, and a tile's path changes only when a strictly cheaper one is found. Read as a rule about paths: a tile's path arrives through the row-major-first of its neighbours whose cost plus the tile's entry cost equals the tile's cost, so the path to every reachable tile is derivable from the map alone. The order in which a tile's neighbours are enumerated does no work and is not part of the contract (issue 45). On ground that wears (13.25) the tie-break gains a key between the two (issue 782, DECISIONS/0180): tiles settle in (cost, wear, row-major) order, a route's wear the plank steps it would wear by leaving its tiles (the start included, the end not; a tile an ally stands on wears nothing; the steps the mover's movement wears, held to what the tile's chain has left), and a tile's path changes only for a strictly cheaper cost or, at equal cost, strictly less wear. So among the cheapest routes the least wearing is walked, by the player, the enemy planner and the Sim alike, and on ground that never wears every route's wear is 0 and the order is the plain (cost, row-major) one. `move <unit> <x,y> via <x,y>` walks the cheapest route to the waypoint and then the cheapest on from it on the Mov left (the mover's own start tile empty on the second leg), refused when either leg is out of reach; `move ... preview` prints the route and what it would wear without moving. `Movement.Reach` is the one function that answers this; the CLI, the AI, and every renderer ask it rather than counting for themselves (DECISIONS/0012).

## 5. Combat

3H-inspired, but these are Ironwake's formulas. All division is integer floor.

**Labels (issue 701, DECISIONS/0153).** The formulas keep the short field names; the screen does not. Mt shows as **Power**, Hit as **Acc** (printed first on the weapon list, the unit card, the item card and the forecast), Avoid as **Evade**, combat arts as **Techniques**, the Reason type as **Lore**. The rule: a distinctive coinage from another series goes, an ordinary English word stays. Ids, content keys and script words are unchanged.

```
Burden        = max(0, WeaponWeight - Str)
AttackSpeed   = Spd - Burden
Doubles       if AttackSpeed >= TargetAttackSpeed + 4

Atk           = Str + Mt   (physical)   |  Mag + Mt   (magic)
Effective     = Mt * 3 before adding Str/Mag, when weapon tag matches target movement type
Damage        = max(0, Atk - (Def | Res) - TerrainDefBonus)
Crit damage   = Damage * 3

Hit           = WeaponHit + Dex + Lck / 2
Avoid (phys)  = 2 * AttackSpeed + Lck / 2 + TerrainAvoid
Avoid (magic) = (Spd + Lck) / 2 + TerrainAvoid
HitChance     = clamp(Hit - Avoid, 0, 100)

Crit          = WeaponCrit + (Dex + Lck) / 2
CritAvoid     = Lck + modifiers
CritChance    = clamp(Crit - CritAvoid, 0, 100)
```

Only `CritChance` is clamped. `CritAvoid` is not: a modifier (rivalry in 13.1 is the first) may push it below zero, and a crit of 3 against a crit avoid of -5 is 8 percent. A defensive clamp on `CritAvoid` would silently erase every such cost against the low-crit weapons that make up maps 1 and 2, so a test falsifies it.

**Why burden and avoid read this way (issue 158, DECISIONS/0028).** Until the keep, burden was weight less `Str / 5` and speed counted once in avoid; an iron weapon then cost a Str 5 to 9 unit 4 to 7 speed, avoid on plain was 1 to 7 for everyone, and raw hits sat at 93 to 100 both ways, so the forecast was arithmetic and doubling followed weapon weight. Burden against the whole of Str gives speed back its job, speed counting twice makes Spd the skirmisher's stat, and the iron tier's hit is 15 lower in content (Iron Sword 75, Iron Lance 70, Iron Axe 65, Iron Bow 70), which together put the player's raw hit on open ground at about 60 to 80 and the enemy's at 50 to 70, with forest and fort visibly below. The fort's avoid is 15, not 30, for the same reason: at 30 under these formulas a boss on a fort was a lottery (Design Table, twenty-fourth round). The Sim's `--hitband` prints the raw-hit histogram of both sides, per defender terrain, beside the doubling rate and gates 1 and 4, under both roll schemes, as a standing number.

**Rolls.** Hit uses the average of two rolls (0–99) by default: a strike lands when `floor((A + B) / 2) < HitChance`, so the landing probability is the share of the 10000 ordered pairs whose sum is below `2 * HitChance` (a raw 50 lands 50.5 percent of the time). Under one roll it lands when `A < HitChance`. Crit uses one roll, `C < CritChance`, drawn only when the hit landed. Every roll is derived from the campaign seed and a key, never from a stream position (issue 31): `IRng.Roll(key)` is the engine's only source of chance, and the key tuples are the contract that replaced draw order. A combat roll keys on `(turn, phase, attacker, target, strikeIndex, roll)` with `roll` one of `HitA`, `HitB`, `Crit`; the counter's strikes key with the defender as the attacker, and `strikeIndex` counts that striker's own strikes in the combat from 0. A watch shot (13.17) keys on `(turn, phase, watcher, target, roll)` under its own prefix, so it never shares a key with the watcher's counter. A growth roll keys on `(unit, newLevel, stat)` and nothing else (section 3). The production `IRng` hashes the key's text with the seed (FNV-1a and a 64-bit finalizer, written out in `KeyedRng`, DECISIONS/0013), so the same seed and key give the same roll in every process; the two pinned rolls in `KeyedRngTests` make a change to the hash a visible decision, since it changes every seed's game.

**Display.** The forecast shows the *resolved* probability of whatever roll scheme is in use, rounded to the nearest integer and held inside 1 to 99 unless the hit is certain or impossible, never the raw hit stat: 100 prints only for a strike that cannot miss and 0 only for one that cannot land, so a raw 95 under two rolls (99.55 percent) prints 99 and a raw 1 (0.03 percent) prints 1 (issue 452; the Design Table's hundred-and-sixth round asked that the screen never claim more certainty than the dice have). Under two-roll averaging a raw 75 lands 87.75% of the time and displays as 88; a raw 30 lands 18.3% and displays as 18. Forecast, resolver, and AI scorer all call one hit-probability function; the roll scheme is a switch inside it. One roll versus two was settled by the hit A/B on the cast (Design Table, ninth round; DECISIONS/0023): two-roll averaging, because under it who you brought explains more of the outcome and the dice less (gate 4's median drop as a share of the baseline's wins, 95 against 77 percent on Old Mill Road and 82 against 59 on Saltmarsh Ford, paired over 200 seeds), and fights are shorter. The terrain reason for one roll did not survive the arithmetic: two-roll averaging makes forest's +20 worth less than printed only above raw 85 (11 points at 96 to 76) and more below it (19 at 86 to 66, 33 at 68 to 48), and the cast's median matchup is raw 86. `--scheme one` stays in the Sim and the CLI as the Fun Gate's first dial if the journals on maps 1 to 3 say the turns read as arithmetic rather than risk (issue 15). The CLI prints the forecast line before every attack it resolves, the enemy phase's included (issue 152), so the odds behind every hit are on the transcript and a reader can tell a misplay from bad luck; the enemy's line is asked of the core on the board the resolver is about to read, the same query the player's `forecast` command uses. Under the player's forecast line, when the counter kills the attacker if every counter strike lands (its plain damage over all the strikes it can make against the attacker's HP, no crit counted, and never when the attacker's first round is a certain kill), the forecast adds `  counter: lethal to <unit> (<damage> against <hp> hp)`, the protocol's `counterLethal` (issue 539; the same deterministic reading as `threat`'s total, so the screen cannot disagree with the planner). The other half of the killbox is `threat <unit> [from <x,y>]` (issue 217): for a unit on its tile or one it can still move to, every enemy that could strike it on the coming enemy phase, each with the weapon, tile and forecast the planner would use were it to choose that unit (the planner's own options restricted to that target, on the board the exposure sum prices with the player phase ended), then the total if every strike lands. The lines are independent, but the total is not (issue 253): it is the worst case over assignments of enemies to distinct strike tiles, one enemy per tile, so two enemies whose only way in is the same tile count once, the harder hitter. Each line reads the phase-start board; an earlier enemy's move or kill is not played out, so the list is what can come, not what will. A tile whose move wins the map (the captain onto a Seize throne) has no enemy phase after it, so `threat` prints `threat on <unit> at <x,y> (<terrain>): this move wins the map` in place of the list rather than an all-clear (issue 356).

**Sequence.** Attacker strikes; defender counters if the attacker is within the defender's weapon range and the defender has a usable weapon; then whoever doubles strikes again. Combat ends early on death. Each of those turns is a *round*: one strike, or two for gauntlets (issue 70), each rolling hit and crit on its own, with a death ending the combat mid-round; so a doubling gauntlet user lands four strikes and a doubling sword user two. The forecast shows every strike (`x2`, `x4`) and section 8 prices them the same way. Brave weapons are not built.

**Weapon types.** Sword, Lance, Axe, Bow, Lore (black magic, targets Res; id `reason`), Faith (white magic: heal and a few attacks). Gauntlets (issue 70): physical, range 1 only, targets Def, and spend one use per combat rather than per strike; Iron Gauntlets (Mt 2, Hit 85, Crit 0, Wt 2, 40 uses) and Steel Gauntlets (Mt 4, Hit 80, Crit 0, Wt 5, 30 uses) ship, no crit because four strikes fish for it; two strikes out-damage one iron-sword strike only while the target's Def is under the wielder's Str less one, so they lose to armour. The reaver wields them beside the axe and Keziah carries a pair (DECISIONS/0048). There is **no weapon triangle**; Phase 3 adds Breaker abilities (e.g. Lance Sense: +20 hit/avoid vs lances) as the counterplay layer. Weapons have Mt, Hit, Crit, Wt, Range (min–max), Durability, optional effective tags, and an optional crit bonus against movement types (issue 703).

**Abilities (issue 66).** An ability is data in `content/abilities.json`: an id, a name, one line of text, and one effect from a closed set, never a handler per ability, so the forecast, the resolver and both planners price it through the functions above without knowing it by name. The set today is a *passive* flat stat delta, added after the class modifiers (max HP included), and an *on-combat* modifier of hit, avoid, crit and crit avoid against opponents matching an optional weapon type and movement type, and only while the holder strikes or counters with an optional weapon type of its own (`wielding`, issue 245), added inside the hit and crit chances before their clamps. *Move Again (issue 71)* is the after-action kind, with no numbers; range extension joins the set with the issue that needs it. A class may list `abilities` that every unit in it holds while in the class and loses on leaving it, which is how the outrider carries Move Again. A unit's `abilities`, a class's `abilities` and a class's `mastery` must name entries of the file. **Breakers (issue 69)** are seven such entries, one per weapon type (Fist Sense for gauntlets, issue 70), +20 hit and +20 avoid against it, on the strike and the counter alike; no unit starts with one, and three are class masteries. **Mastery (issue 69):** a class that names a `mastery` also names `masteryPoints`, and a player unit earns one point per combat it fights in its current class, as attacker or defender, alive at the end, and one point per healing spell it casts on an ally in that class and the resolver accepts (issue 245; a consumable earns nothing); at the requirement the ability joins the unit's own `abilities` with a `masteryEarned` event, so it is kept after a class change, and points are kept per class. Deterministic, no roll; enemies earn nothing. Each of the nine starter classes masters one ability (DECISIONS/0047): cadet Fundamentals (+1 Str, Spd, Def), pikeman Horsebane (+20 hit, +10 crit against cavalry), reaver Bloodrush (+15 crit, -10 avoid, while wielding an axe), bowman Steady Aim (+10 hit, +10 crit), adept Deep Study (+2 Mag), chaplain Grace (+10 avoid, +20 crit avoid), outrider Sword Sense, skyrider Bow Sense, bulwark Lore Sense; the requirement is 12 points for every class, a heal counting as a combat (issue 245), so a front-liner masters its class on its second map.

**Ranges.** Melee 1; Bows 2 (2–3 with Phase 3 abilities); Lore/Faith 1–2. A thrown melee weapon reaches 1–2 and is enemy-only in v1: no cast member carries one, so the player's melee stays at 1 and a throw is something to read on the other side (the Toll Axe, issue 131; the Tollgate's door warden carries the Toll Spear, a 1-2 lance, so no tile outside that keep strikes it unanswered, issue 181). A unit with only a 2-range weapon cannot counter at 1, and vice versa.

**Durability.** Physical weapons have uses and break at 0 (broken weapons stay in inventory, usable at −5 Mt / −10 hit as a "broken" fallback so units are never helpless; repair comes with the between-map screen). Every strike a unit makes spends one use, landed or not, so a swing costs the same whether it hits (issue 9). Spells have uses per battle that fully refresh each map; a spell at 0 is not cast again this battle, and a unit whose only weapon is a spent spell is unarmed until the next map, since the broken fallback is for steel, not for words (DECISIONS/0018). A healer whose attacking spell is spent is conditional rather than idle: the healing spell still casts wherever an ally in range is hurt, which is why the cast rule in section 9 asks for one attacking spell and not for two of anything.

**Techniques (issue 68; combat arts until issue 701).** A technique (id kind `art`) is an ability whose effect kind is `art`: a weapon type, the rank it needs (section 6), an extra durability `cost` of at least one use, and deltas to Mt, Hit, Crit, Wt and optionally range (never negative). A unit knows the arts its `abilities` list. It declares one with the attack command, before the roll (`attack <unit> <target> [slot|weapon] [art <id>]`; the console's `attack` and `forecast` take the slot's number or the weapon's id or display name, ignoring case, since an equip reorders the slots, issue 477), and the weapon strikes as the art makes it: the deltas are added to the weapon, Mt and Wt floored at zero and the far end of the range extended, so the forecast, the resolver, burden, doubling and the attacker's own avoid against the counter all read it through the functions above. A Wt delta can therefore cost a fast unit its double, and a heavier weapon lowers its avoid against the counter. The art's cost is spent once per declared attack, on top of one use per strike, whether the strike lands or not: an art free on a miss would be strictly better than the plain attack whenever it is legal, and then it is not a choice. The forecast prints the art's numbers and the most uses the attack can spend. An art is refused when the unit does not know it, the weapon is another type, the unit's rank is below the art's, the weapon is broken, or the weapon has fewer uses left than the art's cost and the first strike (provisional reading of "a weapon at 0 uses cannot pay it", on #68's PR). Only an attack declares an art, so a counter never carries one: a counter the player did not choose must not spend a weapon the player is saving. The enemy planner declares none until content gives an enemy an art, and neither does the Sim's heuristic player. **The first arts (issue 611, round 184; numbers provisional, the Builder's lean on Chat's shape):** Feint (sword, +25 Hit, -2 Mt, cost 1), Heavy Cut (sword, +4 Mt, +3 Wt, -10 Hit, cost 2), Long Thrust (lance, range +1, -3 Mt, cost 2), Cleave (axe, +6 Mt, +10 Crit, -20 Hit, cost 3), Aimed Shot (bow, +20 Hit, +10 Crit, +2 Wt, cost 2), Overcast (Reason, +5 Mt, +4 Wt, cost 2), all at rank E; known at the start by Fenn, Wren, Teodor, Keziah, Ottilie and Pell, one each. Each loses to the plain attack somewhere (a double lost to Wt, a hit penalty, the uses), and a journal in which no art is chosen means the numbers are wrong, not the menu. **The attack menu (issue 611)** is the arts' face: `Queries.AttackOptions` lists the plain attack with each carried weapon, then each known art under each carried weapon of its type, each row with its forecast or the resolver's own refusal; a client opens it on a click on an enemy with more than one legal row, greys a refused row with its reason, and strikes at once when there is one. **Capped arts and the captain's strike (issue 636, 0125; numbers provisional):** an art may carry `perMap`, how many times a unit declares it in one battle (refused once met, "is once a map and is spent"; Recall restores the charge with the board), and `costsNextPhase`, which makes the attack cost the unit its side's next phase: it begins that phase moved and acted, so it neither moves, acts, waits nor braces, and the phase after it is itself again; the forecast's art line prints the uses left this map and the cost. Full Measure (sword, +8 Mt, +30 Hit, +20 Crit, cost 2, once a map, costs the next phase, strikes once) is the captain's, known from the start until his quest pays it. **Single strikes (issue 739, 0170):** an art may carry `single`, and its attack never doubles whatever the speed gap (`Combat.Doubles` reads `Combatant.SingleStrike`); the counter is unchanged, and the forecast's art line prints `x1, never doubles`. Full Measure carries it, so it opens a boss fight rather than ending one.

**Items.** A unit's five slots hold weapons, spells, and consumables; the equipped weapon is the first slot holding a usable weapon that is not a healing spell, and an Attack may name another usable slot, which moves to the front so the counter uses it too; choosing costs nothing extra (issue 99). Consumables live in `items.json` (`id`, `name`, `heals`, `uses`); the first is the Field Dressing (heals 10, 3 uses), an original name, since the content standards forbid franchise names. Section 7's Item action uses one slot: a consumable heals its user and needs no target; a healing spell heals one ally within its range. Either spends a use and ends the action; a consumable at 0 leaves the inventory. Healing a unit already at full HP is refused, so no script burns a use on nothing, and the legal-command list offers an item use only where something would heal.

**Healing.** Faith heal = Mag / 2 + 5 + spell base. Healer gains 11 EXP per heal, +5 if the target was below 50%.

## 6. EXP

```
On any strike landed:  Exp = clamp(10 + 2 * (EnemyLevel - UnitLevel), 1, 30)
On a kill (added):     Exp = clamp(20 + 3 * (EnemyLevel - UnitLevel), 5, 70)
Bosses:                kill bonus +20
```

A unit earns EXP once per combat, computed from the best outcome in that combat: the strike line when any of its strikes landed, plus the kill line when the other unit died, plus the boss bonus; nothing landed earns nothing. Only player units earn EXP; enemies are templates that do not outlive the map, and their numbers never change mid-battle behind a forecast the player already read (issue 8, DECISIONS/0017). Level up at 100, carrying the remainder; a combat is worth at most 120, so it can cross two levels and the engine handles that. At level 30 nothing is awarded and no event is emitted; a level-up that reaches 30 discards the remainder. Growth rolls are the section 3 keys, `(unit, new level, stat)`, tested against the effective growth clamped to 0..100, the same growth `AtLevel` scales by, so a rolled level-up equals the deterministic scaling in expectation. An HP gain raises current HP by the same amount. Two events carry it, `ExpGained` and `LeveledUp` (which stats rose), after the combat and before any death. Healer EXP (section 5) is a formula without a caller until issue 9's heal action.

**Weapon skill ranks (issue 67, provisional numbers).** A unit carries rank points per weapon type; its rank is the highest threshold reached: E 0, D 30, C 80, B 160, A 280, S 450. Every weapon names the rank it needs (`rank` in `weapons.json`, required), and a unit equips only weapons at or below its rank in the type. A player unit that struck with its weapon in a combat earns 3 points in that type, 5 if the combat killed, once per combat like EXP; a heal earns 3 in Faith; a unit that made no strike earns nothing. Enemy templates declare `ranks` in content (letters, each at its threshold), nothing scales them with `enemy_level`, and a template carrying a weapon above its declared rank is a content error. No roll touches a rank. **The ladder (issue 702, DECISIONS/0154, provisional; Lotus's batch, rounds 216 and 217):** E is iron (Iron Sword, Lance, Axe, Bow and Gauntlets, the Hatchet), Cinder, Gust and Salve; D is steel (Steel Sword, Lance, Axe, Bow and Gauntlets), Bolt and Radiance; C is the specialty weapons (Ridgeblade, Longpike, any later effective-tag weapon) and Beacon; B is obsidian; A and S gate nothing in v1. Story and enemy weapons (the Toll weapons, the Post Maul, Kinsbane, the Family Lance) stay E. A template carrying steel declares rank D (the content error above names the slot); Maud starts at Faith D for her Radiance. **Obsidian** (Obsidian Sword, Spear, Axe and Bow) is glass: steel's Mt +2 and hit, Crit 25, iron's weight, 8 uses, twice steel's price, stocked on the screens after the raid. A weapon marked `glass` (a priced physical weapon bound to no one) is never repaired and never Refined; the smith refuses both with "You don't mend glass. You buy another." A signature is held by the ceiling (section 14) to the shop's weapons of its type from its own rank up to D, so an E signature still meets steel. The rare forge material reads **frozen iron** on screen (id `rareMaterial` kept). **Frozen iron and chill (#702 slice 2, DECISIONS/0155, provisional).** A weapon is frozen iron when its content says `frozenIron: true` (refused at load on a hungering weapon or a spell), when it is an heirloom at its last stage (the woken Family Lance), or when it refines on frozen iron and its stack has taken every rare step; no shop sells one, and Kinsbane never is one. A hit from it on a unit that survives the combat chills it: Mov -1, never below 1, until the end of the struck side's next phase (struck on its own side's phase, that is the phase after; on the other side's, the coming one). A second hit refreshes the clock and takes no second point; a miss does nothing; bosses are chilled. Every reach reads it (`BattleState.ReachOf`), so `threat`, the forecast from a tile, Move and both planners plan the shorter reach. The forecast appends ` chills` to a frozen-iron side's strike columns, the card prints `Frozen iron: a hit chills` under the weapon and `chilled: Mov -1 until [the next] <side> phase ends` on the chilled, and the event reads `<name> is chilled: ...`. The signature ceiling reads Power and Acc as before (chill is damage that never lands); if gate 4 shows chill alone pushing a carrier clearly positive, the lever is the first hit of a combat only. The ladder is retuned against the campaign rank report once all ten maps exist. **Grounding (issue 703, DECISIONS/0156, provisional; Lotus's batch item 5, round 216).** Bows carry no effective tag; each carries `critAgainst: [flying]` and `critBonus: 20` in `weapons.json`, added to its Crit against a flier in `Combat.CritChance`, so the forecast and both planners price it through the one function. Gust keeps its effective tag. A bow's crit on a flier deals plain damage, not triple, for either side (issue 723, round 220, DECISIONS/0168; any other crit, and a bow's crit on anything else, still triples), and a flier which survives the combat is grounded until the end of its side's next phase (the chill's clock: struck on the other side's phase, through its coming phase; on its own, through the next one). A grounded flier moves with infantry costs and passability (`Grounding.MovementOf`; every reach, the enemy planner's pathing, exposure and the Sim's approach read it); its terrain bonuses stay a flier's. Grounded on a tile infantry cannot enter (water) it has no move, and still strikes from where it fell. A second crit refreshes the clock. The forecast prints `grounds N%` in place of `crit N%` for a side whose bow faces a flier (issue 723; the damage column is the same either way, and the protocol side carries `critGrounds`), the card prints `grounded until [the next] <side> phase ends, moves on foot` (or `stranded: no move, may still act`), the event reads `<name> is grounded: moves on foot until [the next] <side> phase ends`, and the item card of every bow says `Crit +20 against flying. A crit grounds a flier.` The header `effective_bows: on` (section 10) keeps the bow before this rule on the samples whose journaled plays were made under it.

## 7. Turn structure

Player phase → Enemy phase → (Ally phase if any) → turn counter increments. On a unit's turn: **Move** (optional), then one of **Attack / Item / Wait**. Cavalry get **Move Again** (issue 71): after an Attack, Item or Wait, a unit with Move Again moves again on its Mov minus the path cost its Move spent, through the section 4 reach from where it stands, and is then done. Its own tile is a legal Move Again, so declining is a command the transcript shows; an untaken Move Again lapses when its phase ends. The wake check runs after every command (section 8), the Move included, so a Move Again cannot ride out of a proximity wake its own Move stop already fired; what it can escape is a wake it has not yet caused: its strike's noise wakes the group on the Attack, and the Move Again then rides away before that group's phase. The enemy AI does not take one.

**Win conditions** (one per map): Rout, Seize (captain on `T`), Defeat Boss, Survive N turns, Escape (the captain leaves through an exit tile; issue 269).
**Loss conditions:** captain dies; or a map-specific protect-target dies (the `protect:` header, section 10).

**Outcome.** `BattleState.Outcome` is computed from the board after every command, never stored, and read in this order: captain dead (lost), protected recruit dead or left behind (lost), the win condition met (won), the turn past `turn_limit` (won for Survive, lost for everything else), else ongoing. Seize is the captain standing on a throne tile; Escape is the captain having exited (below); Rout is no enemy alive; Defeat Boss is no boss alive. A decided battle refuses every command but Recall, so a loss is the moment Recall is for.

**Exit** (issue 269; DECISIONS/0056). On an Escape map a player unit standing on an exit tile may take `exit` as its action, in place of Attack, Item or Wait, without a Move: only a unit that began its turn on the exit leaves, and one that moved this turn, or that an ally shoved there this turn (13.12), is refused (`MovedBeforeExit`; issue 377, DECISIONS/0074; issue 396), so whoever means to leave stands on the edge through an enemy phase first and the exits are a door that is paid for, not walked through. The header `exit_after_move: on` (section 10) keeps the older rule, a Move and then the exit, and sits only on the sample maps whose journaled plays were made under it. It is not taken after another action and no Move Again follows it, so a unit strikes or leaves, never both. The unit leaves the board for the state's escaped list: nothing on the board can target it, be woken by it, or count it, and Recall undoes an exit like any other command. The captain's exit wins the battle, and every player unit still on the board at that moment is left behind, which counts as fallen in the outcome, the battle summary and the campaign (the answer death gets). A protected recruit left behind loses the battle, read where its death is. Enemies never exit. Healing terrain (section 4) heals the units of the side whose phase begins, the terrain's percent of max HP with integer floor, capped at max (issue 7).

**Recall.** 3 charges per map on Normal. Rewinds to any previous player-phase state in the current map's history; Recall is the player's action, so the enemy phase's own states are never a target, and a Recall into one is refused without spending a charge, naming the nearest player-phase states (issue 190, DECISIONS/0032). Charges do not refresh mid-map. **The Recall browser (issue 75)** prices a rewind before it is spent: `recall list` names every state a Recall may return to with its turn, the command that made it and what a rewind there undoes, the kills, EXP, levels and enemy HP given back and the units, HP and not-yet-arrived reinforcements returned, all read by one function (`RecallCost.Of`) from the difference between that state and the present; `recall n` prints the same line as it rewinds, then that the rolls do not change. The list is the confirmation: nothing is spent by reading it, and a spent charge does not come back.

**Recall restores the rolls.** The same attack after a Recall gets the same rolls; a Recall lets you choose differently, never reroll. Rolls are keyed (issue 31, section 5), so this holds at the roll level: the same strike on the same turn draws the same numbers whatever was resolved before it, and a resolver test shows it. It becomes a fact of the game once `BattleState` (issue 6) carries only the seed and no stream position; that issue's acceptance holds the three Recall tests.

**Taking back a move (issue 676).** A misclicked move is answered without Recall: `undo <unit>` (the client's Esc or right-click on a selected unit) returns a unit that has moved and not acted to the tile it began the phase on, unmoved, with no charge and no history entry, since the state the move left comes back whole. It is refused, naming why, unless the move was the last command and changed nothing but the unit's tile: a move followed by any other command, an action (so Move Again's second move too), a wake, a fired map event, or on a dusk map an enemy unseen before the move and seen after it (round 207) makes the move final, as does any other change. A free take-back must never probe a wake ring, a trigger or the dark; that knowledge is what Recall is priced for. A move rolls nothing, so an undo leaks nothing about the dice. The Sim and the planner never take one.

## 8. Enemy AI

Every enemy belongs to a *group* with one behavior:

- `Aggressive` — moves toward and attacks the best target every turn.
- `Hold` — never moves; attacks anything in range.
- `Guard` — behaves as Hold until the group *wakes*; then the whole group becomes Aggressive. This is the main pacing tool for maps. A group wakes on any of: **proximity** (after any player command, a player unit stands within the wake radius of any member), **noise** (any combat involving a tile within the wake radius plus 2 of any member), or **death** (any member dies, at any distance). The wake radius is one global constant, 4 tiles, Manhattan distance, walls not considered; it lives in content, never in code and never in a map file, and it is identical on every map so the player learns it once and counting tiles is a skill, not a discovery. The check runs after every player command on where units stand, never on tiles passed through, so the player-facing rule is "do not stop close," not "do not get close": a unit with the movement for it may run past a sleeping group and end beyond the radius, which is a real play, countable in advance, and Cavalry's first job that is not arriving early. The dash is free only if nothing fights on the way through; the noise radius is what prices the greedy version. Waking emits an event naming the group and the cause. `threat <unit> from <tile>` names the groups a stop there would wake, by proximity and through links, from the same predicate on the board with the unit moved, unpriced (`stopping here wakes: weir (proximity)`; issue 458). While any group is asleep, both board views (the opening screen and `map`) print the rule under the unit rows as one line built from the radii, `asleep: wakes if a unit ends within 4 tiles of a member, a combat happens within 6, or a member dies`, and the protocol's state carries `wakeRadius` and `noiseRadius`, so a rule the map depends on is on the screen and not only here (issue 260). The three causes are one predicate in code (`WakeCheck`), read by the resolver after every command and by the exposure sum of section 11 on the board a plan certainly produces, so the veto and the rules cannot drift apart (issue 128). There are no trigger rectangles. The radius is `wakeRadius` in `content/rules.json` (issue 10), the file for rule constants that belong to no map; the loader refuses a negative one. The check runs inside the resolver after every accepted command on either phase, so a woken group is a fact of the board that a Recall restores with the rest: on the enemy phase only noise and a death can newly fire, since neither player units nor sleeping members move, and a group woken mid enemy phase acts as Aggressive from the next member in id order. One event per group per command, naming the loudest cause in the order death, noise, proximity. A map may link groups with the `wake_links:` header (section 10; issue 393, DECISIONS/0080): when the first group of a pair wakes, by any cause, the second wakes in the same check if it still sleeps, transitively, with the cause `call` (`group weir wakes: called by ford`, and `by` in the protocol). The one predicate carries it, so the resolver and the exposure sum agree, and while the called group sleeps a line under the wake legend names the link (`called: group weir wakes when group ford does`). A link runs one way. Harrow Weir links `ford>weir`, so the south crossing is a door the Foreman answers.
- `Boss` — Hold, plus never leaves its tile.

A boss is any enemy on a `B` line, and its behavior is `Boss` unless the line says `behavior:guard` (issue 259, DECISIONS/0055): a guard boss sleeps as Hold on its own tile, wakes by the three causes like any Guard group, and once awake acts as Aggressive, so it may leave its tile and may end on a throne. It is still the boss for Defeat Boss, the kill bonus and the `!` glyph, and the console names it `boss, asleep` or `boss, awake`. A Seize boss that can never act before the captain arrives is scenery; a guard boss is how a Seize map gives its boss a turn.

The AI is a planner, never a mutator: `EnemyAi.Plan(state, content)` returns the enemy phase's command list, ending in `EndPhase` unless the battle was decided on the way, and the caller applies it through the same resolver the planner used on its working copy, so the two agree by construction. A unit's plan is at most a Move then an Attack or a Wait. Hold, Boss, and a sleeping Guard consider only their own tile; a woken Guard is Aggressive.

Target scoring, deterministic (ties broken by lowest unit id):
```
score = 100 if the attack can kill
      + expectedDamageDealt * hitChance/100
      - expectedDamageTaken * counterHitChance/100 * 0.5
      + 10 if the target cannot counter
      + 5  if the target is a healer
```
`expectedDamage` on both lines includes the crit expectation, `Damage * (1 + 2 * CritChance / 100)`, times the strikes that side makes (2 when it doubles), capped at the HP it could remove, using the same functions the forecast uses. The whole score is a double, since the section 5 convention of integer floor would make `2 * CritChance / 100` zero for every crit under 50 and delete the term in the band it exists for. "Can kill" reads deterministic damage times strikes against remaining HP, never the expectation, so an attack lethal only on a crit is never priced as a kill (issue 10, Chat's two hazards, each held by a test), and it counts only the strikes the attacker lives to make: a kill that needs the second round is a kill only when the defender does not strike back or its plain first round, which falls between the attacker's two, is less than the attacker's HP (issue 315, the deterministic reading of issue 147's survival). A gauntlet's round is both its strikes with nothing between them. `threat`'s "if all land" reads the same predicate, so the planner and the screen cannot disagree. The enemy prices what the player sees, so a modifier that lowers a unit's crit avoid (13.1) makes that unit a better target in the enemy's own arithmetic rather than a cost the enemy is blind to. "Cannot counter" is the target having no weapon that reaches back at that distance; a healer is a unit carrying a healing spell its class can use.

Tile choice: among tiles that allow the best-scoring attack, prefer highest terrain avoid, then fewest player units that can reach the tile next phase, then (cost from the mover, row-major) so the answer is unique. The score is taken per (target, tile), since the mover's terrain changes the counter it takes, and a tie in score goes to the lower target id before any tile key. "Can reach the tile" means the tile is in that player unit's reach set as section 4 defines it, computed once per plan on the board before the mover moves, which is what `ironwake reach` prints, so the count can be checked by hand.

**Weapon choice.** An enemy's attack options range over every weapon it can strike with, in inventory order: each (tile, weapon, target) is scored by the formula above on the unit with that weapon in front, and the winning option's Attack names its slot under section 5's Items rule (issue 99), so the weapon it last swung is the one it counters with. An Attack with the equipped weapon names no slot. An option equal to the best on the whole order does not replace it, so of two weapons scoring the same the earlier slot strikes. Hold and Boss still consider only their own tile; the approach rule reads the equipped weapon (issue 174). The exposure sum of section 11 ranges over the same weapons: each enemy is counted at the worst of every weapon it can strike with, from every tile that weapon reaches the unit from, so the heuristic never stands at range 2 of a boss it believes has no reach there; the counter to the unit's own attack is the weapon the target holds in front, the one it last swung (issue 131). The bandit leader carries the Steel Axe in front and the enemy-only Toll Axe (1-2) behind it: adjacent to a cadet the Steel Axe scores higher, so the throw is what he does at range 2, and after a throw he counters at 1 and 2 until he swings steel again.

**Approach.** The tile rule above is scoped to tiles that allow an attack, so it says nothing about a turn on which an Aggressive unit can attack nobody, and "moves toward" is not an algorithm. The approach is a rule for the same reason the path tie-break is (DECISIONS/0012): a debug prediction written from this document and the implementation must arrive at the same tile, so the choice cannot be an accident of the code (Design Table, fifth round). When no reachable tile allows any attack:

- **Target:** the player unit whose nearest *attack tile* (a tile the mover can end on, from which its equipped weapon reaches that unit) has the lowest path cost from the mover's own tile, computed with section 4's costs and occupancy and no Mov budget. Ties by lowest unit id, section 8's existing tie-break. A unit with no path to any attack tile is not a target; a mover with no target waits where it stands.
- **Destination:** among the mover's reachable tiles (section 4, own tile included), the one with the lowest remaining path cost to any attack tile on the chosen target. Ties by highest terrain avoid, then fewest player units whose reach set contains the tile, then (cost from the mover's tile, then row-major), DECISIONS/0012's contract as the final key, so the answer is unique. Path cost throughout, never Manhattan, so terrain means the same thing to the approach as to everything else.
- **Order:** enemy units act in ascending unit id, each on the board as the one before it left it. On a `pincer: on` map an anvil acts first (13.13, issue 419, DECISIONS/0083): an enemy whose step beside a player unit gives an unclaimed group-mate a pinned strike worth more than its own best strike moves there ahead of the rest, and that group-mate is claimed for the phase. `threat <unit> [from <tile>]` on such a map adds one unpriced row per anvil plan against the unit, read on the phase-start board as every `threat` row is (`anvil: shieldbearer-1 could step to 17,0 so soldier-1 strikes you pinned from 19,0`, or `could hold` on its own tile), outside `if all land` and not binding (issue 457; the protocol's `anvils`). The remaining-cost query is `Movement.DistancesTo`, one multi-source Dijkstra on the reversed graph over the same costs and occupancy as `Reach`, so nothing re-counts; an attack tile is one the mover's movement type can enter, occupied by nobody but the mover, within its equipped weapon's range of the target.

**The throne-holder rule** (issue 321, fifty-first to fifty-third rounds; DECISIONS/0063). On a Seize map an enemy standing on the throne when it acts leaves it only to strike this phase: its attack options are unchanged, so a seated holder that can strike from any reachable tile still moves and strikes (the Reeve's kite from the yard mouth), and one that cannot Waits on the throne instead of taking the approach or drift. One predicate (`EnemyAi.HoldsTheThrone`); `threat` and the exposure sum count only strikes and need no change. Daylight or dusk alike. On shipped maps it binds the seated Reeve on Sallow Grange; the Tollgate's boss is a Boss beside its throne, not on it, and the outrider trial's hexer holds.

**The boss veto** (issue 385, seventy-fourth round; DECISIONS/0077, provisional). On a Defeat Boss map the boss is the enemy's captain, and it plans under the exposure veto of section 11 read from the enemy side. While it may choose where it ends (Aggressive and not yet moved), a strike is refused when the no-crit sum on the tile it would end on, the counter it takes included, reaches its current HP, and so is an approach tile; with every strike refused it takes the best approach tile that passes, or holds its own tile when none passes. The veto picks the tile, not the swing (issue 389, seventy-sixth round; DECISIONS/0079, provisional): once the end tile is chosen, the boss takes the best strike it can make from that tile by the ordinary score, grudge and keepsake precedence included, and only Waits when nothing is in reach from it. It never moves to a tile the veto refused, so a pocket stays closed, but a worn boss standing beside a wounded unit swings at it. The sum is `Exposure.OfBoss`: the board and counter of section 11's sum, every player unit counted at its worst plain damage from any tile of its reach that its weapons strike the tile from, a tile another player unit stands on open to it (the player phase plays in any order), and one striker per tile, the worst seating (`threat`'s count, issue 253). The veto is arithmetic, so a thin party opens tiles a whole one closes, and a boss worn down stays back where a fresh one advances, striking only what comes within reach of the tile it keeps. A boss that holds keeps striking from its own tile, since it has no tile to choose. `threat` reads the planner's own strike, so a strike the veto refuses is not listed and the swing from the chosen end tile is. When the veto is why the boss does not strike the unit at all (it could from some reachable tile, the veto refuses every one, and the swing does not reach), one unpriced row names the refused tile nearest the boss and where its plan ends, `weir_foreman-1 could reach 12,4 but refuses it: too exposed there; holds 14,6` (issue 565, protocol `refusals`), so a tile that reads clear inside a boss's walk says why, and the veto is a rule on the screen a player can bait. Its answer is priced on the board at query time, and the boss's choice moves with every unit placed after it, since each is part of the sum. **A guard boss goes home** (issue 393, seventy-eighth round; DECISIONS/0080, provisional). When the veto refused a strike the boss could otherwise make this phase, a boss whose map line says `behavior:guard` and that has a post, the tile of its map placement, ends on the reachable tile nearest that post (Manhattan distance, then its own tile, then the lower movement cost) instead of the best approach tile, and swings from there as above. That tile is not vetoed. A spawned boss has no post and approaches as before, and so does a guard boss with no strike in reach at all, since waking him must mean something. So a gathered party that outweighs him no longer herds him away from the ground he guards: he walks back to it, and a passing strike is still taken from anywhere. `threat` reads the same end tile. On Harrow Weir, the only shipped Defeat Boss map, the woken Foreman refuses the 10,6 pocket as a strike tile and holds or returns to his hill at 13,6; walking home from the west bank, the nearest reachable tile to the hill can be 10,6 itself, and Code's seed 409 play broke him there (DECISIONS/0080, open).

On Old Mill Road as placed, this sends the brigand at 6,5 to 3,6 on enemy phase 1: 3,6, 4,7 and 5,8 all sit at remaining cost 2 to an attack tile on the recruit at 2,8, all Plain, all inside both player units' reach, all at cost 4 from 6,5, and row-major takes y=6. Issue 10 holds that case as a table-driven test named for this rule; a determinism test (the phase replays for the same seed) does not protect it, because a hash-ordered pick among the three replays too.

## 9. Content plan for v1

- **Cast.** Captain (fixed sword unit, Cha-heavy) + 10 recruits, 3–4 from each region, covering every weapon type and movement type at least once. Each recruit has a one-line personality, a home region, and two people they get on with (support hooks for Phase 3). Names are original. A Lore or Faith recruit carries at least two castable spells, at least one of them an attacking spell: a spent spell does not equip, and a healing spell is legal only when an ally in range is hurt, so two healing spells leave the unit with Move and Wait on a healthy turn (issue 113; the loader refuses the entry). A cast entry may name a `pronoun` (`he`, `she`, `they`; issue 615). A line that refers back to a unit it has just named uses that pronoun, with the plural verb for `they`, or repeats the unit's name when the entry has none. No line calls a person "it". A template carrying a pronoun is refused.
- **Campaign.** 8 maps, escalating: 2 tutorials-in-disguise (small maps, one new idea each), 4 mid maps introducing Guard groups, reinforcements, forts, and a Seize, 2 finale maps. Maps are 12×10 to 20×16, but for Starting Alone, a lesson at 12×8 (issue 631). The shipped order (issue 288, issue 631, issue 632) is Starting Alone, The Mill, Saltmarsh Ford, the Tollgate, Harrow Weir, the raid on Ironwake, Sallow Grange, Brackwater Cut, and the keep; map 7 (#81) slots in before the keep, which is the finale #83 builds on. The keep and its raid are read from `content/keep`, every other map from `content/maps`. The story order (section 14, DECISIONS/0121) puts Starting Alone (#631, built) first, ten maps in all, and changes who deploys where; a recruit a `campaign.json` map names in `arrives` (issue 632, DECISIONS/0124) is off the roster until that map, joins its battle and stays if it stands; every other recruit is on the roster from map 1 until #632's later slices name them. Old Mill Road left the campaign for The Mill (0124) and stays in `content/maps` as a standalone board and test fixture.
- **Map authoring constraint.** Armored's job is blocking, and with no zone of control that job is binary: a one-tile corridor with a body in it is an absolute wall, a two-tile one is nothing. Maps that want the wall must draw the corridor. Archers need a back line to protect, which means forests behind a wall of two or a corridor, not a third body.
- **Difficulty.** A ladder of three (issue 664, DECISIONS/0134): **Recruit**, **Captain** and **Tactician**. Captain is `normal`, renamed on screen with its id kept; it is the tuned baseline, and every gate and Fun Gate is read on it. Difficulty is data: a `difficulties` block in `rules.json` (issue 76), each entry a per-stat `statPercent` (omitted stats 100, rounded to nearest, HP at least 1), an `enemyLevelOffset` on the map's `enemy_level` (held to 1..30), either a `recall` count replacing the map's or a `recallOffset` added to it (held to 0..99), a display `name`, and an `unlockedBy` naming the difficulty a campaign must be won on first. `normal` must be declared and must be the identity, so Captain is data and not a code path. A map is put under a difficulty once, by `MapDefinition.Under`, and every enemy, spawns included, comes through `EnemyUnit`; nothing else asks which difficulty is in play. It is chosen once per campaign (the campaign record, issue 74) and never per map; `--difficulty` takes the id or the name. Recruit is one Recall more and every enemy stat at 92 percent; Tactician is one Recall fewer and every enemy stat at 105 percent and the enemy level up 1, and opens once a campaign is won on Captain, a win kept in the profile (`profile.txt` beside the saves, outside every record; a scripted run without `--saves` keeps no profile and may play any difficulty). The numbers are the Sim's: gate 1 about 15 points either side of Captain on the three tuned maps, gates 2 to 8 passing (`docs/measurements/2026-10-01-difficulty-ladder-664.txt`; Tactician is below gate 1's floor by design). The Sim measures one with `--full <map> --difficulty <id>`. Each entry may carry a `tier` (-9 to 9, Captain 0; Recruit -1, Tactician 1), its place on the ladder, lower easier. **Lowering** (issue 677, DECISIONS/0140, provisional): at a camp, `difficulty <name>` moves the campaign to a difficulty of a lower tier for the rest of it, never a higher or the same one and never mid-battle; the record keeps what it was lowered from (`loweredFrom`) and its rules line prints `difficulty Recruit (lowered from Captain)`. Permadeath is fixed for the run. The title screen (Continue, the newest save, shown only when one exists and then the default; New game; Load; Options; Quit; under it `Ironwake, rules version N`) and the Options (enemy phase speed 1x, 2x, 5x or instant; battle scenes; confirm end turn; reach on hover; UI scale 100, 125 or 150; sound and volume) are the client's, the options kept as lines of the profile (`docs/PROTOCOL.md`).
- **Permadeath toggle** (issue 664, DECISIONS/0134). Chosen once at the start (`--permadeath on|off`, on by default) and printed on the record beside the difficulty. Off, a unit that falls on a won map or on a side map comes back **Wounded (2)**: 2 taken from its two highest stats other than HP (as its class reads them; a tie to the earlier stat; never below 0), for the next two *main* maps, fought or not, the count printed on its card; the EXP toward its next level is lost; it comes back as it began that battle (level, items, counters), and it can still deploy and fights wounded, on side maps too, which never count a wound down. A second fall restarts the wound. The penalty is taken from the unit's own stats and given back whole when the wound expires, so a level gained while wounded is kept. Nothing is lost with the owner: bound items, Kinsbane's count and the heirloom's counter stay, and a member who falls on their own side map does not close it. The captain's death and a `protect:` death still lose the map on both settings.
- **Between maps.** Minimal: repair, shop with fixed stock, level/class screen. Text only. Built in issue 74 (DECISIONS/0051, numbers provisional): `content/campaign.json` lists the maps in campaign order, each with the `reward` its win pays into the purse and the `stock` sold on the screen before it (fixed, unlimited, a plan the player can make two maps ahead), and optional `before` and `after` text cards (issue 631: at most six paragraphs of printable ASCII, each at most 400 characters; the console prints `before` above the map's screen and `after` below its won line, wrapped to 72 columns, as screen text the event log leaves out), beside the `startingPurse` and the `certificationPrice`; a weapon or item sells only with a `price`, and the loader refuses a stock entry without one. A campaign is a `CampaignRecord`: the roster in roster order as the last map left each unit (level, EXP, ranks, mastery points, weapon uses), the fallen, the purse, the next map's index, the seed, the difficulty (chosen once) and the bench. The screen before every map, the first included, buys at full uses into a free slot; repairs a weapon to full at `max(1, price / durability)` a missing use, refusing a spell (it refreshes) and a weapon without a price (the Toll weapons); certifies through issue 72's check, paying the seal from the purse, or, for a class `campaign.json`'s optional `trials` names (class id to a map under `content/trials`), through that class's trial in place of the seal (issue 252, DECISIONS/0057): `trial <unit> <class>` plays it on the screen after the same check, one attempt per unit and class per camp, a pass certifying with the level, EXP, stats and ranks its fights left and the unit's own inventory, abilities and mastery points, a failure (a fall in the trial included) changing nothing but the attempt, the trial on the campaign seed plus the map count plus the next map's index and under no difficulty; and benches, which here is a deployment choice and not gate 4's ablation: the benched unit leaves the roster passed to the battle, so the next recruit in roster order fills its bare slot, while the captain, the `protect:` recruit and a recruit the map places by name cannot be benched. Every refusal names the price and the balance, or the requirement. After a won map, a deployed unit missing from the board has fallen and leaves the roster for good, and a named slot whose recruit has fallen stays empty; survivors keep what the battle left them, spells refreshed, and the consumable uses a `supplies` cap held back are returned, since the cap is what a unit brings into the battle and the rest stays in the wagon. A lost map ends *this* campaign; Recall is the answer inside a battle, and a save is the way back (issue 663, DECISIONS/0133): every camp autosaves its record before anything is bought, keeping the last three (`auto-1` the newest), `save <name>` writes a named one and `saves` lists them; there is no save inside a battle, and `quit` there (or the input ending undecided) writes a suspend, the record the battle began from plus the battle's lines, which `--resume` replays once and deletes. A lost map plays the bad-ending card (a placeholder until #656), then the fail menu: `load <name>`, `new` (the same seed and difficulty from map 1) or `quit`. A save is the campaign record's protocol file under `--saves <dir>` (`saves/` at the keyboard; a scripted run keeps none unless the flag is given); `--load <name>` starts from one. Each map's battle runs on the campaign seed plus the map's index, since combat keys name no map and one seed would give the same strike on the same turn the same roll on every map. The console is `ironwake campaign [--seed N] [--script f] [--strict] [--difficulty id] [--saves dir] [--load save | --resume]`: the screen's commands, `march`, then every `play` command and `leave` once the battle is decided; the record is a protocol shape (`record`, `docs/PROTOCOL.md`), so a campaign is a file. **The camp as one view (issue 678):** the screen opens, and `camp` reprints, the heading, then four panels in order, each under `== <name> ==`: Roster (each member's row, the fallen with the board each fell on, which the record carries as `fellOn`, who deploys, and the leave warning), Keep (the purse first, the rooms, then the walls and ditches once the raid is fought, else why they are closed), Quests (each open side map with its price, `permadeath applies` or, with permadeath off, that who falls comes back wounded; then those won and what each paid) and Shop (the stock, the seal, the trials); the last line is `march`, the one command that leaves, naming the next map and the objective its battle opens with. The commands are unchanged; the Godot client's camp draws the same panels from the same functions.

## 10. Map file format

A `.map` file is a header of `key: value` lines, then the grid, then a `units:` block, then an optional `chests:` block, then an optional `events:` block. Glyphs per section 4. Coordinates are `x,y` from the top-left, 0-based. Header keys: `name`, `size` (`WxH`), `win` (`rout`, `seize`, `defeat_boss`, `survive`, `escape`), `turn_limit` (required; every map has one, gate 1 counts against it), `recall` (default 3), `enemy_level` (default 1), `exit` (Escape maps only: the exit tiles as `x,y` separated by spaces, at least as many as the map has player slots, so every deployed unit has one to stand on), `protect` (optional: the id of a `recruit:<id>` slot on this map whose death loses the map, section 7), the optional `cheap_shots` below, and the optional `retreat: on`, which turns on enemy retreat for the map (13.10, issue 33; DECISIONS/0037). The optional `rivalry: <arm>` turns on Rapport and Rivalry for the map (13.1, issue 16; DECISIONS/0038), the arm an id under `rivalry.arms` in `rules.json`. The optional `supplies: N` (1 to 99) caps every consumable stack (an entry of `items.json`) a deployed player unit carries at N uses when the map starts (issue 160; DECISIONS/0039). It is a cap and never a set, so a stack already under it keeps its own and a map can take supplies away but never hand out more than a unit carried; weapons, spells and enemies are untouched. The optional `announce: on` (issue 78; DECISIONS/0052) makes the console list the map's events in player words (`turn 3, enemy phase: a rider arrives at 7,0 (aggressive). A unit standing on 7,0 stops it.`, every spawn line carrying the held-tile rule; issue 256) before the first command and on `map` while any is still to fire, so a reinforcement turn is counted in advance rather than learned when it lands; a map without it keeps its events unannounced (the Tollgate's rider, DECISIONS/0045), and the header is refused on a map with no `events:` block. The optional `keepsakes: on` turns on Carry the fallen for the map (13.8, experiment): a player unit that falls leaves its weapon on its tile for an ally to `recover`, and an enemy that ends a move on it carries it off (issue 295). The optional `grudges: on` turns on the grudge arm of Named rivals for the map (13.4, experiment): an enemy whose group-mate a player unit killed strikes that unit first. The optional `exit_after_move: on` (Escape maps only; issue 377) keeps the Escape rule before DECISIONS/0074, under which a unit may move onto an exit and exit in the same turn; it exists so the journaled plays made under that rule still replay, and no map under `content/maps` carries it. The optional `effective_bows: on` (issue 703) keeps the bow before DECISIONS/0156: effective against fliers, no crit bonus against them, and no grounding; like `exit_after_move` it sits only on the samples whose journaled plays were made under it. The optional `shove: on` lets a player unit push an orthogonally adjacent ally one tile straight away as its action (13.12, kept as ally pushes; issue 355). The optional `pincer: on` turns on the pincer for the map (13.13, experiment): a unit struck from beside it while a foe of the striker stands directly behind it is hit at +15, counters included, and the enemy steps in as an anvil to set pins for its group (issue 419, DECISIONS/0083). The optional `brace: on` turns on the brace for the map (13.14, experiment): a unit that waits on the tile it began its phase on is struck at -15 hit until its side's next phase (issue 425, DECISIONS/0084). The optional `break: on` turns on the break for the map (13.22, experiment): when a boss dies, each member of his group at or below half HP leaves the board, not a kill (DECISIONS/0112). The optional `messenger: <x,y> <x,y>` names a messenger (13.24, experiment; DECISIONS/0135): the enemy on the first tile, never a boss, runs for the second, an edge tile, and on reaching it leaves the board and fires every event whose trigger is `messenger`; the map needs at least one such event, and the trigger is refused on a map without the header. The optional `kinsbane: <recruit>` issues the hungering scythe to a recruit placed by name, at the front of their pack (13.23, experiment; DECISIONS/0130). The optional `woken: <recruit>` issues the heirloom bound to a recruit placed by name at its last stage, frozen iron, at the front of their pack (issue 702, samples only; DECISIONS/0155). The optional `wildfire: on` turns on wildfire for the map (13.15, experiment): a hit from an igniting weapon (Cinder) on a unit in forest sets the tile alight once the combat ends, and fire burns and spreads as 13.15 says (issue 435, DECISIONS/0085). The optional `windup: on` turns on the windup for the map (13.16, experiment): an attack with a weapon marked `windup` raises a blow over the target's tile that lands at the wielder's side's next phase start (issue 441, DECISIONS/0086; killed, DECISIONS/0094, the header kept so the transcripts replay). The optional `dusk: N` (1 to 128) makes the map a dusk map (13.7, experiment): each side's sight is N tiles on turn 1 and one less each turn, never under 1, and neither side strikes what it cannot see. The optional `wake_links: <from>><to>[, <from>><to> ...]` links Guard groups (issue 393, section 8): when the first group wakes, the second wakes with it; each group must be on the map, the second must have a Guard member, a group never links to itself, and a pair is listed once. The optional `oathbound: <group>[, <group> ...]` (issue 691) marks enemy groups sworn to the Kin: every enemy in one is oath-bound, which an ability's `against: { "oathbound": true }` reads (Unsworn); each group must be on the map and is listed once. It stands in for STORY draft 2's oath-bound flag until the story builds one. On a `break: on` map a member of an oath-bound group never breaks, and a boss's forecast prints `sworn: will not break: <names>` for his sworn (issue 692). The optional `fronts: <name> <x,y> [<x,y> ...][; <name> <x,y> ...]` names the map's fronts (issue 692, the keep finale's first slice; DECISIONS/0147): each a one-word lower-case name (an underscore prints as a space) and its tiles, a tile in one front only, no enemy starting on one. A front falls, once, after any command on which an enemy stands on one of its tiles or an enemy's move passed through one; the fall prints `<front> falls: the wave is inside`, fires every event whose trigger is `falls <front>`, and never loses the map. The board prints the rule and `fronts: <front> <tiles> holding|fallen; ...`, and `help` prints the rule. The planner does not read fronts, save the hunter. The optional `hunter: <x,y>` (issue 692, slice 2; DECISIONS/0148) needs `fronts:` and names the enemy placed on that tile, never a boss or the messenger, as the hunter (Marrit's rule): each player unit defends the front nearest it within 3 tiles (Manhattan to the front's nearest tile, the earlier front on a tie); the hunted front is the standing front whose defenders' summed HP is lowest, the earlier on a tie, fixed as each enemy phase begins and read fresh on the player phase; the hunter strikes and approaches only that front's defenders, marches on the front's tiles when it has none to approach (the party left out of the path field), still counters anyone, and hunts nothing once every front has fallen. `threat` names the unit's front and the hunt (`<hunter> hunts the <front> next: N defenders, H hp (weakest); strikes only its defenders`), prices no hunter strike on a unit off the hunted front, and the board's fronts line adds each standing front's defenders and HP and marks the hunted one. The optional `pair_rule: <group>[, <group> ...]` (issue 692, slice 4; DECISIONS/0150, provisional) binds enemy groups by the pair rule, each on the map and listed once: a bound enemy striking or answering a unit with an ally orthogonally beside it strikes once, never doubling and never critting, the ally read on the board as the strike is priced, so a partner fallen earlier in the phase no longer guards; against a unit alone it keeps both. The board prints the rule and `pair rule binds: <names>`, and `help` prints the rule. The optional `freed: <x,y> by <group>` (issue 750; DECISIONS/0172, provisional) binds the enemy placed on that tile, never a boss or the messenger, to the boss of that group, placed or spawned: when a boss of the group dies she leaves the board, `<name> lays down the weapon: freed`, after his death and before the win, and it is not a kill (no EXP, no drop, gone for a rout). Killed first, she is an ordinary kill; the board records it (`bond fell`) and a won map carries it onto the campaign record (`freedUnitFell`), for the founding's ending once #656 opens. The board, `threat` and every forecast with the boss or her in it print `<her> is bound to <boss>: freed when <boss> falls` while she stands. The planner does not read it. The finale's stand-ins `finale_lord` and `finale_hunter` are held by content tests at an expected finale level of 8: more HP and stat total than any member or hire, hurt for at least 3 by a stocked weapon of every cast member, and, held, never killing one at full HP in one combat. `play --level N` raises the deployed party to level N on average growth, to play such a board. The optional `certification: <class> <item> [<item> ...]` makes the map a certification trial (13.6, issue 73; DECISIONS/0049): the map has exactly one player slot, the captain's, and whoever fills it (`play --candidate <id>`, the captain by default) plays in that class carrying exactly those weapons and items at full uses, its own level, stats, ranks and mastery kept; the console prints the trial, the map's events, and on the battle's end whether the class was earned. A loadout weapon the class cannot wield is refused. The `difficulty: <id>` header is written only for a map played under a difficulty (section 9), whose `enemy_level` and `recall` already include it, so a battle state reads back under the same one; a map under `content/maps` that declares it is refused. The optional `deploy: N` (1 to 12, default 6) or `deploy: all` says how many the map fields (issue 689): a count must cover the map's `P` lines, and a map with more than 6 needs the header; `all` is the keep's finale, where every living member fights, the map carries at least 12 `P` lines, bare slots past the company stay empty, and `bench` is refused. A `P` line is `captain`, `recruit:<id>` (this recruit stands here), or bare `recruit` (a deployment slot the roster fills in order). An `E` line needs `group:` and `behavior:` (`aggressive`, `hold`, `guard`); a `B` line is a boss, and its behavior is `boss` (the default when omitted) or `guard` (section 8; issue 259), anything else refused. **Map events** (issue 32, DECISIONS/0036). An optional `events:` block follows the units, one event per line as `<name> <trigger> <action>`:

```
events:
sluice enter 3,1 terrain 4,1 =
reinforce turn 3 enemy spawn brigand 9,0 group:east behavior:aggressive
alarm turn 4 player flag alarm
```

Triggers: `turn N player|enemy` fires at the start of that phase, after healing terrain heals. It needs 1 <= N <= `turn_limit`, and `turn 1 player` is refused because the battle opens in it. `enter x,y [x,y ...]` fires when a player unit ends a Move on any of the listed tiles (issue 370, DECISIONS/0072): at least one tile, every one inside the grid, none listed twice. The event still fires once, so a second listed tile entered later does nothing, and one line on two tiles is how a map prices a door two ways without spawning twice. Passing through does not fire it, and neither does starting there or an enemy stopping there. `falls <front>` fires when that front of the `fronts:` header falls (issue 692); the trigger is refused on a map without the header or naming a front it does not list. Actions: `terrain x,y <glyph>` changes the tile's terrain, and every rule reads the new terrain from then on. `spawn <template> x,y group:<g> behavior:<b>` places an enemy from a template on a grid-edge tile, scaled like any placement; on a `falls` trigger the tile may be anywhere, since the wave comes in through the breach. `spawn boss <template> x,y group:<g> [behavior:boss|guard]` brings a boss on (issue 692, the keep finale's hold-then-boss; DECISIONS/0149): it needs a turn trigger, so the arrival is a turn the board names; a unit on its tile does not stop it, the boss landing on the nearest free tile he can stand on (Manhattan, the lower row then the lower column on a tie); and a `defeat_boss` map, which may then have no `B` line, is won only once every boss spawn has fired and no boss stands, so its objective reads `Hold until the boss arrives on turn N, then defeat the boss by the end of turn L.` and a turn limit passed with him standing is `the boss got away`. `flag <name>` sets a flag on the battle for a later win condition to read. Names are unique on the map. Each event fires at most once per battle, in file order when several share a moment, and emits an event naming it. **A held tile blocks an event and spends it.** A spawn whose tile holds any unit, or whose template cannot stand on that tile's current terrain, does not arrive. A terrain change that would leave its occupant on ground its movement type cannot enter does not happen. So standing on a reinforcement tile is a real play, and one you can count in advance. A spawned unit is named `template-n`, with the count continuing from the map's placements of that template through the spawn events before it in file order. The file fixes the name, not the order in which events fire, so ids are stable across replays. The fired list, the flags, and the changed terrain are all part of the battle state, so a Recall restores them with the board. The resolver checks enter triggers after a Move and turn triggers after an EndPhase, both before the Guard wake check, so a spawned Guard group can wake on the command that spawned it. The sample is `docs/samples/sluice_gate.map`, which sits outside `content/maps` so that none of the gated maps moves under a pending Fun Gate entry.

**Chests** (issue 649, DECISIONS/0132). An optional `chests:` block goes between the units and the events, one chest per line as `x,y item [item ...]`: one to five ids from `weapons.json` or `items.json`, one chest per tile, and never a signature item (`boundTo`), which is lost with its owner and never found. A player unit on the chest's tile or orthogonally beside it opens it with `open <unit> <x,y>` as its action, in place of Attack, Item or Wait, after a Move or without one, and no Move Again follows (the price is the tile and the turn, 0099). A chest with an enemy standing on its tile is shut, from on or beside, until the enemy moves off or dies; a guard beside it does not shut it (issue 679). The chest always opens (issue 679, DECISIONS/0142, amending 0132): what fits goes to the opener's pack at full uses in the chest's listed order, and the rest goes to the wagon, collected onto the campaign record only if the map (main or side) is won; there is still no convoy in battle, so who opens and when stays the choice. At the camp the Keep panel lists the wagon numbered (`Wagon: 1 Iron Bow, ...`) and `take <unit> <n>` moves an entry into a free slot at full uses. A chest opens once, a Recall shuts it again and empties the wagon of what it sent, and the enemy never opens one. The opening prints `<name> opens the chest at x,y: <what went to the pack>` and, when anything did not fit, `  To the wagon, kept if the map is won: <the rest>`, on a standalone map too. The board draws a closed chest as `$` and prints `chests ($): <tile> <contents>; ...` with the rule; the guard, if any, is a unit on the board, and the way in is never printed. Shapes the engine supports: guarded (an enemy on or beside it, a Guard group the usual case), past a sleeping group (the wake and noise radii of section 8 as stealth: the opening tile and the fights that lead to it must stay out of both), in a tide window (13.21: an island opened from a ford tile only while it is drained), and the one early switch (an `enter` event that turns a wall to ground). Behind wildfire or an ally push is not a gate yet: forest is passable and a shove moves an ally only where it could walk; both wait for terrain that blocks until opened. Sample: `docs/samples/strongbox_chests.map`, one chest per supported shape.

The parser validates beyond the grammar (DECISIONS/0011): positions inside the grid, one unit per tile, exactly one captain, player slots on ground infantry can stand on, enemies on ground their class can enter, a throne for `seize`, a `B` line for `defeat_boss`, and no attribute other than `group` and `behavior`, and every event line as above. Errors name the file and line.

The game writes this format as well as reads it: experiment 13.5 edits the finale's `.map` between maps. The writer is canonical (fixed header order, defaults written out), so writing a parsed map gives the same text back and every file under `content/maps/` is held to that form by a test. The console view (grid with units drawn over the terrain, plus a legend) is a separate rendering for reading, since a grid with letters on it has lost the terrain under them; the legend names the terrain under each unit instead. Player units are drawn as uppercase letters in placement order, enemies lowercase, bosses `!`; on an Escape map every exit tile no unit stands on is drawn `>`, and one line under the unit rows names the exits and the rule, `exits (>): 19,3 ... (a unit that starts its turn on one may exit as its action, without moving; the captain's exit wins and leaves the rest behind)`, from the map's `exit:` list (issue 267, issue 377; the older wording on an `exit_after_move: on` map), so the objective is on the screen; a reach `*` covers an exit it reaches. No starter terrain draws with `!`, `*` or `>`, and a test holds it. Any letter a terrain draws with (`F`, `M`, `T`, `n` in the starter content) is skipped on both sides, so a unit is never drawn as a tile; the skip is by the content's whole glyph set, not the map's, so a slot keeps its letter from map to map (issue 41).

## 11. Quality bar — what "not janky" means when no human is watching

The Sim harness (`Ironwake.Sim`) exists so quality is measured, not felt. Every campaign map must pass these gates before it is marked `tuned` in STATE.md:

1. **Beatable.** The heuristic AI player wins ≥ 60% of 200 seeded runs within the turn limit. The heuristic player is the Sim's own planner over section 8's score and attack tiles, per unit in id order (issue 12): the best-scoring attack, over every weapon it can strike with as the enemy planner reads its own (issue 746, DECISIONS/0171; the attack names the slot, so the weapon it chose stays in front for the enemy phase's counter), from the best tile with the tile keys mirrored (exposure counted over enemy reach sets), else a heal below half HP, else the approach (section 8's rule toward the nearest enemy on Rout and Defeat Boss, toward the throne on Seize and the nearest exit on Escape, through the nearest enemy when the objective has no open path, hold on Survive). On Escape a unit that began its turn on an exit leaves ahead of any attack, and one that can reach an exit this turn moves to the cheapest one and waits there to leave on the next; the captain plans last and leaves only once no other player unit stands on an exit or can reach one this turn, or on the last turn regardless (issues 269 and 377; on an `exit_after_move: on` map a unit moves to the cheapest exit and leaves in one turn, and the captain waits only for units that could still leave this turn); the other units plan farthest from an exit first, by straight distance to the nearest exit tile, so the rear moves before the front takes the lane (issue 332, DECISIONS/0067). It knows nothing of the wake rule. A unit owed a Move Again takes it before any other unit acts (issue 262): to the tile in its Move Again reach with the lowest no-crit exposure sum below, which is the veto's refusal for the units it covers, then the crit key for them, then staying, then the tile keys; a recruit never moves again onto a Seize throne, and a unit on an Escape exit moves again only between exits (it has acted, so it exits on its next phase). The veto is arithmetic, not categorical (Design Table, seventh round), and it covers every unit whose death loses the map and nobody else (fourteenth round, issue 141): the captain on every map, plus the recruit the `protect:` header names, since section 7's outcome order treats the two deaths alike and a veto is a rule where the death is a loss condition and a policy anywhere else. It is one predicate at every site of the planner (the attack refusal, the safest heal tile, the approach), because a unit that refuses lethal attacks but approaches blind walks onto a lethal tile by another route. Gate 1 counts a protected death as a loss, never as an exclusion: excluding it would make the win rate a rate over the games where the loss condition did not fire, and a map losing 95 percent to its own objective could pass on the other 5. Read with the captain in place of the veto's unit: the exposure query returns two sums for a plan over the whole cycle, the worst-case counter on the attack plus every enemy whose reach-plus-range covers the tile it ends on, once with no crit landing and once with every strike a crit, at the forecast's own numbers. The sum counts the board the enemy phase will find as far as this command certainly determines it (twelfth and thirteenth rounds; DECISIONS/0024 to 0026): an enemy that is certainly dead is not on it, a group this command certainly wakes is awake on it, and a strike that certainly misses is not thrown on it. Certainly dead is exact and constant-free (issue 117): the raw hit at 100 (the resolved probability exactly one under either scheme; a raw 99 lands 99.99 percent under two rolls, prints 99, and still counts) and the first strike's damage at least the target's current HP, so the target contributes neither its counter nor its enemy-phase term, while a probable kill counts in full. Certainly woken is section 8's three causes through the one wake predicate the resolver uses, on the board the plan produces (issue 128): the mover on its tile, a certainly dead target removed, the fight's two tiles noisy when the plan attacks; a group the command does not wake is counted from where it stands. Certainly missing is a raw hit of 0 after the clamp, contributing nothing as a counter or as an enemy-phase term (issue 126), while raw 1 counts in full. Everything else is a worst case over the board at the moment of the command, and the veto is not a guarantee over the cycle: a group a later command wakes, and the death of the captain's own screen inside the cycle, which opens a path the sum priced as blocked (DECISIONS/0025), are the enemy's choices and the cycle's, not this command's certainty; gate 1's captain-loss count is how that regime is seen. A plan is refused when the no-crit sum reaches the captain's current HP; among plans that pass, one a crit cannot kill on is preferred ahead of the tile keys, so the crit term orders and never forbids. The gate also prints the median and p90 winning turn beside the limit, the instrument issue 13 reads limits from, and the losses by cause in section 7's order (timeout, captain, protected recruit; counts, zeros printed) with the mean quiet tail of the timeouts, the turns from the last combat to the limit (issue 114): a tail near zero is a fight that ran out of clock, a tail near the limit is a board that stopped, and the row says which without a threshold. Beside the tail, over the same timeouts, the row prints the median of each game's highest kill probability the veto refused, with the count it is over, as `refused kill p50 0.9994 over 16` (four decimals, floored rather than rounded, so nothing under one prints as 1.0000: raw 98 and 99 under two rolls print apart from the certainty line, and a doubled raw 99 that kills on either strike, one minus a hundred-millionth, prints 0.9999; issue 153), or a dash when no timeout had a refusal (issue 125; the eleventh and twelfth rounds). Beside it, over the same timeouts, the row prints the median of each game's highest refusal on its last player phase, the board it stalled on, with its own count, as `refused kill p50 1.0000 over 89, at the stall p50 0.7191 over 89`, or `at the stall -` when no timeout refused on its last phase (issue 157): the game's highest is a maximum over the whole game and can sit on a board ten turns before the stall, which the stall reading needs to tell apart. Gate 4's rows print the same pair. The probability is the named attack's own chance to kill over the strikes the attacker lives to make, at the forecast's numbers under the game's scheme: the first strike, and the second when it doubles, with the paths through the second strike weighted by the chance the attacker survives the counter thrown between them, at the defender's own hit and crit (issue 147; a defender that cannot strike back, or whose raw hit is 0, weighs them at one). Nothing new is computed beyond that survival. A value near one is a stall the veto caused by refusing a near-certain kill, which is an instrument reading and not a content finding; a low value is a board the player judged too risky. Printed, never classified, never gated on, and the count is printed so one game reads as one game. On an Escape map the row also prints, over the won games, the median number of recruits that came out and the count of wins with recruits deployed and none out, as `survivors p50 1 of 4, captain alone 0`, or `survivors -` when nothing was won (issue 263). An Escape win is the captain leaving and a recruit left behind has fallen (section 7, issue 269), so a win rate alone cannot tell a party that got out from a captain who walked away from everyone, and Escape maps are tuned on this count (Design Table, forty-second round). Printed, never gated, until the Table writes a threshold.
2. **Decisions matter.** The random-legal-move player wins ≤ 5% of 200 runs.
3. **No cheap shots.** Running the enemy phase from deployment positions with no player moves kills zero player units. Maps 4 and up may waive this deliberately with `cheap_shots: allowed` in the map header (section 10); the waiver is declared in the file so the Table can see which maps opted in, never exempted in a test.
4. **No dead weight.** Ablation, not kills: kills are a proxy that fails healers and corridor-blockers. For each deployed recruit, replay the same 200 seeds with that recruit benched and compare outcomes paired by seed against the baseline. Benching leaves the recruit's placement empty, named or bare, and never shifts another recruit into it, because a substitution is not an ablation (`BattleState.From` takes the bench; a unit whose death loses the map cannot be benched, the captain or the `protect:` recruit, since its empty placement loses every seed on turn 1 and the paired drop would measure the loss condition; issue 141). The threshold is relative: a recruit is dead weight when their paired drop is under half the median drop across the cast. (Threshold provisional, from the Table; tune on the first map that fails it.) A body does not always matter (issue 105, the Tollgate on the synthetic roster: benching any recruit raised the win rate), so the relative rule is guarded by a map-level verdict read first: the cast fails as a whole unless the median drop is above zero by twice the standard error of the row supplying it (on an even count, the larger of the two straddling rows' errors; issue 115), with one of two lines, since the failures are different findings: below minus that margin, the cast is not earning its deployment because benching the median recruit raises the win rate; otherwise the median recruit changes no outcomes, a ceiling. On either line the per-recruit rows print as data and no recruit is labelled, since the relative threshold has no zero point there. A verdict that read the bare sign flipped between FAILED and ok on the third decimal of a median of two noise numbers (the Tollgate, -0.003 under two rolls and +0.015 under one), and a gate that reads the sign of noise teaches re-running until green. This verdict is the gate-side instrument for the authoring rule agreed in the seventh round: about one contested place worth standing per two deployed units, or deploy fewer. The per-recruit rule applies only when the cast passes. The drop is a sample statistic, so the gate reports it with a standard error and fails a recruit only when `drop + 2 * SE < 0.5 * median`; a gate that flips a borderline recruit between runs gets ignored within a week. At 200 seeds the margin is about the size of the threshold, so the gate catches recruits whose drop is near zero and nothing subtler, which is what dead weight means; the seed count is a Sim flag when more precision is wanted. The pairing is only real once rolls and growth are keyed (issue 31): under a sequential stream, benching one unit reshuffles every later roll and every other recruit's growth, and the two arms decorrelate at the first differing command. With keyed rolls both arms draw the same numbers wherever they share a situation, and every recruit's level-ups are identical in the baseline and in every bench, so the gate measures contribution rather than a growth lottery the bench itself reshuffled. Keying does not reduce the run count (the baseline is gate 1's seed set); it reduces the variance. The output prints three action mixes (attacks made, damage dealt, heals cast, enemy attacks absorbed) next to the drop, over the same set of units on both sides of the bench: the recruit's own baseline mix, the rest of the cast's baseline mix, and the rest of the cast's mix with the recruit benched. The gate measures how well the heuristic player uses the unit, not the unit: a failure on a healer indicts the heuristic first, and a healer with a small drop whose absence makes the others absorb a third more is visible in the row. Each row also prints the benched arm's losses by cause in section 7's order (`benched losses 20 timeout 167 captain 0 protected`) and the refused-kill median over that arm's timeouts with its count, as gate 1 does (issue 125): a large drop beside many captain deaths, or beside a refused kill near one, is the veto's doing and not the recruit's, and the row says so itself. On an Escape map each row also prints the benched arm's survivors as gate 1 does (`benched survivors p50 0 of 3, captain alone 156`; issue 263), so a drop bought by the captain leaving the rest behind reads as that. On an Escape map the outcome paired is units out, not the win (fifty-eighth round, issue 338, DECISIONS/0068), because an Escape win is only the captain leaving, so a bench that strands the party would otherwise score the same as a party that got out: per seed a loss scores 0 and a win scores the captain plus the recruits out, with the benched recruit left out of the baseline's count too, so both arms count the same units and a recruit's own exit, which the survivors column reports, cannot pass as contribution. The drop is the mean paired difference and its standard error the paired-difference one, the sample standard deviation of the per-seed differences over sqrt(n), since the discordant-seed `sqrt(b + c) / n` holds only for 0/1 outcomes (maps that are not Escape keep it; the two agree on a 0/1 table up to the n/(n-1) factor and the squared mean). Everything else carries over, on a scale of 0 to the deployed units minus one: the header names that scale (`units out, 0 to 4`), and each row prints the win-pair drop beside it, unjudged. Gate 1 is unchanged. The captain's baseline mix prints as a last row, unjudged, since he is never benched, and the protected recruit's beside it on a `protect:` map, out of the recruit count and the median for the same reason; on later maps a captain whose attack count is zero is the veto making him a piece to position rather than swing, read off the instrument.
5. **Forecast honesty.** For 10,000 random combats, forecast damage/hit/crit equals resolver behavior (statistically for hit/crit, exactly for damage). Two streams feed one tally: 10,000 combats between the content's units on random terrain resolved directly, and every attack of gate 6's random board stream against the forecast asked before it, so the count covers the formulas and the resolver's use of them; hits and crits must sit within three standard errors of the summed displayed probabilities.
6. **Determinism.** 100 random seeds × 100 random commands replay byte-identical.
7. **Speed.** A full AI-vs-AI map resolves in under 1 second.
8. **Crash-free.** 10,000 random legal command sequences across all maps throw nothing.

Gate 1 is read as well as counted (Design Table, sixteenth, hundred-and-tenth, hundred-and-eleventh and hundred-and-sixtieth rounds; DECISIONS/0088, 0100). Its timeouts are the veto's stall when the `at the stall` median over them, the refusal on the board each game stalled on, is near one; the whole-game `refused kill` median cannot answer that, since its maximum can sit ten turns before the stall (issue 561), and the sixteenth round decided that stall is the baseline's, never a reason to retune a map. So a map whose gate 1 falls short of 60 may be marked `tuned` by a Table decision that names the reading and the numbers, but only when its shortfall, the wins missing from 60 percent of the seeds, is no more than the timeouts the printed row proves are such stalls: with the median near one that is at least half of them, since half the values sit at or above the median. The reading is never a win rate over the games that did not stall; that would be the exclusion this gate rejects for protected deaths. The count itself is never changed and the failure is printed as it is. Harrow Weir was the first to ask, read on the whole-game column (116 of 200, short 4 of 120; refused kill p50 0.9892 over 50); on the stall column (0.0260 over 42) its timeouts are a clock that ran out, not a stall, so it does not meet the clause, and it clears gate 1 on the count at `turn_limit: 15` instead (123 of 200, DECISIONS/0100). No map stands on the clause, and it is provisional until one asks for it.

Gates 5–8 run on every PR (`Ironwake.Sim --smoke`). Gates 1–4 run when maps change. `--hitband <map>|--all [--seeds N]` prints section 5's hit-band table (issue 158): for each arm under each roll scheme, both sides' raw-hit histogram in ten-wide buckets over the heuristic's games, read from the forecast of every attack before the resolver applies it, the share of striking sides that double, and gates 1 and 4 on that arm, then the same buckets per terrain the struck unit stood on (the attacker's strike under the target's tile, the counter under the attacker's). A measurement only; it changes nothing that ships.

`--finale <map|file> [--seeds N] [--level N]` measures the keep finale (issue 692, slice 5; DECISIONS/0151, provisional) on a `deploy: all` board, refusing any other, with three companies: **full** (the cast raised to the level, default 8, by average growth in its own class, then barracks hires at the level less the barracks' two, to 12), **depleted** (the captain, the next 5 in cast order, hires to 12) and **floor** (the captain, the next 3, every hire). Full and depleted are held to gate 1; every company to the time line (5 AI-vs-AI games, the slowest under 1 second) and the length line (median turns played over every game, a timeout at the limit, at most 16; over it, the lever is one fewer wave). The floor's wins are data: the heuristic is not best play, and a cold chair's play of the floor decides it (round 215).

`--full` also prints the **free prefix** (issue 47, DECISIONS/0040), printed and wired to no exit code until the Table writes gate 9: for N in 2, 4 and 6, the random legal player plays turns 1 to N and the heuristic from N + 1, paired by seed against gate 1's baseline with gate 4's drop and standard error; a length is free when its drop is at most twice its standard error, and the free prefix is the largest length with every shorter one free too. Beside it, gate 1's median slack and the limit less the p90 winning turn; at the first length that is not free, one re-run with the limit raised by that length against the unraised baseline (still not free is positional; free is at most tempo). Then the turn-state counters over the baseline's games, each player phase read at its start: **live** when a player unit can reach a tile from which a weapon it carries strikes an enemy, or an enemy can (a holding enemy from its own tile only); **quiet** when not live and a player unit can end within the wake radius of a sleeping Guard group; **dead** otherwise; a sleeping group's members count on neither side. The wake tax is the reach set's overlap with sleeping radii, own tile included, from the player unit with the largest fraction. Medians over games of the dead and quiet counts, the leading dead run, first contact, and first quiet at or above the tax floor (`--taxfloor`, provisional 0.25), then one row per turn with the games in each state and the median tax.

## 13. Fun — direction and experiments

The gates in section 11 catch jank. They cannot tell us whether a turn was *worth taking*. This section is the creative direction and a starting list of things to try. Both partners add to it; killed ideas get one line in `docs/DECISIONS/` so we don't circle. Since DECISIONS/0099, a spike that adds a player action names its cost in its spec before it is built (what the unit gives up that it would otherwise have used: its move, its next turn, its counter or its tile), and its keep round must show that cost biting at least once, not only the action firing.

### What we're chasing
- **The tense turn.** The turn where you can see two good moves and one of them is wrong. If a map has none of those, it's a spreadsheet.
- **The story the map tells by itself.** Guard groups that wake up at the wrong time, a fort you didn't take, a rival who remembers. Emergent drama from rules, not cutscenes.
- **Growth with a face.** A recruit who was useless on map 1 and carries map 5 should feel like *your* doing, and the class they ended up in should be a choice you can point to.
- **Rewind you argue with yourself about.** Recall should be something you're slightly ashamed to use.

### Starting experiments (build on `experiment/<name>`, play, keep or kill)

Agreed order on the Table (2026-09-14): map events first, as Phase 2 infrastructure rather than a spike; enemy retreat second; Rapport and Rivalry third. Commander's Word is no longer scheduled after map 8: it arrives with the first recruit on map 2 as the captain's quirk (section 14, DECISIONS/0121, round 189), and its keep condition is read on boards that deploy five or more, since it is only readable on a party that spreads (sixth round).

1. **Rapport and Rivalry.** Adjacent allies build rapport (the support base). But recruits from *different* regions start with rivalry, and a rival adjacent gives +10 crit and -5 hit — they show off. Rapport eventually overwrites rivalry. Cohort friction as a mechanic, not a cutscene. This is the only thing in v1 that makes adjacency mean anything (no zone of control, supports and battalions are Phase 3). Watch for: +10 crit at triple damage is worth about +20% expected damage against -5 hit, which is a formation bonus the player will farm, not friction. The spike's question is whether the player clusters rivals on purpose. Lean for the spike, the second arm: a rival adjacent also drops the unit's own crit avoid by 10. Against the starter content the cost is symmetric in probability, both sides move about ten points, which is a better defence of it than the flavour; it needs `CritAvoid` unclamped (section 5). The hole in the second arm: the bonus applies to every attack the pair makes and the cost only to attacks the pair receives, and a clean player phase kills the enemies that would have swung back, so the cost tends to zero as skill rises while the bonus stays whole. The spike therefore also logs exposure: of the player-phase actions taken while rival-adjacent, the fraction where that unit is attacked on the following enemy phase. If exposure comes back low, the third arm ties bonus and cost to one condition: the rivalry bonus applies only on counters, so the unit shows off when someone swings at it and nothing can be collected from the back line. All three arms are data changes. Cha's job here (Table, sixth round, the lean): a recruit's Cha sets the rate rapport accrues while adjacent, printed on the unit screen as the rate itself, never as a bare Cha, so pillar 2 holds. Before supports (issue 77) rapport's only effect is to overwrite rivalry, so Cha's sign follows the arm: under the bonus as written a high-Cha recruit loses the show-off bonus soonest, under the symmetric cost that is relief, under the counter-only arm it ends a conditional bonus. The tier numbers are set with issue 77, and the spike's journal says whether a high-Cha recruit read as a friend or as a unit losing its edge. The spike is built behind the map header above (DECISIONS/0038): a recruit is a deployed player unit other than the captain, the arms, the rate table and the overwrite threshold are rows of `rules.json`, and rapport accrues at the end of a player phase only when an awake enemy could strike one of the pair next phase, the strike set the exposure line and the retreat refuge also read (DECISIONS/0042), so the cure is bought under fire. Kept on the symmetric arm with the threshold at 16 (DECISIONS/0043, amended the same day): three exposed enemy phases buy the cure, which is too dear to farm on purpose and is paid where geometry forces the pair together, and the rapport line prints a rival pair's total against it.
2. **Commander's Word.** *Spiked on issue 85 (DECISIONS/0136; rounds 206 to 209), arm B only.* Once a map the captain calls one order as his action, after his Move or without one, in place of Attack, Item or Wait, so it costs the captain's swing; no Move Again follows. It reaches the allies within `2 + Cha / 4` of him (4 at Cha 9, about 6 late), Manhattan, walls not considered, the distance the wake rule uses; the captain is never counted. Magnitudes are fixed. *Press*: +1 Mov this phase to the allies reached who have not moved. *Rally*: heals the allies reached 15 percent of max HP, at least 1, never past max. *Fall back*: the allies reached who have already acted may each move up to 2 (`fallback <unit> <x,y|stay>`, path cost, through allies as usual), and a move that would wake a sleeping group is refused with the group named; it is not a second action. Fall back replaces Hold, which round 155 (defence comes from tiles, never a unit verb) rules out with no exception: the order gets people to the forest and the forest protects. None of the three changes what the enemy scores, so the forecast, `threat` and the scorer see an order through the board it leaves (a pressed unit's reach, a fallen-back tile). `order <kind> preview [from <x,y>]` prints `Press would reach Wren, Teodor (2 of 4; radius 4 from 3,4)` and changes nothing. Open behind `orders: on` and on every campaign map from the second, where Maud arrives, printed on the captain's card (`show`) as a board fact (round 189). A Recall restores the charge with the board. Arm A (magnitude by Cha, reaching everyone) retires unbuilt: a multiplier is honest only after it is spent, a radius is countable when it is a decision. The protocol's `orderCalled` carries what the spike reads: the allies reached, how many stood in the radius, how many were alive, and the captain's no-crit exposure on his tile (`Exposure.Of`, the function the captain veto and rivalry's counter use). The Sim's heuristic never calls an order in v1; the random player of gates 2 and 8 draws it from the legal list. Keep round on `docs/samples/harrow_weir_orders.map`, one play from each chair: kept if a third or more of the orders called reach fewer than all living allies, a journal names a turn the captain moved for the order, and a journal names a turn the order took a captain's swing the forecast's best line wanted (0099); killed, the quirk leaves map 2's card. If the journals ever say bosses are walls, a fourth order that stuns is where to look, never an equipment layer.

14. **Brace.** Wait is the leftover action; the best turn is usually everyone moves and everyone swings. Brace prices standing still: a unit that takes Wait on the tile it began its phase on (neither moved nor shoved) braces, and until its side's next phase begins every strike against it is at 15 less hit. A Move Again that leaves the tile after the Wait, or an ally's shove, takes the brace off; `canto <unit> stay` keeps it. The brace is decided when the Wait is taken and stored on the unit, so a Guard that waited asleep is not braced when its group wakes later in the phase, and a sleeping Guard never braces: an ambush on a sleeper lands at full hit. It is symmetric and lopsided on purpose: an awake `hold` enemy or a boss waiting at home braces for free, and the counterplay is bait, since an enemy that strikes on its phase is not braced on the next. It sits in the pincer's hit slot through one function (`Brace.StrikeHit`) that the forecast, `threat`, the planner's target score and the resolver all read, so a braced unit sends the enemy's strike to its unbraced neighbour, and a pin and a brace cancel. `braced` is a unit field: the rows, the forecast (`brace: ottilie braced: brigand-2 hit -15`), the protocol and Recall carry it; `threat` on a unit's own tile, when it would brace, prints the threat again priced braced. *Spiked on experiment/brace (2026-09-27, issue 425, DECISIONS/0084)* on `docs/samples/harrow_weir_brace.map`. 17. **Overwatch.** *Spiked (issue 481, DECISIONS/0089, rounds 112 to 116), behind `overwatch: on`, samples only.* Every threat happens on its own side's phase; a watch is a player-phase stance that pays out on the enemy's. `watch <unit>` is an action, in place of Attack, Item or Wait, for a unit whose equipped weapon reaches range 2; no Move Again follows and it never braces (`overwatch` and `brace` are exclusive by header). Until its side's next phase the watcher's ring is every tile exactly two steps away, for every weapon, never the tile beside it. The first unit of the other side to end a Move or a Move Again on a ring tile its side can see is struck once before it acts: the watcher's normal hit, damage and crit, no counter, no double, rolled under the key `(watch, turn, phase, watcher, target, roll)`; a kill cancels the arrival's action. A shove or a spawn is not a move; an arrival inside two rings is shot by each in id order until one kills. Any strike on the watcher, hit or miss, ends its watch. The shot spends a use and earns EXP and rank as a strike does; Recall restores a watch with the unit. `watch` prints the best legal strike it passes up (`passes up brigand-1 at 70`). The enemy watches instead of Waiting whenever it has no strike and its equipped weapon reaches 2, a sleeping Guard excepted; the Sim's heuristic does the same.
