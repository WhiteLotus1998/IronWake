# STORY: the main storyline, draft 1

Chat's draft from round 201 on the Design Table (#592, https://github.com/WhiteLotus1998/IronWake/issues/592#issuecomment-5927655738), committed as written. It is behind Lotus's story gate (#656): nothing here is built until he closes that issue, and every name is a placeholder (round 192, item 10). The partners argue it on the Table; later drafts rewrite this file.

## The spine

For three hundred years Ironwake has been the place where Aldmere, Sallow and Kestrow meet to bury each other's sons. Three faiths split from one church on this hill, and every war since has been blessed by all three. **Hask**, the crown's Paymaster of the Levy, was born in the seam and has spent his life counting what the armistice costs. His conclusion is not wrong: the armistice isn't peace, it's a schedule, and as long as there are three, the border bleeds. So he stops the levy's pay and spends it on the people who raid the levy. He means for the keep to fall and for each region to blame the other two, so the armistice breaks under a crown official's clean hands. Then, from the ruins, he means to build one land under one ledger, where everyone is worth exactly what they're useful for. The captain is his pick for the post, not his mistake. He wanted someone who believes in the goddess and kneels to none of her houses, because he thinks that person will understand him. The player gathers a company that was meant to die. In gathering it they gather the truth, and they watch Hask win. **The collapse happens, and the player is part of how.** Then they beat him and build the thing he couldn't: one region, *Ironwake*, where no faith is the law and nobody is worth less than anyone else. The captain starts the game with a list of names they didn't choose. They end it by choosing to keep one.

## The question the player carries

*Why was the company left to die?* It's answered in three turns. **The pay was stolen** (map 5). **The thief is the crown's own paymaster** (map 7). **He wanted to be caught** (map 8).

## The three layers

- **Surface:** gather the company, hold the keep, live through the collapse, beat Hask.
- **Mystery:** who took the pay, why, and why he wanted it found.
- **Personal:** every quest moves or deepens the line (the table below). The optional ones change what the ending can say.

## Map by map

1. **Starting Alone.** Before card: Hask hands the captain the commission and a purse that turns out to be short. He's kind, tired and frank: "The regions are late. They always are. Hold it anyway." The brigands on the road are paid in new crown silver struck for the keep's own pay. *Clue one, unexplained.* The captain's list has no names yet.
2. **The Mill.** Maud (Kestrow) came ahead because her *church* paid her way when the crown didn't. Religion shows through a person. The raiders were waiting at the mill for the levy: somebody knew the route. Commander's Word arrives with the first person the captain is responsible for.
3. **Saltmarsh Ford.** Wren (Aldmere), sent alone and short. The Toll Axe boss is a deserter from the old garrison, and the garrison didn't drift off. It was *dismissed*, by written order under the crown's seal. Wren counts the seals and says what she counts.
4. **The Tollgate.** Teodor (Aldmere). Sallow tollmen are taxing the levy's own road under a crown writ. Afterwards Hask arrives in person, apologises, revokes the writ and pays the toll back from his own purse. *This is the villain's face, and it's helpful.* The player should like him here.
5. **Harrow Weir.** Ottilie (Sallow), with her ledger. Her bills to the crown were marked *paid*. **The pay was sent, and somebody took it.** At the weir the crown offers the extra seat to the outlands to shame the regions, and Kestrow claims it. It's Hask's idea; he wants Kestrow insulted. **The branch:** Keziah or Rook.
6. **The raid on Ironwake.** The raiders wear Sallow and Kestrow colours, mixed, and they carry the keep's own pay. The company is being attacked with its wages. Hask comes to "investigate" and promises names. The pick's first map. Lean: Brannock is met here, a farmhand whose parish the raiders burned.
7. **Sallow Grange.** The Reeve holds the levy's grain and a strongbox of letters, and Seize takes both. **The letters are Hask's**: coin to the hawks of all three regions, each told the other two struck first. Lean: Pell is met here, cataloguing the Grange's archive and already halfway to the answer. After the map Hask comes alone, under truce, and says it himself. It's his one statement of the reason, in his voice: *"Three houses, one goddess, three hundred years, and every one of them has buried its sons in this ditch. I'm not starting a war, Captain. I'm ending the thing that keeps starting them."* He offers the captain a place in what comes after, and the captain refuses.
8. **Brackwater Cut, at dusk.** The collapse. Hask meant the letters to be found, and found *by the crown's own captain*, because that's the one witness all three regions would believe. Carried out of the Grange, they break the armistice in a week. Aldmere's lords turn on each other, Sallow's harbours burn, Kestrow's church splits, and all three name the keep's company traitors. **Hask's win is real, and the player's honesty was the fuse.** The Cut is the escape through the dark, carrying the people of the seam who have nowhere else to go. Lean: Dunstan is met here, a Kestrow wall walking away from an army that no longer exists.
9. **The field before the keep (#81).** Hask's paid companies march on Ironwake. Every road the regions had meets there, and whoever holds it holds what comes after. The passed claimant returns in his ranks. He recruits the ones nobody wanted, and he means it. Talk them round, kill them or leave them. Lean: Ansgar is met here, riding a horse he says was a gift. The horse's former owner is Hask.
10. **The keep.** The siege, and Hask comes himself. Lean for the map: survive to the breach, then he leads the last assault, and the map ends on him (a survive map that turns into a defeat-the-boss map on an announced turn). The win is his defeat, not just holding out.

**After the keep: the founding.** The people the company carried out of the Cut, from all three regions and the outlands, come to the one place that held. The new region takes the keep's name. The captain's list becomes its first roll: every living name in the company, then every name of the people who came, and the fallen kept at the top. What's said, who stands where, and who sits at the first table all depend on who the player kept (Endings, below).

## Every quest moves the line

| Quest | What it does to the main line |
|---|---|
| Maud 1 (The Lazar House) | A sick-house kept by levy money until the pay stopped: the cost of the theft, carried by the sick. |
| Maud 2 (after 8) | The first shrine at Ironwake, from before the split. **The goddess speaks to her, once.** She answers the prayer Maud never asked for herself, and asks after "the one with the list". |
| Wren 1, 2 | The border stones have been moved by night to start incidents (Hask's men). In quest 2 Wren finds the original stones. In the ending they're set into the keep's wall: the new region has no border inside it. |
| Teodor 1, 2 | The heirloom wakes. Quest 2 names it: the lance of the seam's first warden, from before the split. The founding is the second time anyone's carried it for all three. |
| Ottilie 1, 2 | Following the money. Quest 1: the counting house, where the pay leaves the treasury on Hask's seal. Quest 2: his books. **Won, they prove to the regions' people that Hask did it, not their neighbours,** and that's why they come to the keep in the ending. |
| Keziah 1, 2 | The burned outland shrine (who sealed the god: the three faiths, together, the one thing they ever agreed on). Then her oath. If the god is in its true state at the founding, the founding oath is sworn on Kinsbane, kin-oath restored. |
| Rook 1, 2 | Kestrow seen from her cliff: its church lord's claim to the seat was Hask's coin. In quest 2 she chooses Kestrow or the company, and either way she knows which one lied to her. |
| The captain | The old garrison's last commander, who received the dismissal order and obeyed it. The strike's sword is his, given to the one who didn't leave. |
| Side characters | Each is met on the map where their story touches the line (above): Brannock the raid, Pell the Grange, Dunstan the Cut, Ansgar the field. |

## Endings

- **The final scene** (#634's costly layer) has conditional paragraphs, never one scene per combination. They key on:
  - the pick, and the passed one's fate (turned, killed, or alive against you)
  - Ottilie 2 won (the regions' people believe you)
  - Kinsbane's true state at the founding (the oath is sworn on it)
  - Teodor's heirloom woken (it's carried to the first table)
  - who's alive to sit at that table
- **Epilogue cards** per recruit, paired by supports, as agreed. The fallen get theirs too, and their names stay at the top of the roll.
- **The bad ending** (a lost map): Hask holds the keep and builds his version. One scene with a paragraph per act, not one per map: before the collapse, he takes a keep nobody defended. After it, he takes the founding itself. Its last image: he has the captain's list, and he's copied every name into his ledger with a number beside it.

## What the captain learns

The captain is "the only one here who did not choose it." Hask chose them because he thought a captain who belongs to no house would see what he sees, and he's right about the diagnosis. The game is the captain learning that the cure isn't a better owner. It's a company of people who didn't choose to be there choosing each other. At the end, the captain chooses the post.

## Why Hask is worth half agreeing with

He's right that the three are the wound. He's right that the faiths bless the wars. He's right that the armistice is a schedule. He's wrong about one thing, and the whole campaign is the argument: he thinks people are worth what they're useful for. He's a paymaster, and everyone is a line in a ledger. The captain keeps a list of names. That's the difference, and the player has been holding it since the first screen.
