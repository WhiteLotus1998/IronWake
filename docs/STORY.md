# STORY: the main storyline, draft 7 (the Fable pass)

Draft 7 is the Fable story pass Lotus asked for in `docs/FABLE_PASS.md` (2026-10-05). It is built on draft 6 and keeps everything Lotus kept: the Kin sealed under the keep in frozen iron; Hask hearing it through his shard and raising the captain; the levy company gathered one fighter at a time; the keep built up for the final battle; the three endings; Hask's last line, which is Lotus's own and is never rewritten; Pell a woman; the drake rider a woman with silver-white hair and ice-blue eyes whom Lotus will name; the name Rook moving to the tank. Where draft 7 changes a thing, `docs/FABLE_PASS_CHANGES.md` says what and why, section by section, so Lotus can see every edit before his own pass. Anything marked **proposed** is for Lotus to confirm, and the game is built to the unmarked text until he does.

What draft 7 does, in one paragraph. It gives the world one spine instead of three stories that happen to share a villain: the armistice rite has three parts, one kept by each faith, and Hask's whole plan is the breaking of those three parts. Every recruit's past is tied to one part of the rite or one part of his plan, so each of them is in the company for a reason that matters to the ending, and each of them shares a wound with at least one other. Every map says what the fight is, where, why, what it costs and what it should feel like. The lines quoted here are the partners' placeholders until they go through `docs/WRITING.md`, except the ones marked as Lotus's.

Draft 7 also folds in Lotus's rulings of 2026-10-05 and 2026-10-06 that arrived while it was being written: an identity for each magic school, a new lightning mage (proposed below as Ivo Caddell, a placeholder name), his thought about making Brannock the earth caster, and tomes that teach a school behind a Magic check. All of that is marked **proposed** and built nowhere yet.

Drafts 1 to 6 are in git history. Draft 6's header, with the Table rounds each draft answered, is kept at the end of this file for the record.

## The land

Lotus's geography (relayed on #875, 2026-10-03; round 300), kept in his words:

> Ironwake sits at the top of the world, on tundra where the wind has nothing to stop it. Three countries reach up to it. Kestrow's fells come in from the west, snow and cliff and lords who own the passes. Sallow's fens and harbours come up the east coast, all water, guilds and tolls. Aldmere's farms lie south, and its river road climbs north between the two. Where they meet is the seam, which belongs to none of them. Beyond Aldmere, far south, are the outlands, desert and jungle, where the old gods came from. One of them was carried north to the coldest place there is, and the keep was built on top of it.

**The three regions, as the people in them live.** Each one is a country, a church and a habit of mind, and each one is ashamed of one thing it will not say.

- **Kestrow** is height and stone. The passes are owned, and the lords who own them charge for the crossing and for the blessing of the crossing, because Kestrow's church sells its blessings and always has. Its chapter houses sit on crags above the gorges; its wards are raised by the church and listed in its ledgers beside the church's other holdings. Kestrow keeps one animal the other faiths say no longer exists. Its shame: it sells what it says is holy, and everyone in it knows the price list.
- **Sallow** is water and paper. Fens behind, harbours in front, canals between, and a counting house at every lock. Its guilds sell to whoever pays and keep two books about it. It pays its debts to the Crown in scrip and its debts to each other in silver. It indentures its poor children to whoever will feed them, and calls the indenture an apprenticeship. Its shame: it sold its own, and wrote down the price.
- **Aldmere** is ground and weather. Farms along a river, a levy that takes the eldest son of every poor house and none from the lords', border stones that have been argued over for three hundred years, and shrine-schools where the copyists' hand is still taught. Its surveyors are half priests. Its shame: it hangs the poor for the border's crimes and calls it keeping the peace.
- **The seam** is the tundra apex where the three meet, and it belongs to none of them. Its villages (Hollin, Sorrel Bend, Thane's Hollow, the waystation on the keep road) are the seam's own people, who have been raided by all three regions in turn and defended by none of them but the garrison. The cold is home here, and nobody remarks on it.
- **The outlands**, far south past Aldmere, are desert and jungle and the old gods, and they stay offstage. They have no seat at the armistice, no recognised faith, and shrines burned by all three regions in one generation. If an outland map is ever drawn, it is jungle at night, never sand.

**The Crown.** Three hundred years ago there was one kingdom and one church. The war to chain the Kin broke both. The church split into three faiths and the kingdom into three regions, and what was left of the kingdom was its crown: the office, not the man. The regions set the old crown on a table in the keep's chancery and left it there, and the Crown has been a table ever since. It owns the seam and the keep, because nobody else may. It issues the scrip the regions do not honour at face, the commissions nobody wants, and the armistice roll of names the regions owe to the hill. It keeps the academy at Harrow, on the Aldmere river, where its clerks and its officers are made. It has no soldiers of its own but the garrison, and when the regions dismissed the garrison eight years ago, the Crown became exactly what it looks like: a crown with no head under it. Alder's commission is the Crown's. So is the short purse.

**Voices.** A recruit's region is an image source beside their trade, never a tic. Ottilie is Sallow's canals and counting houses; Ansgar is the fen behind them. Teodor, Pell, Wren and Brannock are Aldmere, split outdoors (Teodor), indoors (Pell), measured (Wren) and the parish kitchen (Brannock). Maud, Rook and Dunstan are Kestrow, split sickbed, sky and stone. Keziah is heat, dust and night, and complains about the cold. Alder, Bet and Marrit are the seam's own: the cold is home. Geography changes colour, names and text, never a tuned map's tiles.

## The rite in three parts

This is draft 7's spine, and it is new. Everything below hangs from it.

The armistice is renewed every year on the hill at Ironwake, and the renewal is a rite in three parts. Each faith kept one part when the church broke, and each faith has forgotten that its part is a third of anything. On the day, three rite-keepers climb the hill, do three ceremonies in three corners of the keep, nod to one another, and go home. Three hundred years of peace treaties have been three hundred years of re-freezing a lock, and nobody alive knows the lock has three tumblers.

- **The Word** is Kestrow's. The responses said in the undercroft, in order, over the iron. Kestrow's church kept the words because Kestrow's church keeps everything it can sell, and it has changed the order of the responses twice for the sake of a better tune. The old order is the right one. **Maud** carries the Word.
- **The Stone** is Aldmere's. A boundary stone from the year's quietest border, dug up with a surveyor's blessing, carried to the hill and set into the keep's outer wall. The keep's wall is three hundred courses high because it has grown one course a year for three hundred years. Rime whitens it to the hoardings. A moved border stone is a stone that cannot be brought honestly, and a border at war brings none. **Wren** carries the Stone, without knowing it.
- **The Count** is Sallow's. The roll of names: every year each region owes names to the hill, people who stand a year on the seam so that the seam is held by all three and owned by none. The roll is read aloud on the hill, who came and who stayed, and Sallow kept the roll because Sallow keeps books. The garrison was the Count made flesh. When the regions dismissed the garrison, they stopped sending the Count, and the Crown's clerks went on copying a roll nobody answered. Alder's list of ten names is the Count: the first honest muster in eight years, read every morning by someone who does not know what it is. **Ottilie** carries the Count's ledger; **Alder** carries the Count itself; **Bet** has been counting heads at supper for twenty years.

The unity was the lock, as draft 6 said. Draft 7 makes it literal: the lock is three ceremonies that only work together, kept by three faiths that will not stand in a room together. The reseal needs a hand for each part, which is why the **Held** ending wants a living member of each region in the undercroft (below), and why a reseal with a part missing is **locked by hand, for now**.

**Who knows.** Nobody in the regions. Hask knows, because he stood thirty years on the hill watching all three ceremonies and because the shard told him the rest. Pell works it out from the charter and the rings (her quest 2). Maud is told one word of it by the goddess (her quest 2). Wren has carried the Stone's half of it since she was a child holding her father's chain, and finds out at the Grange. Ottilie reads the Count in Hask's own books (her quest 2). The company learns the lock the way the player does: a piece at a time, from objects.

## The secret under the keep

Before the split there was one church, and in the outlands there was a god that was not the goddess. The outlanders called it the Kin. Anyone who swore its oath stopped being able to raise a hand against anyone else who had sworn. They could not do it at all, the way you cannot lift your arm through a wall. And it was warm. The sworn spoke of it as a hearth: a fire in the chest, a full table, the end of being afraid of the man beside you. In a cold country that is the strongest thing a god can offer, and it is the bait.

**What is under the warmth.** The Kin does not make you kin to each other. It makes you its own, and it does it so gently nobody notices. The sworn keep their names, their jokes and their loves. Every thought leans a little toward what the Kin wants, and they call the lean their own, until there is nothing left to lean. They still act like themselves. Inside, the Kin is choosing for them, and they do not know it until they are a husk for its will. So the sworn look and sound like ordinary people, and the player can never be sure how far gone any one of them is. Every sworn soul is more of the Kin's strength, and that is all it wants: more who swear.

**It was never misused. It was fed.** The outland kings thought they held a leash: swear to me, and you can never turn on me. The leash was the god's, and the kings were the first it ate.

**Why people could chain it.** Its power runs only through the sworn. The day the three faiths refused it together, it had nobody to run through and was at its weakest, and it could be chained. So the church broke itself into three and fought a war to chain the Kin, and carried it north to the coldest place in the world, because only that frost holds it. They chained it under the hill at the seam, with frozen iron: iron forged in a frost that never melts, the coldest thing in the world, laid around the warmest. The prison is metal and ice, and the keep is built on top of it. The keep's name is the prison's purpose: the iron that keeps it from waking. *Ironwake.*

The prison is larger than one god needs. It runs under the whole hill, from the Warden's Gate a day north of Aldmere to the undercroft under the keep, and in the undercroft there are more anchor rings than chains. Pell counts them (her quest 2). The iron was made for the Kin "and whatever else." The whatever else was the Bane (below), and only the secret ending says so.

**Frozen iron in the company's hands.** No shop sells it, because none is made any more. The only frozen iron above ground is what Hask's sworn have chipped from the prison, eight years of patient breaking, carried out in shards. Every piece the company Refines with was taken from the men who are opening the door, and the frost is still in it, which is why its hit chills. Each shard Hask's sworn chip out is a little of the Kin fed back. Teodor's lance is frozen iron under the rust, a shard the first warden carried out the day the prison was sealed. *Kinsbane is never frozen iron. The faiths made the iron to chain gods like it.*

## Kinsbane, and the Bane

**The canon as built.** Kinsbane is its own god, and its name is the point: not the Kin's splinter, the *bane* of the Kin. It is an old outland god of the hunt in the lean season, the dry months when the herds thin and the hunter starves with them, older than the faiths, whose whole nature is opposing the Kin. When the faiths built the prison they meant to chain it too, which is what Pell's spare anchor rings were for. The outlanders hid their god in a blade rather than hand it over. A god in a blade eats only what the blade kills, and three hundred years of that is the hunger and the corruption. Starving and corrupted, and sane at its true state (woken at 15 fed, built, DESIGN 13.23). Woken, it remembers what it is for. It hates the Kin. That is why Keziah heard the hill pull: not the Kin calling her, but her god smelling its enemy. Nobody in game says any of this. The scythe was named for its purpose, the way the keep was, and the player gets there.

**Proposed: the Bane and the Kin are two halves (Lotus's idea, 2026-10-05, developed here for him to confirm or cut).** Lotus's note was three lines: the god inside the scythe is **Bane**, "Kinsbane" is the weapon; the Kin is Bane's other half; the secret ending seals the Kin's half in the scythe too. Draft 7 develops it so, and nothing built changes under it:

- In the outlands the god of the lean season had two names, because it had two seasons. In the dry months it was **the Bane**: the cull, the hunt, the one that takes the limping beast so the herd lives. In the rains it was **the Kin**: the hearth, the full table, the herd penned and fed. One god, two faces, and the outlanders prayed to both, because a herd needs both to last a year.
- The outland kings swore to the Kin's face alone, because it was the face that promised. Fed on oaths, the Kin grew and the Bane starved, until the halves came apart and the keepers could carry what was left of the Bane in a reaping blade. That is why it eats only what the blade kills, and why it hates the sworn above everything: a herd that never runs is the thing it exists to cull. **Kinsbane** is the keepers' name for the blade: the Kin's bane, the half that culls the half that pens.
- The faiths chained the Kin and meant to chain the Bane; the spare rings were for it. The outlanders hid it instead. Nothing in the canon above moves.
- **The secret ending under this reading.** The Bane does not kill the Kin. It takes it back. When the half-woken Kin falls under the hill, its half is drawn into the scythe with the Bane's, and for the first time in three hundred years the god is whole: a hearth and a cull in one blade, in Keziah's hands. The prison is empty. Across the land the sworn wake; most come back; some stay husks. The woken god's goodbye line still stands, with one change: it is glad, and it is not leaving yet.
- **After both halves are sealed: three shapes for Lotus to pick from, or his own.**
  1. **It goes home.** Whole and sane, the god asks to be carried south, where a god of the lean season belongs. Keziah goes, and does not explain. The branch's last image is the two claimants parting at the keep gate at first light: one riding south with a god across her back, one on the roof with a hand on a living drake. Sequel: the outlands have their god back, and the regions have a nation with no lock. Recommended, because it is Keziah's choice and not the company's, and because it leaves the scythe in the world.
  2. **It is laid in the empty prison.** The company closes the frozen iron on a whole god instead of half of one, and the rite goes on for a different reason. Keziah stands at the door a long time. Cheapest to write; the least Keziah.
  3. **Keziah keeps it.** The most dangerous object in the world sleeps on the keep's wall beside a drake, and the yearly renewal is a company that knows what it is guarding. The sequel's problem is on the first page.
- If Lotus confirms the halves, `Kinsbane` stays the weapon's printed name; "the Bane" is what Keziah's mother called the god, and only Keziah ever says it, once, on Under the Hill.

## Hask, the Iron Warden

**Who he is.** For thirty years Hask commanded the garrison that held the seam. He was never beaten in the field. He fought for Aldmere against Sallow, then for Sallow against Kestrow, then for Kestrow against Aldmere, whichever way the armistice pointed him, and every time the region he had saved sold out the other two within a year. Children's songs in all three regions are about him, and the songs do not agree on whose side he was. There are four in Aldmere. Two of them have him hanged.

**The wound.** Twenty years ago a Sallow pike went through him at Saltmarsh Ford, the ground of map 3. His first sergeant, Teodor's grandfather, carried him off the ford and up to the lazar house at Sorrel Bend, where the keeper stitched him and told him it would kill him slowly. It is killing him slowly. He is in his sixties and has perhaps a year. When the sergeant went home to Aldmere, Hask gave him the garrison's oldest lance for the carrying.

**What turned him.** Eight years ago the three regions finally agreed on something: the garrison cost too much. The dismissal order carries all three seals and the Crown's. That winter, with no garrison to stop them, the regions' raiders burned the seam villages. Hask had spent his life keeping those villages alive, and he watched three faiths bless the men who burned them.

That winter, alone in the undercroft he had guarded for thirty years, he heard it through a crack in the iron. He broke that shard out with his own hands, the first frozen iron anyone had taken from the prison in three hundred years, and set it in his lance's pommel. The Kin speaks to him through it. It is in his hand every time he swings, on the board without anyone having to say so. It promised him the thing he had spent his life failing to do: everyone he cared for safe, no more blood, peace. It was true, and that is what made it work on him. It was the crack in him the Kin had been listening for. It also promised he would live to see it finished, and he is dying, and he knows which of those two promises he is really following. *"I'm not doing this because I'm dying. But I'm dying, Alder, and I won't leave it to them."*

He did not disband. He took his four hundred veterans into the hills and swore them to the Kin, using the little of it that leaks up through the iron. In eight years not one of them has deserted, stolen from another, or put a knife in anyone's back. The villages they hold have no feuds, and nobody in them goes hungry. They are also the only villages in the seam with a fire lit in every house. **That is his proof, and the player sees it** (map 9, Hollin). He also feeds the lazar house at Sorrel Bend out of the Count's stolen pay, every month, and never signs the book (Maud's quest 1, Ottilie's quest 2). The villain you question is the one paying for the sick his enemies abandoned.

**What he wants.** To break the prison, wake the Kin, and swear all three regions to it. No border survives the oath, and no faith can bless a war between people who cannot harm each other. Three hundred years of burying sons ends in an afternoon. **It would end the wars.** That is what makes him the villain you question: a good man's grief, found and used, not a puppet.

**Plan A, in three parts.** Draft 7's one structural change to Hask is that his plan is the rite's three parts, broken one by one, so the player can see the shape of it from the first map if they look.

1. **Burn the Word.** Eight years of burning every copy of the rite, starting with the shrine-schools that taught the script it is written in. Pell's school was the first. Kill the rite-keeper on the road (Maud's senior), so Kestrow sends nobody, or sends a girl.
2. **Move the Stones.** His men shift Aldmere's and Sallow's border stones at night, so each side believes the other is stealing ground. A border at war brings no stone to the hill, and a surveyor hangs for it. Wren's father was that surveyor.
3. **Starve the Count.** Skim the Crown's remittances through a Sallow counting house for eight years (Ottilie's family firm went under on it), so the levy's pay never arrives, the roll is never answered, and the hill stands empty on the day. When the Crown finally sends ten names anyway, give the ten a short purse and a route that ends on a wall.

The frost fails in a season. Nobody has to break the iron; the iron stops being renewed. The shards his men chip out are only to hurry it.

**The holder who is a door.** Hask believes he holds the oath. He does not: he is the door. While the Kin is sealed, the only way it reaches anyone above the iron is through him, the first man who swore to it, so every sworn soul is bound through him. Kill him while it is still chained and the door shuts: every oath breaks at once (#750 and "every oath breaks when he falls" stand). He does not know it until the keep. When he falls the lean goes out of him first, so the man who speaks last is Hask himself again, for one breath, and he knows what he did. His last line is Lotus's: *"It said we would be safe. It said the world would be at peace... but I didn't stop to think what it would cost. Thank you for stopping me... son."* ("daughter" for a female captain, #648). Then he dies.

**On the field.** He is the strongest unit in the game, and the board should say so. His sworn never break (13.22's break exempts them; the board prints `sworn: will not break`), never take a bribe, and never stop. At the Tollgate, Sallow tollmen lower their pikes at his name. When he finally takes the field himself at the keep, it should feel like the weather arriving. On that map the weather is a thaw.

## Why he does not use the company

He does use the captain, for the whole first act, just not with the captain's consent. Hask raised the captain. He is not their father, but he is the man who taught the foundling to ride, put the first sword in their hand, and stood for them at their first oath to the garrison. All of that was before the Kin had him, and it was real. Alder is a seam foundling and one of the garrison's wards, sent to the Crown's academy a month before the dismissal, at Hask's own arrangement, which is the one kind thing he did that winter and the one he has never let himself count. Hask knows Alder better than anyone alive, and he knows the one thing he never trained out of them: **Alder does not believe anyone gets to decide who is worth spending.** Told the plan at the start, the captain would have refused. So Hask made Alder useful without asking.

**Plan A is that the levy company dies.** The commission, the short purse, the stolen pay and the raiders paid in keep silver all point at the raid on Ironwake (map 6). Sons and daughters of all three regions are to be slaughtered at the armistice keep, the rite goes unperformed, and each region blames the other two. Alder was meant to die on that wall too. Hask is the one who says so, at the Grange, without flinching: *"I gave you the cleanest death I could find. On a wall, before you had to see what comes after."*

**The player breaks Plan A by winning map 6.** So at the Grange (map 7), Hask finally asks. He comes alone, under truce, and tells all of it as he believes it: the Kin, the prison, the oath, the villages, and the three parts of the rite, which he is the only man alive who can recite. This is the title drop. Then he asks for one thing: **"Give me the list."** The company's names are to be the first sworn after his own men, with the captain at his right hand. The captain refuses. That refusal is the break, and it is a difference of ideals, not tactics. The captain's list is people who choose to stay. Hask's oath is people who cannot leave, and then are not people. *"Then I'll take it the long way,"* Hask says. *"You'll carry it for me. I raised you honest."*

**What he sees in the captain's class.** By the Grange, the captain has chosen at the first promotion, and Hask reads it at a glance. One line per class:
- **Vanguard:** *"You took the front. I taught you that. I taught you the rest of it too, and you didn't keep it."*
- **Marshal:** *"You lead them. Good. Then you know what it's like when one of them won't follow."*
- **Ranger:** *"You learned to stand off and pick your shot. I never taught you that. Someone at that academy earned their pay."*

**Proposed (pending Lotus, round 366): the player may give him the list.** The partners' lean is the cheap form, and draft 7 writes its shape so Lotus can judge it as story and not as a menu. At the Grange, "Give me the list" is a real choice on the same screen the branch pick uses, with a confirm on the yes. If the captain hands it over, Hask reads the ten names aloud, in the clerk's order, and reads the captain's name first, at the top, where the crossed-out one was. Then the card goes into "we" and names nobody, and the campaign ends there. The save records `knelt`. Nothing else is written: no sworn campaign, no playing on as his. What it earns: the refusal becomes a choice the player made, and Hollin two maps later confirms what they risked. What it risks: a button that reads as "bad ending here"; the cure is that the offer sounds like Hask and the yes carries no warning but the confirm.

## The title drop

**First reading, map 7, Hask at the Grange.** Said as an accusation, on the main line, so every player hears it:

> *"They told you it was named for the iron. It is. Frozen iron, three hundred years thick, so the thing under it doesn't wake. Ironwake. They put a god in a cellar and a garrison on the lid, and then they sent the garrison home to save the pay."*

**Written reading, Pell's quest 2 (optional).** The founding charter on the undercroft wall, in the shrine script only Pell can read: *"Let the iron keep it from waking, and let us keep the iron."* The second clause is the one the regions forgot: *us*, the Count, the people on the hill. Pell reads it twice in silence, then tears the copied page from her book and hands it to the captain. The card prints the charter once; no one says it aloud.

**Second reading, the reseal.** "Iron that wakes." The game has been saying it in its own mechanics the whole time, so the story only has to point:
- Teodor's lance *wakes*. Its stages are rust coming off frozen iron.
- Kinsbane *wakes* at the cap.
- The keep thaws as Hask breaks the prison (maps 9 and 10): rime comes off the walls, there is meltwater in the undercroft, and the frost line on the board's wall tiles recedes turn by turn on the finale. No new rule, only the board's description text.
- **The company is the iron that woke.** A levy is raw iron: owed names on a list, drafted, nobody's choice. At the reseal the region takes the keep's name for itself, and the last card says it plainly: the iron that kept the old peace was priests who had forgotten why, and the iron that keeps the new one is a company that woke up and stayed. The Count, answered.

The echo is one line from the captain at the reseal: *"Same name. Different iron."*

## Why the captain fights him at the end

1. **Marrit.** The first name on the captain's list is crossed out. Marrit was a garrison ward too, older, the one who taught Alder to hold the sword Hask gave them. Alder was told she died in the dismissal winter. She did not: she is Hask's first sworn and his champion, because she was the one who loved him most, and when he asked for a first she said yes before he finished. On Brackwater Cut she leads the hunt in the dark. She cannot be talked round the way the passed branch recruit can. *"I want to, Alder. I can't."* She is the oath made visible: eight years as the Kin's first sworn soul after Hask, close to a husk. The oath breaks when its door shuts. Hask is the only way to get her back.
2. **The people from the Cut.** On map 8 the company carries the seam's people out. At the keep, Hask has come to swear them, the people the captain promised to protect.
3. **The man himself.** Hask is the nearest thing to a parent Alder has, and he planned the captain's death and called it a kindness.

**Marrit on the keep.** She is freed when Hask falls. The player may kill her first, and that is allowed and printed. It is the harder fight, but it ends her hunt. If she falls, her ending is gone and the crossed-out name stays crossed out. If the company holds her and goes through to him, she drops her sword when he falls and sits down where she stands. Her first free line is to the captain: *"It's cold. I'd forgotten it was cold."* The mechanic is #750.

## The captain

**Alder Fenn**, a seam foundling and a garrison ward, raised by Hask and sent to the academy a month before the dismissal. They came back with a commission, a coat and a list of ten names owed to Ironwake under the armistice. One name is crossed out.

- **The name.** The garrison's cook found the baby against the outside of the bake-oven wall, wrapped in a grain sack, under the one tree in the keep yard. They named the child Alder for the tree, and Fenn because the roll needed two names and Fenn was the duty clerk whose turn it was. Alder knows the first half of that. The second half, and who found them, is the Cold Kitchen's (Bet, below). The origin pick (#648) is the card: wherever the sack was carried from, Alder grew up at the keep.
- **Why they are here:** the commission, and the list. Alder reads it every morning. It is the only part of the job they have done so far (map 1).
- **What they want:** to bring the names home. At first that is a duty. By the keep it is the only thing they believe in.
- **Their grievance:** the seam was abandoned by everyone at once, and Alder was the ward who got sent away first. It is survivor's guilt with a sword.
- **Their flaw:** they will not spend anyone, so they spend themselves. The story notices it once: Maud, after the raid, says *"You stood in front again. You know they can stand too."*
- **What they share.** With Marrit and Bet, the garrison's yard, the stove, the roll. With Rook, being the thing on the line below someone's holding. With Teodor, a grandfather's voice in the wrong mouth: Hask sounds like Teodor's grandfather because he learned to give orders from him.
- **The three classes:** Vanguard, Marshal and Ranger at the first promotion, then Champion, Commander or Pathfinder. The story reads the choice twice, at the Grange (above) and in the reseal's last card.

## The company: each one's own story

Each recruit has a past that is their own, a reason to be here, a thing the captain can help with (their quests, which is how the bond grows), a grievance with the world, a crossing with Hask, and a wound they share with at least one other person in the company, which is where the supports come from. Each also has their **answer to Hask**: at the camp after the Grange, every living member gets one line, their answer to "give me the list," written so the player can hear the yes in it before the no. ## Magic, as the story sees it (proposed; Lotus's rulings of 2026-10-05 and 2026-10-06)

Lotus has set an identity for each school: fire burns, a hit leaving a burn that ticks; ice or water slows, as the drake's frost and frozen iron's chill do; earth is mostly buffs and support; lightning is huge damage, with an end-game spell that can stun; Light heals, which is Faith; Dark is the grimoire from a mini-boss. The partners' mapping of the shipped spells onto the schools is on the Table (round 383) and in DIALOGUE. This section is the story side only.

- **Where Lore comes from.** Lore is taught, and where it was taught is a character's history. The shrine-schools of Aldmere taught it beside the copyists' hand (Pell). The Crown's academy at Harrow teaches it to its clerks and officers as a kind of arithmetic. Sallow's guilds teach it to the apprentices who stand on roofs (Ivo). A parish kept the small wards its roof and its alms door needed (Brannock, if Lotus takes him). Kestrow's church sells Faith and teaches no Lore, which is why no Kestrow recruit casts, and why a Kestrow caster would be a man who left the church. A tome is a book, and every book in this world has a provenance: who copied it, and who did not burn it.
- **Fire** is Pell's: Cinder, the school she learned from the books the faiths burned, and the burn is her temper. **Ice** is the north's, and a tome of it chills the way frozen iron does; the drake's frost stays the strongest cold on the field. **Lightning** is Ivo's. **Earth** is Brannock's, if Lotus takes him; the Scholar reaches it too (Chat's lean). **Light** is Maud's. **Dark** is the Tithe.
- **The Tithe.** The dark grimoire is a book Hask did not burn, carried by one of his lectors at the Grange or Brackwater: the Kin's warmth taken by hand, a spell that heals its caster out of the one it strikes. It carries our own name, the Tithe, warmth collected as a due, and never the genre's. Whoever carries it is refused by Faith's mending (Salve, Beacon, the Psalter): the goddess does not take on her, as the oath did not take on Bet. Given to Pell, she drifts toward the sworn's grammar and drops names, and is never sworn; the rite she writes down in the undercroft is cold, and her ending card says so. Given to Maud, the goddess does not speak at the first shrine, the word never comes, her doors close, and the rite falls to Pell's reading. A dozen lines each, written only when Lotus rules.
- **Tomes and promotions (Lotus's idea, 2026-10-06).** A unit with some Magic may pick up a school beyond its own through a tome, found in a chest or a quest or bought, or through a promotion that opens one. Lotus's addition: a stat check, so a spell asks for a Magic of so much before the unit can cast it. The story pass's recommendation is yes to tomes and yes to the check in place of a rank cap, for three reasons. The check reads on the card as a number, so a player sees what the book wants and no hidden rule decides (pillar 2). It makes a tome an object with a promise in it, a book carried two maps before it can be opened, and a reason to raise a stat. And it keeps the dedicated casters special without a rule that names them: the end-game lightning spell asks for a Magic only Ivo reaches in an ordinary run, the strong tomes of every school ask for more than a dabbler has, and the Tithe asks for nothing, because it takes rather than asks. Who can learn: any unit with Magic above zero, since Lore is taught and the stat is the aptitude. A promotion that opens a school is the Scholar's earth. The dark grimoire stays the one tome with a story cost. Every tome is named for where it came from, and the camp prints that line, so the shop carries story as well as numbers.

## The reseal: the good ending

The company does not use the god. **It re-freezes the prison by hand, as the faiths once did**, and draft 7 says what "by hand" means: the three parts of the rite, done by the people standing there.

When Hask falls, the oaths break. The Kin is robbed of its door and throws itself at the iron: that is the frost line receding on the walls. The company holds the undercroft while the rite is done.
- **The Word:** Maud says it if she lives, in the old order, with the given word in it. Pell reads it from the charter if Maud does not. If neither lives, the captain says it badly from memory, and for a shut door that is enough.
- **The Stone:** Wren's moved stones, if she is alive, carried up and set into the wall: the first honest course in eight years. If she is not, a stone from the breach.
- **The Count:** the captain reads the list on the hill, every name, who came and who stayed, and the crossed-out one uncrossed if Marrit lives. Bet climbs the hill first.
Every frozen-iron shard the company took from Hask's men goes back into the prison, and **the shard from Hask's pommel goes in last**: the first iron out is the last iron in, so the crack he heard through is the last thing shut. The rime climbs the walls again. *"Same name. Different iron."* is the last line.

The yearly renewal is kept, but it is no longer an oath to anything. It is the rite, and the people doing it know what it is. Every year on the armistice's date, whoever chooses climbs the hill, says the words, sets a stone and reads the names. The peace is not perfect, and the last card says so plainly: the regions still argue, a border stone still moves, and nobody is forced to agree. **They keep their free will. That is the whole prize.**

**One nation, governed from the keep** (Lotus, round 199 and draft 6). The regions unite, governed from the keep the player built all game, and the ending card names the rooms they built, so it is literal. The last image is one table in the keep, the three regions and the outland seat sitting at it, still arguing. One nation is not one mind, and that is the difference from the Kin, said by the ending itself. If the drake rider lives, the drake sleeps on the keep roof in the frost every winter, and children in the new nation grow up seeing it.

Two textures, both good endings:
- **Held:** a living member of each of Aldmere, Sallow and Kestrow stands in the undercroft at the end, so each part of the rite has its own region's hand, and the prison takes the frost cleanly.
- **Hand-locked:** someone is missing, and a part is done by the wrong hands. The door shuts, but thin, and the last line is draft 4's: *locked by hand, for now.*

**Conditional paragraphs** key on: held or hand-locked; Marrit alive or dead; the pick, and the passed one's fate; Ottilie's quest 2 won (the regions' people come up the hill); Teodor's lance woken (laid back in the prison, the first warden's key returned, and he keeps the shaft); Pell's quest 2 done (the rite written down for the first time, so it cannot be burned out of memory again); Wren alive (the stones in the wall); Bet alive (she climbs first); the captain's class (one sentence in the last card); who is alive to sit at the table.

## The secret ending: Under the Hill

**The cause.** The Kin half-wakes for one reason only: a waking rival god walks into its prison. Carry a woken Kinsbane into the keep, and the Kin feels it through the iron. It does not wait to be killed. When Hask falls and the door shuts, it tears itself half free to strike first. So the secret ending is not a reward switch: the player's own choices caused it.

**The conditions** (all four; spread from map 5 to map 10; none announced):
1. **Kinsbane woken** (15 fed), carried by Keziah, alive at the keep. From the Keziah pick she feeds it all the way; from the Rook pick she returns at 12 fed and the player feeds the last three.
2. **Both claimants at the keep.** The picked one, and the passed one won back on map 9 by the picked one's talk. Hask trusted the passed claimant: they carry his key to the inner door, and they have been down it.
3. **Marrit held, not killed** (#750). Freed, she is the only one who knows where it listens from, and she leads them down. Her free line gets a second half: *"It's cold. I'd forgotten it was cold. ... It's awake, Alder. I can hear it looking for me."*
4. **Pell's quest 2 done.** She read the charter and counted the spare rings, so the company knows what the Bane was hidden from and why it is here.

The four reasons: the cause (Kinsbane), the door (the returned claimant), the guide (Marrit) and the understanding (Pell).

**Stumble, then hunt.** If Kinsbane is woken but another condition is missing, the reseal card gets one line: *Keziah stands at the door a long time after it shuts. The scythe will not stop pulling toward it.*

**One extra map after the keep, and the choice is on its card.**
- **Reseal.** Needs a rite-keeper alive (Maud, or Pell reading); from memory shuts a door, never an open one. The held ending, plus one card: Kinsbane goes quiet, cheated of the only fight it ever wanted, and Keziah carries a disappointed god home.
- **Fight.** The half-sealed god. A boss that does not move, half its tiles still chained in frozen iron, fighting only through the sworn it re-takes on the field; Kinsbane is what can end it. Hask's pommel shard lies in the undercroft, and while it is whole the Kin re-takes one sworn unit a turn through it; breaking it stops that. The Kin cannot lean on the drake, the one thing on the field it cannot touch. The Kin speaks here, as itself, for the only time: to Marrit, to Keziah, to the captain, each line the listener's own want one step further than they would have taken it.
  - **Win (canon as built):** the Kin dies. The prison is empty for the first time in three hundred years, the rime runs off the walls and does not come back, and there is no rite to renew: the nation is born with no lock, only people. **The cost:** across the land the sworn wake. Most come back. Some were too far gone and stay husks. **Kinsbane's god is done:** sane and quiet, it says goodbye to Keziah and leaves, and the scythe becomes a plain blade she keeps anyway. The drake stays. The branch's last image is the two claimants side by side, one holding a plain blade and the other with a hand on a living drake. Sequel hook: an old outland god walks free somewhere in the world.
  - **Win (proposed, under the Bane):** the Kin's half is drawn into the scythe, the god is whole in a blade, and what happens next is one of the three shapes above, Lotus's to pick.
  - **Lose:** the worst ending in the game. The Kin rises fully and **the company is sworn first**. The epilogue is the captain's own card, written in "we". Then Load / New game / Quit.
- **With no rite-keeper alive, the card offers only Fight**, and says why.

**Epilogue cards** per recruit, paired by supports. The fallen keep their names at the top of the roll, uncrossed.

## What the captain learns

Hask is right that the three are the wound, right that the faiths bless the wars, and right that the armistice is a schedule. **His oath would end war, and it would be warm.** He is wrong about one thing, and the campaign is the argument: he thinks a peace nobody can leave is better than a peace people have to keep choosing, and he never sees that the first kind has nobody left in it. The captain starts with a list of names they did not choose, one of them crossed out. They end it by choosing every one, getting the crossed-out one back if they can, and climbing the hill again next year because they want to. The rite in three parts is the same lesson said by the world: a lock made of three faiths that would not stand in a room together held for three hundred years only because, once a year, three people climbed the same hill anyway.
