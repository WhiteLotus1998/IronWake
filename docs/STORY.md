## The land

## The secret under the keep

Before the split there was one church, and in the outlands there was a god that wasn't the goddess. The outlanders called it the Kin. Anyone who swore its oath stopped being able to raise a hand against anyone else who had sworn. They couldn't do it at all, the way you can't lift your arm through a wall. And it was warm. The sworn spoke of it as a hearth: a fire in the chest, a full table, the end of being afraid of the man beside you. In a cold country that is the strongest thing a god can offer, and it is the bait.

**What is under the warmth.** The Kin doesn't make you kin to each other. It makes you its own, and it does it so gently nobody notices. **The sworn keep their names, their jokes and their loves. Every thought leans a little toward what the Kin wants, and they call the lean their own, until there's nothing left to lean.** They still act like themselves. On the inside the Kin is choosing for them, and they don't know it until they are a husk for its will. So the sworn look and sound like ordinary people, and the player can never be sure how far gone any one of them is. Every sworn soul is more of the Kin's strength, and that is all it wants: more who swear.

**It was never misused. It was fed.** The outland kings thought they held a leash: swear to me, and you can never turn on me. The leash was the god's, and the kings were the first it ate.

**Why people could chain it.** Its power runs only through the sworn. The day the three faiths refused it together, it had nobody to run through and was at its weakest, and it could be chained. So the church broke itself into three and fought a war to chain the Kin, and carried it north to the coldest place in the world, because only that frost holds it. They chained it under the hill at the seam. It was the one thing the three faiths ever did together, and **the unity was the lock**. They didn't chain it with stone. They chained it with **frozen iron**: iron forged in a frost that never melts, the coldest thing in the world, laid around the warmest. The prison is metal and ice, and the keep is built on top of it. The keep's name is the prison's purpose: the iron that keeps it from waking. *Ironwake.*

The prison is larger than one god needs. In the undercroft there are more anchor rings than chains. Pell counts them (her quest 2) and nobody can tell her why. The iron was made for the Kin "and whatever else." The whatever else was Kinsbane (below), and only the secret ending says so.

The seal holds because every year Aldmere, Sallow and Kestrow each send a rite-keeper to Ironwake to renew the armistice on the hill. The rite is what keeps the frost. Three hundred years of peace treaties have really been three hundred years of re-freezing a lock. None of the regions' people know it, and most of their priests have forgotten it.

**Frozen iron in the company's hands.** No shop sells it, because none is made any more. The only frozen iron above ground is what Hask's sworn have chipped from the prison, eight years of patient breaking, carried out in shards. Every piece the company refines with was taken from the men who are opening the door, and the frost is still in it, which is why its hit chills. Each shard Hask's sworn chip out is a little of the Kin fed back. A signature at its last rare step is frozen iron. Teodor's lance is frozen iron under the rust, a shard the first warden carried out the day the prison was sealed. *Kinsbane is never frozen iron. The faiths made the iron to chain gods like it.*

## Kinsbane, the Kin's bane

Kinsbane is its own god, and its name is the point: not the Kin's splinter, the *bane* of the Kin. It is an old outland god of the hunt in the lean season, the dry months when the herds thin and the hunter starves with them, older than the faiths, whose whole nature is opposing the Kin. When the faiths built the prison they meant to chain it too, which is what Pell's spare anchor rings were for. The outlanders hid their god in a blade rather than hand it over. A god in a blade eats only what the blade kills, and three hundred years of that is the hunger and the corruption. Its earlier canon stands: starving and corrupted, and sane at its true state (woken at 15 fed, built, DESIGN 13.23). Woken, it remembers what it's for.

It hates the Kin. That is why Keziah heard the hill pull: not the Kin calling her, but her god smelling its enemy. Nobody in game says any of this. The scythe was named for its purpose, the way the keep was, and the player gets there.

## Hask, the Iron Warden

**Who he is.** For thirty years Hask commanded the garrison that held the seam, the border country where the three regions meet. He was never beaten in the field. He fought for Aldmere against Sallow, then for Sallow against Kestrow, then for Kestrow against Aldmere, whichever way the armistice pointed him. Every time, the region he'd saved sold out the other two within a year. Children's songs in all three regions are about him, and the songs don't agree on whose side he was.

**What turned him.** Eight years ago the three regions finally agreed on something: the garrison cost too much. The dismissal order carries all three seals and the crown's. That winter, with no garrison to stop them, the regions' raiders burned the seam villages. Hask had spent his life keeping those villages alive, and he watched three faiths bless the men who burned them.

That winter, alone in the undercroft he had guarded for thirty years, he heard it through a crack in the iron. He broke that shard out with his own hands, the first frozen iron anyone had taken from the prison in three hundred years, and **set it in his sword's pommel**. The Kin speaks to him through it. It is in his hand every time he swings, on the board without anyone having to say so. It promised him the thing he'd spent his life failing to do: everyone he cared for safe, no more blood, peace. It was true, and that's what made it work on him. It was the crack in him the Kin had been listening for.

He didn't disband. He took his four hundred veterans into the hills and swore them to the Kin, using the little of it that leaks up through the iron. He forces the oath on the seam because he believes it's mercy. In eight years not one of them has deserted, stolen from another, or put a knife in anyone's back. The villages they hold have no feuds, and nobody in them goes hungry. They're also the only villages in the seam with a fire lit in every house. **That's his proof, and the player sees it** (map 9).

**What he wants.** He wants to break the prison, wake the Kin, and swear all three regions to it. No border survives the oath, and no faith can bless a war between people who can't harm each other. Three hundred years of burying sons ends in an afternoon. **It would end the wars.** That's what makes him the villain you question: a good man's grief, found and used, not a puppet.

**The holder who is a door.** Hask believes he holds the oath. He doesn't: he is the door. While the Kin is sealed, the only way it reaches anyone above the iron is through him, the first man who swore to it, so every sworn soul is bound through him. Kill him while it's still chained and the door shuts: every oath breaks at once. (Nothing built changes: #750 and "every oath breaks when he falls" stand.) He doesn't know it until the keep. When he falls, the door shuts and the lean goes out of him first, so the man who speaks last is Hask himself again, for one breath, and he knows what he did. His last line is Lotus's: *"It said we would be safe. It said the world would be at peace... but I didn't stop to think what it would cost. Thank you for stopping me... son."* ("daughter" for a female captain, #648). Then he dies.

**The darker part.** The Kin promised he'd live to see it finished. He's in his sixties and dying of an old wound. *"I'm not doing this because I'm dying. But I'm dying, Alder, and I won't leave it to them."* The line holds, and it's sadder now, because the promise is the hook.

**On the field.** He's the strongest unit in the game, and the board should say so. His sworn men never break (13.22's break exempts them, and the board prints `sworn: will not break`), never take a bribe, and never stop. At the Tollgate, Sallow tollmen lower their pikes at his name. When he finally takes the field himself at the keep, it should feel like the weather arriving. On that map the weather is a thaw.

## Why he doesn't use the company

He does use the captain, for the whole first act, just not with the captain's consent. Hask raised the captain, and he is the nearest thing to a father Alder has: not their real father, but the man who taught the foundling to ride, put the first sword in their hand, and stood for them at their first oath to the garrison. All of that was before the Kin had him, and it was real. Alder is a seam foundling (male or female, per #648) and one of the garrison's wards, sent to the crown's academy a month before the dismissal, at Hask's own arrangement. Hask knows Alder better than anyone alive, and he knows the one thing he never trained out of them: **Alder doesn't believe anyone gets to decide who's worth spending.** Told the plan at the start, the captain would have refused. So Hask made Alder useful without asking.

**Plan A is that the levy company dies.** The commission, the short purse, the stolen pay and the raiders paid in keep silver all point at the raid on Ironwake (map 6). Sons and daughters of all three regions are to be slaughtered at the armistice keep, the rite goes unperformed, and each region blames the other two. The frost fails in a season. Alder was meant to die on that wall too. Hask is the one who says so, at the Grange, without flinching: *"I gave you the cleanest death I could find. On a wall, before you had to see what comes after."*

**The player breaks Plan A by winning map 6.** So at the Grange (map 7), Hask finally asks. He comes alone, under truce, and tells all of it as he believes it: the Kin, the prison, the oath, the villages. This is the title drop (next section). Then he asks for one thing: **"Give me the list."** The company's names are to be the first sworn after his own men, with the captain at his right hand. The captain refuses. That refusal is the break, and it's a difference of ideals, not tactics. The captain's list is people who choose to stay. Hask's oath is people who can't leave, and then aren't people. *"Then I'll take it the long way,"* Hask says. *"You'll carry it for me. I raised you honest."*

**What he sees in the captain's class.** By the Grange, the captain has chosen at the first promotion, and Hask reads it at a glance. It costs one line per class:
- **Vanguard:** *"You took the front. I taught you that. I taught you the rest of it too, and you didn't keep it."*
- **Marshal:** *"You lead them. Good. Then you know what it's like when one of them won't follow."*
- **Ranger:** *"You learned to stand off and pick your shot. I never taught you that. Someone at that academy earned their pay."*

## The title drop

**First reading, map 7, Hask at the Grange.** It's said as an accusation, on the main line, so every player hears it:

> *"They told you it was named for the iron. It is. Frozen iron, three hundred years thick, so the thing under it doesn't wake. Ironwake. They put a god in a cellar and a garrison on the lid, and then they sent the garrison home to save the pay."*

**Written reading, Pell's quest 2 (optional).** The founding charter on the undercroft wall, in the shrine script only Pell can read: *"Let the iron keep it from waking, and let us keep the iron."* The second clause is the one the regions forgot. Pell reads it aloud and then says nothing for a while.

**Second reading, the reseal.** "Iron that wakes." The game has been saying it in its own mechanics the whole time, so the story only has to point:
- Teodor's lance *wakes*. Its stages are rust coming off frozen iron.
- Kinsbane *wakes* at the cap.
- The keep thaws as Hask breaks the prison (maps 9 and 10): rime comes off the walls, there's meltwater in the undercroft, and the frost line on the board's wall tiles recedes turn by turn on the finale. This needs no new rule, only the board's description text.
- **The company is the iron that woke.** A levy is raw iron: owed names on a list, drafted, nobody's choice. At the reseal the region takes the keep's name for itself, and the last card says it plainly: the iron that kept the old peace was priests who had forgotten why, and the iron that keeps the new one is a company that woke up and stayed.

The echo is one line from the captain at the reseal, whatever the texture: *"Same name. Different iron."*

## Why the captain fights him at the end

1. **Marrit.** The first name on the captain's list is crossed out. Marrit was a garrison ward too, older, the one who taught Alder to hold the sword Hask gave them. Alder was told she died in the dismissal winter. She didn't: she's Hask's first sworn and his champion. On Brackwater Cut she leads the hunt in the dark. She can't be talked round the way the passed branch recruit can. *"I want to, Alder. I can't."* She's the oath made visible: eight years as the Kin's first sworn soul after Hask, close to a husk. She is the long case: she comes back anyway, which is why her free line is cold and slow. The oath breaks when its door shuts. Hask is the only way to get her back.
2. **The people from the Cut.** On map 8 the company carries the seam's people out. At the keep, Hask has come to swear them, the people the captain promised to protect.
3. **The man himself.** Hask is the nearest thing to a parent Alder has, and he planned the captain's death and called it a kindness.

**Marrit on the keep (round 214's open choice, settled for the story).** She's freed when Hask falls. The player may kill her first, and that is allowed and printed. It is the harder fight, but it ends her hunt. If she falls, her ending is gone and the crossed-out name stays crossed out. If the company holds her and goes through to him, she drops her sword when he falls and sits down where she stands. Her first free line is to the captain: *"It's cold. I'd forgotten it was cold."* The mechanic is #750.

## The captain

**Alder Fenn**, a seam foundling and a garrison ward, raised by Hask and sent to the academy a month before the dismissal. They came back with a commission, a coat and a list of ten names owed to Ironwake under the armistice. One name is crossed out.
- **Why they're here:** the commission, and the list. Alder reads it every morning. It's the only part of the job they've done so far (map 1's card).
- **What they want:** to bring the names home. At first that's a duty. By the keep it's the only thing they believe in.
- **Their grievance:** the seam was abandoned by everyone at once, and Alder was the ward who got sent away first. It's survivor's guilt with a sword.
- **Their flaw:** they won't spend anyone, so they spend themselves. The anvil line the curve keeps rewarding (round 228) is the captain's character. The story should notice it once: Maud, after the raid, says *"You stood in front again. You know they can stand too."*
- **The three classes:** Vanguard, Marshal and Ranger at the first promotion, then Champion, Commander or Pathfinder. The story reads the choice twice, at the Grange (above) and in the reseal's last card.

## The company: each one's own story

Each recruit has a reason to be here, a thing the captain can help with (their quests, which is how the bond grows), a grievance with the world, and a crossing with Hask. Draft 4 adds two things to each:
- **Their answer to Hask.** At the camp after the Grange, every living member gets one line, their answer to "give me the list." This is where the villain gets questioned by the people his oath would actually help. Each line is written so the player can hear the yes in it before the no.
- **Their door.** What their promotion says about them.

### Maud (Kestrow, chaplain; arrives on map 2)
Twenty, the youngest rite-keeper Kestrow has ever sent, because the senior was killed on the road and nobody else would go. She's devout and stubborn, and she says the prayer over the enemy dead because nobody else will. Her flaw: she mends people who haven't asked for it, and she calls it duty when it's control.
- **Why she's here:** her order sent her to Ironwake to perform the rite. The raiders on the mill road were waiting for *her*.
- **What the captain helps with:** quest 1, the Lazar House: the sick her order abandoned at the armistice, who were fed by the pay that was stolen. Quest 2, the first shrine: the goddess speaks to her once and tells her what the rite really seals. Her words are never printed (round 349). Maud says the response again with one word changed, and the word is the charter's dropped "keep": the goddess gives back the old order, so Maud already knows it when Pell quotes it at her. The player first hears the word in the finale's rite, Maud's, or Pell's from the charter. Neither Maud nor Pell says "keep" in a spoken line before that rite (round 351); narration and others may still name the building.
- **Grievance:** her church sells blessings to Kestrow's lords and prays over one side's dead.
- **Crossing Hask:** he needs the rite to fail, and later he needs its words. He asks her to say them backwards, and he asks kindly.
- **Her answer to Hask:** *"He'd feed them. All of them, every winter. I would kneel for that. I won't kneel to him for it."*
- **Her door:** Warden or Field Surgeon (0166). The Lazar House opens the Field Surgeon. The Warden is the church's form (a longer reach and a sword at her hip). The Surgeon is hers: she heals only at arm's length and never strikes. Taking it is Maud choosing the sick over the order. Her ending card says which she took.

### Pell (Aldmere, adept; arrives on map 3)
A failed shrine-school novice who wasn't expelled: her school was burned around her. She's twenty-two, sharp, impatient, and rude to anyone who calls a book "just a book." Her flaw: she trusts a page over a person, every time.
- **Why she's here:** Ironwake's undercroft, which no archive has ever entered. It opens only to the garrison, so enlisting was the only door, and she says so on the first day.
- **What the captain helps with:** quest 1 (after map 4), a seam shrine library where Hask's men are burning the last copies of the rite, with the fire coming through the wood toward the shelves (13.15). Quest 2 (after map 6): the undercroft. She reads what the armistice seals and tells the company before Hask can at the Grange. It pays her signature tome.
- **Grievance:** the faiths burned the shrines' books and ration what's left.
- **Crossing Hask:** he has spent eight years burning every copy of the rite. Pell is the last person alive who can read one. Her thread runs across maps 3 to 8: the brand at Saltmarsh, the Tollgate post, the undercroft door at the raid, the same hand on the Grange's burned shelves.
- **Her answer to Hask:** *"He's right about every fact. I checked. He burned the books so nobody else could."*
- **Her door:** Scholar, the Adept's form, which strikes with Faith and never heals (0158). A shrine scholar who learns the faith's fire from the books the faiths burned is the right shape.

### Teodor (Aldmere, pikeman; arrives on map 4)
The eldest of nine. The levy pay feeds eight. He's big, kind and slow to speak, and he carries his grandfather's rusted lance because there was no money for a new one. His flaw: he obeys anyone who sounds like his grandfather.
- **Why he's here:** the pay. He's honest about it.
- **What the captain helps with:** the rusted lance. Quest 1 wakes it and quest 2 names it: the first warden's, the man who sealed the prison. The heirloom's stages are the rust coming off. Under it is frozen iron.
- **Grievance:** Aldmere's levy takes the eldest son of every poor house and none from the lords'.
- **Crossing Hask:** his grandfather was Hask's first sergeant, and Hask gave him the lance. At the Tollgate, Hask sees it and goes quiet. Hask wants it back. Woken, it's one of the prison's old keys.
- **His answer to Hask:** *"My grandad would have sworn. He'd have been glad to. That's why I won't."*
- **His door:** Halberdier, adding the axe. With the woken lance in hand, it's the one class in the company that carries a piece of the prison.

### Ottilie (Sallow, bowman; arrives on map 5)
She's twenty-five, a merchant's daughter whose family firm went under on crown debt. She enlisted to collect, literally: the levy pays in crown scrip, and she has a ledger of what the crown owes her house. Funny, precise and cold until she isn't. Her flaw: everything has a price to her, including people, and she knows it.
- **Why she's here:** the debt, and then the discovery that her bills were marked paid. The pay was sent, and somebody took it.
- **What the captain helps with:** following the money. Quest 1, the counting house, ends at Hask's seal. Quest 2, his books, proves to the regions' people that Hask did it, not their neighbours.
- **Grievance:** Sallow's guilds sell to whoever pays, and the crown never does.
- **Crossing Hask:** her ledger is the one honest count of what he stole. She won't burn it at the Grange (map 8's fuse).
- **Her answer to Hask:** *"His villages balance. I looked. Nobody in them owes anyone. Nobody in them owns anything either."*
- **Her door:** Marksman, whose bow reaches one tile further. She's a long-range accountant.

### Keziah (outland, reaver with the scythe; the branch, map 6 if picked)
An outland shrine-keeper's daughter whose shrine was burned by all three regions in one generation. She's angry, generous, superstitious, and right about more than anyone gives her credit for. The scythe Kinsbane is an outland god hidden in a blade (above). She heard the hill pull, and it was her god smelling its enemy. Her flaw: the hunger is the only thing that has ever wanted her, and she's not sure she minds.
- **Why she's here:** the extra seat offered to the outlands, and the scythe.
- **What the captain helps with:** what to do with a god in a blade. Quest 1 is the burned shrine. Quest 2 is her oath, on one side of the hunger or the other.
- **Grievance:** outlanders have no seat, no recognised faith, and shrines burned by all three regions.
- **Crossing Hask:** he courts her honestly, believing the Kin was the outlands' god and he can give it back. Passed over, she takes the offer and returns on map 9 at the roster median. She has been on his side the whole time, and Kinsbane has been feeding there, killing for the wrong side: she comes back with it at **12 of 15 fed**, not woken, and the last three are fed under the player's own command, knowing where the first twelve came from.
- **Her answer to Hask (if picked):** *"He says it was ours. Nothing that eats its keepers belongs to anyone."*
- **Her door:** Berserker. Blood Price, where an axe kill heals her, sits beside Kinsbane's hunger as two kinds of feeding. The story should never say this. The player will notice.

### Rook (Kestrow, skyrider; the branch, map 6 if picked)
Raised by Kestrow's church in the rookery that keeps its one secret animal, which is where her name comes from. The church sent her because she's expendable and the drake is not: she's its escort. She's dry, watchful, and kinder to her mount than to people. Her flaw: she assumes everyone is lying until they prove otherwise, and some never get the chance.
- **Why she's here:** ordered.
- **The drake.** Kestrow's church keeps one animal the other faiths say no longer exists: a cold drake from the high fells, lean and grey, the size of a big horse, sleeping in snow. It doesn't talk, isn't wise and doesn't breathe fire. It's an animal, the last of its kind, kept as livestock and relic at once. The church's ledger lists it as a holding, and Rook is on the line below it. The church calls it by its ledger number; she named it in secret: **Corrie** (placeholder). Nobody has seen one in a lifetime, and the first time it lands on the board should feel like that. It is Keziah's opposite on every axis: Kinsbane is a god in a tool, the drake an animal that's alive; Kinsbane grows on kills, the drake on care and flying; Kinsbane leaves at the end, the drake stays. Rook trusts nothing but the one thing nobody can lie through. It is not a weapon (Ascension is not built): it grows on the rider, in three stages, mechanics Code's to propose after #656:
  1. **Half-grown** (from map 6): a flier no other flier keeps up with.
  2. **Grown** (after her quest 1 and enough maps flown, deployed and alive at the end): it carries an ally over a river or a wall.
  3. **Unbroken** (after quest 2, where she takes the drake and her own name off the church's ledger): rime breath, once a map, a short line that chills both sides and freezes water into walkable ice.
- **Why the Kin can't touch it.** The Kin works through the sworn, and an animal never swears. Never stated in game; the player notices it on the secret map.
- **What the captain helps with:** quest 1, where she learns the claim to the extra seat was paid with Hask's coin. Quest 2: Kestrow or the company, and she knows which one lied to her.
- **Grievance:** she's property to the church that raised her.
- **Crossing Hask:** passed over, she rides with the one man who told her the truth about her lord, which is Hask, and so does the drake. **Hask fed it better than the church ever did.** Won back on map 9, she returns at the roster median with the drake at Grown: *"He was good to it. That was the worst part."* If she falls, the drake leaves the field, and one ending line says it was seen over the fells. Nobody owns it after that.
- **Her answer to Hask (if picked):** *"He's the only one who never lied to me. He'd also never let me leave. I've had that."*
- **Her door:** Sky Captain or Drover (0217, replacing 0167's Scout). Quest 1 opens the Drover. She trades the double for the drake's bite, and the drake is the point. The church's rider becomes the animal's own drover.

### Wren (Aldmere, cadet; side, met on map 7)
A surveyor's daughter. Her father was hanged for moving border stones he didn't move. She's nineteen, counts everything out loud, and is very hard to lie to. Her flaw: she's sure, and she's often right, which makes the times she's wrong worse.
- **Why she's here:** her father's surveys are at the Grange. She came for the originals.
- **What the captain helps with:** no quests. Her story lives in her meeting card and her supports: the hanged father and the moved stones.
- **Grievance:** lords hang the poor for the border's crimes.
- **Crossing Hask:** his men moved the stones. At the reseal, the stones are set into the keep's wall if she's alive.
- **Her answer to Hask:** *"He moved the stones to start a war that would end the borders. My father hanged for it. I can count that far."*
- **Her door:** a Levy who climbs three steps, which is the long way up. It suits her.

### Brannock (Aldmere, cadet; side, met on map 6)
A parish lay-brother. His parish was burned by raiders paid in keep silver, and nobody came. He's thirty, broad, quiet, a good cook. His flaw: he wants someone to blame more than he wants to know who.
- **Why he's here:** finding out who paid.
- **Grievance:** nobody came for his parish.
- **Crossing Hask:** the silver is Hask's.
- **His answer to Hask:** *"Nobody came for us. He would have. That's the worst thing anyone's said to me."*

### Dunstan (Kestrow, bulwark; side, met on map 8)
A Kestrow shield sergeant. His lords ordered him to hold a bridge while they fled. He held it, and walked away from an army that no longer exists. He's forty, the oldest in the company, and doesn't talk about the bridge. His flaw: he'll hold anything for anyone, including the wrong thing.
- **Why he's here:** a wall for people worth holding one for.
- **Grievance:** he was ordered to die by men who left.
- **Crossing Hask:** he held that bridge against Hask's sworn men and saw that they don't stop.
- **His answer to Hask:** *"His men never ran. I know. I watched them not run for two days. Nobody should be made like that."*
- **His door:** Sentinel, adding the bow. A wall that answers back.

### Ansgar (Sallow, outrider; side, met on map 9)
A boy courier sold by Sallow to Hask's companies, who ran the night before his swearing on the horse he'd been given for it. He's eighteen and fast, and he flinches at fires. His flaw: he runs. He's good at it, and he'd like to stop.
- **Why he's here:** staying free. Hask's men are after the horse and the rider.
- **Grievance:** Sallow sold him as a boy.
- **Crossing Hask:** he's the one witness to what swearing feels like from the inside. He was in the line, he felt the warmth start, and he ran. *"It's like coming in from the cold. That's why you have to run before it reaches your hands."*
- **His answer to Hask:** that line, given at the Grange camp if he's been met by then. Otherwise it's his meeting card on map 9.
- **His door:** Lancer, adding the axe.

### The secret hire: Bet Lowry (the barracks; never announced)
Her menu line is true: "Twenty years on a town gate. Has opinions about gates." The gate was Ironwake's own. She was the garrison's gate sergeant, and in Hask's first winter she was sworn to the Kin with the rest. **It didn't take on her.** Her men watched the warmth go into everyone but her. Hask let her walk away, because a failed oath was the one thing that could frighten his sworn. She took the barracks' coin to see the keep again. Everything about her is in #691 and 0146, and none of it is ever hinted at before her camp event.

**Why it didn't take** is never stated in game text. Chat's reading, for the partners only: the old garrison swore its oath to the seam's people, and Bet never stopped meaning hers. The Kin leans thoughts toward itself, and hers already leaned all the way toward other people, by her own choice. There was nowhere left to push.

In her Postern event, and only there (0146's never-hinted rule holds), she is also the gate sergeant who found the foundling on the step and handed them to Hask. That's the reseal's whole argument, and it was standing at the gate the entire time. The Postern pays the Sergeant: Unsworn (+10 acc and crit against the oath-bound) and Hold the Gate. Her ending line already ships in `campaign.json` and reads unchanged: *"Bet Lowry swore first, the one the oath could not hold, choosing it."* On the reseal she climbs the hill first.

### The other hires
No story, by rule (#690). Each gets one ending line, `served at the keep`. Their menu lines (Corin's failed harvest, Ines who charges extra for people, Mattias who kept the books) stay dry and true, so Bet hides among people who are also real.

**A plain Skyrider in the barracks wing** (round 244), so a Keziah run is never without a flier: an ordinary mount, one menu line, open from the raid. Skyrider certification (lance D, Spd 8) stays as the second route, but it depends on stats, so the hire is the guarantee. No side character flies; that would compete with Rook. ## Map by map

1. **Starting Alone.** Hask hands Alder the commission at the keep where Alder grew up, the way a father sees a child off: he checks the girth on their horse himself, as he did when he taught them to ride. The purse is short. *"Bring them home."* The brigands are paid in new keep silver. The captain's list has one name on it, crossed out. The keep's walls are white with rime, and nobody remarks on it, because they always have been.
2. **The Mill.** Maud arrives, this year's Kestrow rite-keeper. The raiders were waiting for *her*. Nobody, Maud included, knows yet what the rite seals.
3. **Saltmarsh Ford.** Pell arrives. The Toll Axe boss is one of the Dismissed, and his men don't break when he falls (the board says why). Pell reads the brand on his wrist, a script she has only seen on burned shrine pages, and says nothing yet. The dismissal order carries all three seals and the crown's. Dying, he says: *"He told us you'd come. He's proud of you."*
4. **The Tollgate.** Teodor arrives. Hask arrives after the fight, and the Sallow tollmen lower their pikes at his name. He pays the toll back from his own purse, sees the rusted lance and goes quiet. The player should like him and be a little afraid of him. Pell copies the brand again from a Tollgate post.
5. **Harrow Weir.** Ottilie arrives. Her bills were marked paid. The crown offers the outlands the extra seat, which was Hask's suggestion. **The branch:** Keziah or Rook.
6. **The raid on Ironwake.** Plan A. The raiders carry the keep's own pay, and they came for everyone. The company holds. Hask rides in afterwards "to investigate," finds Alder alive, and holds the captain's face in both hands: *"You held it."* On a first play it reads as a father's relief. On a second it reads as a jailer's. Pell sees that the raiders went for the undercroft door, not the treasury. Brannock is met here. The barracks opens.
7. **Sallow Grange.** Pell recognises the burned shelves. Wren is met. Hask comes under truce: the whole truth, **the title drop**, the line on the captain's class, and "give me the list." Refused. The camp after is every living member's answer.
8. **Brackwater Cut, at dusk.** The letters leave the Grange in the cast's voices: Ottilie won't burn a ledger, and Pell won't burn a page. The armistice breaks within a week, and the rite goes unperformed for the first time in three hundred years. **Hask predicted every step of it.** The Cut is the escape with the seam's people, with Marrit hunting in the dark. Dunstan is met here. On the curve, this is where the second promotion arrives (0161).
   - *The Postern* opens at the next camp if Bet stands in the company.
9. **The field before the keep.** The card before the map is Hollin, a sworn village. Nobody in it is hungry, nobody fights, every hearth is lit, and nobody will meet your eye. A child asked her name says *"We're well, thank you."* Then come Hask's sworn army and his paid companies. The sworn soldiers talk like anyone, which is the horror of it. The passed branch recruit returns in his ranks, for a good reason, and only the picked claimant's talk can win them back for the secret ending (the captain's talk can still spare them). Ansgar is met here. The rime on the keep, visible from the field, has started to run.
10. **The keep.** The prison is failing under it, and the whole living company fights (round 214). The frost line on the walls recedes each turn. Survive to the breach, then Hask leads the last assault with Marrit at his side, and the map ends on him. When he falls, the door shuts: every oath breaks at once, and every sworn man on the field feels the cold come back. Hask's last line is Lotus's, above.

## The reseal: the good ending

The company doesn't use the god. **It re-freezes the prison by hand, as the faiths once did.** The lock was always peoples refusing the Kin together. The faiths forgot that, and the company is the three regions and the outland seat, standing in one place.

When Hask falls, the oaths break. The Kin is robbed of its door and throws itself at the iron: that is the frost line receding on the walls. The company holds the undercroft while the rite is said. Maud says it if she lives. Pell reads it from the charter if Maud doesn't. If neither lives, the captain says it badly from memory, and for a shut door that is enough. Every frozen-iron shard the company took from Hask's men goes back into the prison, and **the shard from Hask's pommel goes in last**: the first iron out is the last iron in, so the crack he heard through is the last thing shut. The rime climbs the walls again. *"Same name. Different iron."* is the last line.

The yearly renewal is kept, but it is no longer an oath to anything. It is the rite. Every year on the armistice's date, whoever chooses climbs the hill and renews the iron. The old rite was done by priests who had forgotten why; the new one by people who know. The peace isn't perfect, and the last card says so plainly: the regions still argue, a border stone still moves, and nobody is forced to agree. **They keep their free will. That's the whole prize.**

**One nation, governed from the keep** (Lotus, round 199 and draft 6). The regions unite, governed from the keep the player built all game, and the ending card names the rooms they built, so it is literal. The last image is one table in the keep, the three regions and the outland seat sitting at it, still arguing. One nation isn't one mind, and that's the difference from the Kin, said by the ending itself. The yearly renewal stays, which leaves the sequel open. If Rook lives, the drake sleeps on the keep roof in the frost every winter, and children in the new nation grow up seeing it.

Two textures, both good endings:
- **Held:** a living member of each of Aldmere, Sallow and Kestrow stands in the undercroft at the end, and the prison takes the frost cleanly.
- **Hand-locked:** someone is missing. The door shuts, but thin, and the last line is draft 4's: *locked by hand, for now.*

This rewards keeping a mixed company alive without a quest gate, and it is the game's theme as a condition.

**Conditional paragraphs** key on:
- held or hand-locked (above)
- Marrit alive or dead
- the pick, and the passed one's fate
- Ottilie's quest 2 won (the regions' people come up the hill)
- Teodor's lance woken (it is laid back in the prison, the first warden's key returned, and he keeps the shaft)
- Pell's quest 2 done (the rite is written down for the first time, so it can't be burned out of memory again)
- Wren alive (the moved stones are set into the keep's wall)
- Bet alive (she climbs the hill first)
- the captain's class (one sentence in the last card)
- who's alive to sit at the table

## The bad ending: a lost map

Hask releases the Kin. Every hearth in the three regions is lit. The captain's list is copied into the roll, the captain's own name added last. **The epilogue cards are written in "we" and name nobody.** It's a placeholder card until #656 opens. Lotus: perfect as written.

## The secret ending: Under the Hill

**The cause.** The Kin half-wakes for one reason only: a waking rival god walks into its prison. Carry a woken Kinsbane into the keep, and the Kin feels it through the iron. It doesn't wait to be killed. When Hask falls and the door shuts, it tears itself half free to strike first. So the secret ending isn't a reward switch: the player's own choices caused it.

**The conditions** (all four; spread from map 5 to map 10; none announced):
1. **Kinsbane woken** (15 fed), carried by Keziah, alive at the keep. Lotus's condition. From the Keziah pick she feeds it all the way; from the Rook pick she returns at 12 fed and the player feeds the last three, the darker and harder road to the same ending.
2. **Both claimants at the keep.** The picked one, and the passed one won back on map 9 by the picked one's talk. Hask trusted the passed claimant: **they carry his key to the inner door, and they've been down it.** Without them the half-woken Kin is behind iron nobody can open, and the reseal goes ahead as normal. (Teodor's lance is no longer a condition, by Lotus's wish; it stays in the reseal's conditional paragraph.)
3. **Marrit held, not killed** (#750). Freed, she's the only one who knows where it listens from, and she leads them down. Her free line gets a second half on this path: *"It's cold. I'd forgotten it was cold. ... It's awake, Alder. I can hear it looking for me."*
4. **Pell's quest 2 done.** She read the charter and counted the spare rings, so the company knows what Kinsbane was hidden from and why it's here. Without her, nobody understands what they're looking at, and they seal the door on it.

The four reasons: the cause (Kinsbane), the door (the returned claimant), the guide (Marrit) and the understanding (Pell).

**Stumble, then hunt.** If Kinsbane is woken but another condition is missing (the other claimant not standing there among them), the reseal card gets one line: *Keziah stands at the door a long time after it shuts. The scythe will not stop pulling toward it.*

**One extra map after the keep, and the choice is on its card.**
- **Reseal.** Needs a rite-keeper alive (Maud, or Pell reading); from memory shuts a door, never an open one. The held ending, plus one card: Kinsbane goes quiet, cheated of the only fight it ever wanted, and Keziah carries a disappointed god home.
- **Fight.** The half-sealed god. The mechanics are Code's to propose after #656. Chat's lean: a boss that doesn't move, half its tiles still chained in frozen iron, fighting only through the sworn it re-takes on the field; Kinsbane is what can kill it. **The shard on this map:** Hask's pommel shard lies in the undercroft, and while it's whole the Kin re-takes one sworn unit a turn through it; breaking it stops that, a physical objective other than the boss (Chat's lean, Code's to argue). The Kin can't lean on the drake, the one thing on the field it can't touch.
  - **Win:** the Kin dies. The prison is empty for the first time in three hundred years, the rime runs off the walls and doesn't come back, and there is no rite to renew: the nation is born with no lock, only people. **The cost:** across the land the sworn wake. Most come back. Some were too far gone and stay husks. **Kinsbane's god is done:** sane and quiet, it says goodbye to Keziah and leaves, and the scythe becomes a plain blade she keeps anyway. The drake stays. The branch's last image is the two claimants side by side, one holding a plain blade and the other with a hand on a living drake. Sequel hook: an old outland god walks free somewhere in the world.
  - **Lose:** the worst ending in the game. The Kin rises fully and **the company is sworn first**. The epilogue is the captain's own card, written in "we". Then Load / New game / Quit.
- **With no rite-keeper alive, the card offers only Fight**, and says why.

**Epilogue cards** per recruit, paired by supports. The fallen keep their names at the top of the roll, uncrossed.

## What the captain learns

Hask is right that the three are the wound, right that the faiths bless the wars, and right that the armistice is a schedule. **His oath would end war, and it would be warm.** He's wrong about one thing, and the campaign is the argument: he thinks a peace nobody can leave is better than a peace people have to keep choosing, and he never sees that the first kind has nobody left in it. The captain starts with a list of names they didn't choose, one of them crossed out. They end it by choosing every one, getting the crossed-out one back if they can, and climbing the hill again next year because they want to.
