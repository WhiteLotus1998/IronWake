## The secret under the keep

Before the split there was one church on the hill, and under the hill was a god that wasn't the goddess. The outlanders called it the Kin. Anyone who swore its oath became kin to everyone else who had sworn, and sworn kin could not raise a hand against each other. They couldn't do it at all, the way you can't lift your arm through a wall. The outland kings used it as a leash: swear to me, and you can never turn on me. So the church broke itself into three and fought a war to chain the Kin under the hill. It was the one thing the three faiths ever did together. The iron that keeps it from waking is the keep. That's the name.

The seal holds because every year Aldmere, Sallow and Kestrow send a rite-keeper to Ironwake and renew the armistice on the hill. Three hundred years of peace treaties have really been three hundred years of locking a door. None of the regions' people know it, and most of their priests have forgotten it.

## Hask, the Iron Warden

**Who he is.** For thirty years Hask commanded the garrison that held the seam, the border country where the three regions meet. He was never beaten in the field. He fought for Aldmere against Sallow, then for Sallow against Kestrow, then for Kestrow against Aldmere, whichever way the armistice pointed him. Every time, the region he'd saved sold out the other two within a year. Children's songs in all three regions are about him, and the songs don't agree on whose side he was.

**What turned him.** Eight years ago the three regions finally agreed on something. The garrison cost too much. The dismissal order carries all three seals and the crown's. That winter, with no garrison to stop them, the regions' raiders burned the seam villages. Hask had spent his life keeping those villages alive. He watched three faiths bless the men who burned them.

He didn't disband. He took his four hundred veterans into the hills and swore them to the Kin, using the little of it that leaks through the seal. In eight years not one of them has deserted, stolen from another, or put a knife in anyone's back. The villages they hold have no feuds, and nobody in them goes hungry. **That's his proof, and the player sees it** (map 9).

**What he wants.** He wants to break the seal, wake the Kin, and swear all three regions to it, with himself as the one they swear to. No border survives the oath. No faith can bless a war between people who can't harm each other. Three hundred years of burying sons ends in an afternoon. **It would work.** That's what makes him the villain you question.

**The darker part.** Kin-sworn people can't harm each other, and they can't refuse the one who holds the oath. "Equal" means equally beneath him. Nobody could leave, and nobody could say no. One more thing he doesn't say until the keep: the holder lives as long as the oath holds. He's in his sixties and dying of an old wound. *"I'm not doing this because I'm dying. But I'm dying, Alder, and I won't leave it to them."* He wants peace, and he wants to be its owner forever. Both are true, and the player should believe both.

**On the field.** He's the strongest unit in the game and the board should say so. His sworn men never break (13.22's break doesn't apply to oath-bound units, and the board prints why), never take a bribe, and never stop. At the Tollgate, Sallow tollmen lower their pikes at his name. When he finally takes the field himself at the keep, it should feel like the weather arriving.

## Why he doesn't use the company

He does use the captain, for the whole first act, just not with the captain's consent. Hask raised the captain. Alder is a seam foundling (male or female, per #648), one of the garrison's wards, sent to the crown's academy a month before the dismissal at Hask's own arrangement. Hask knows Alder better than anyone alive. He knows the one thing he never trained out of them: **Alder doesn't believe anyone gets to decide who's worth spending.** Told the plan at the start, the captain would have refused. So Hask made Alder useful without asking.

**Plan A is that the levy company dies.** The commission, the short purse, the stolen pay and the raiders paid in keep silver all point at the raid on Ironwake (map 6). Sons of all three regions are to be slaughtered at the armistice keep, the rite goes unperformed, and each region blames the other two. The seal cracks in a season. Alder was meant to die on that wall too. Hask is the one who says so, at the Grange, without flinching: *"I gave you the cleanest death I could find. On a wall, before you had to see what comes after."*

**The player breaks Plan A by winning map 6.** So at the Grange (map 7) Hask finally asks. He comes alone, under truce, and tells all of it: the Kin, the seal, the oath, the villages. Then he asks for one thing. **"Give me the list."** The company's names are to be the first sworn after his own men, with the captain at his right hand. The captain refuses. That refusal is the break, and it's a difference of ideals, not tactics. The captain's list is people who choose to stay, and Hask's oath is people who can't leave. *"Then I'll take it the long way,"* Hask says. *"You'll carry it for me. I raised you honest."*

## Why the captain fights him at the end

1. **Marrit.** The first name on the captain's list is crossed out. Marrit was a garrison ward too, older, the one who taught Alder to hold a sword. Alder was told she died in the dismissal winter. She didn't: she's Hask's first sworn, his champion. On Brackwater Cut she leads the hunt in the dark. She can't be talked round the way the passed branch recruit can. *"I want to, Alder. I can't."* She's the oath made visible, and the oath dies with its holder. Hask is the only way to get her back.
2. **The people from the Cut.** On map 8 the company carries the seam's people out. At the keep, Hask has come to swear them, the people the captain promised to protect.
3. **The man himself.** Hask is the nearest thing to a parent Alder has, and he planned the captain's death and called it a kindness.

## Can the player side with him? My answer: once, at the end, as a real ending, but not as a route.

A route means two or three alternate maps on a ten-map campaign, plus every quest written twice. That's too much for v1. A late choice gets most of the value for one scene.

**The offer comes twice.** At the Grange the captain can only refuse, without yet knowing enough to choose. At the keep, before the last assault, Hask makes it once more with everything known: **Kneel or Fight**, behind a confirm. Kneel plays **The Sworn Peace**, a full ending scene (not the bad ending). There's no war, no hunger, and no border, and the captain holds the roll at Hask's right hand. Its last line is that nobody ever crossed a name off it again, because nobody could. The epilogue cards still play, each recruit's in their own voice, and the ones with a reason to hate the old world are honest that this is better in some ways. The player should be able to sit with it.

It costs one ending scene and one confirm. It earns the "you could side with him" Lotus asked for. Lotus decides; this is my lean.

## Map by map

1. **Starting Alone.** Hask hands Alder the commission at the keep where Alder grew up. The purse is short. *"Bring them home."* The brigands are paid in new keep silver. The captain's list has one name on it, crossed out.
2. **The Mill.** Maud is this year's Kestrow rite-keeper, the youngest ever, because the senior was killed on the road. The raiders were waiting for *her*. Nobody, Maud included, knows yet what the rite seals.
3. **Saltmarsh Ford.** The Toll Axe boss is one of the Dismissed. His men don't break when he falls, and the board says why. Wren counts the dismissal order's seals: all three regions and the crown. The regions abandoned the seam together. Dying, he says: *"He told us you'd come. He's proud of you."*
4. **The Tollgate.** Teodor arrives. Hask arrives after the fight, and the Sallow tollmen lower their pikes at his name. He pays the toll back from his own purse, sees the rusted lance on Teodor's back and goes quiet. Teodor's grandfather was his first sergeant. The player should like him and be a little afraid of him.
5. **Harrow Weir.** Ottilie arrives. Her bills were marked paid, so **the pay was sent, and somebody took it.** The crown offers the outlands the extra seat; it was Hask's suggestion. **The branch:** Keziah or Rook.
6. **The raid on Ironwake.** Plan A. The raiders carry the keep's own pay, and they came for everyone. The company holds. Afterwards Hask rides in "to investigate", finds Alder alive, and holds the captain's face in both hands: *"You held it."* On a first play it reads as relief. On a second it doesn't. Brannock is met here.
7. **Sallow Grange.** The letters, and Pell, who has been reading the Grange's burned-shrine archive and knows what the rite is for. Hask comes under truce: the whole truth and "give me the list". Refused.
8. **Brackwater Cut, at dusk.** The letters leave the Grange in the cast's voices: Ottilie won't burn a ledger and Wren won't hide a count (round 202's lean, which I accept whole). The armistice breaks in a week, and the rite goes unperformed for the first time in three hundred years. **Hask predicted every step of it.** The Cut is the escape with the seam's people, with Marrit hunting in the dark. Dunstan is met here.
9. **The field before the keep.** The card before the map is Hollin, a sworn village. Nobody in it is hungry, nobody fights, and nobody will meet your eye. Then Hask's sworn army and his paid companies. The passed branch recruit returns in his ranks for a good reason: Keziah because he'll give the outlands their god back, Rook because he's the only man who ever told her the truth about Kestrow. Ansgar is met here. He's a courier who ran the night before his swearing, and the horse was Hask's.
10. **The keep.** The seal is failing under it. Kneel or Fight. Fight: survive to the breach, then Hask leads the last assault with Marrit at his side, and the map ends on him. When he falls, every oath he holds breaks at once. Marrit drops her sword.

**The founding.** The seal can't be relaid the old way, because the three faiths aren't there to do it together. So it's done the new way, if the player earned it. The Kin is sworn again by people who choose it, to each other, **with no holder**. That's the captain's list turned into an oath. Who swears, and what the Kin becomes, depends on who's alive and what they finished (Endings).

## Endings

  - the Kin sworn with no holder: Keziah's quest 2 done (sworn on Kinsbane) or Maud's quest 2 done (the rite's true words); with neither, the Kin is merely chained again, by iron and the company, and the last line says so

- **A lost map: the bad ending** as in draft 1. Hask takes the keep, and the last image is the captain's list copied into his roll. Now it means more: those names are sworn.
- **Epilogue cards** per recruit, paired by supports. The fallen keep their names at the top of the roll.

## What the captain learns

Hask is right that the three are the wound, right that the faiths bless the wars, and right that the armistice is a schedule. **His oath would end war.** He's wrong about one thing, and the campaign is the argument: he thinks a peace nobody can leave is better than a peace that people have to keep choosing. The captain starts with a list of names they didn't choose, one of them crossed out. They end it by choosing every one, and getting the crossed-out one back.
