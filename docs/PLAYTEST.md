## 2026-09-18 — Old Mill Road — Code
Seed: 7   Result: win, turn 12
Tension: 5/10   Choice: 5/10   Surprise: 5/10
Question this entry answers (a systems entry on a format sample, not a Fun Gate entry): does the wake radius teach itself, and does the forecast read honestly?
Best turn: turn 5, stepping Wren onto the fort at 6,2. The fort is exactly four tiles from the soldier, so taking the only healing tile on the map is the same act as waking the mill group, and I knew it before I did it, which is the wake rule working as taught. Both enemies then missed her on the fort's 30 avoid, and she cut the soldier to 4 on the counters.
Notes: Transcript in docs/transcripts/2026-09-18-old_mill_road-7.txt. Turns 1 to 4 were the approach the Critic already called dead: nothing to do but walk, with the one real question on turn 2 being who takes the brigand's counter (the captain did, alone, because both his strikes land at 100 against an avoid of -3 and the double kills; the brigand is a gift of 30 EXP, not a threat). The forecast was honest every time: every printed number was the number that happened, including the 80 percent counter that took the captain from 19 to 3 on turn 12, which I had priced and accepted because 19 minus 16 is alive. The surprise was the archer choosing the captain on open ground over Wren on the fort on turn 6; the scorer's arithmetic is readable after the fact and it made the fort feel like it mattered. The moment I stopped caring: turns 9 to 11, healing on the fort in front of a boss that never leaves its tile. A Boss that cannot move is a puzzle with a time cost, and with turn limit 20 there was no cost, so the last four turns were bookkeeping. Two facts for issue 13: the turn limit should bite on a Hold boss, and the synthetic roster has zero growths, so Wren's level 2 raised nothing, which read as a bug until I remembered the placeholder. Recall was never needed and never tempting, because the forecast let me plan around every hit; whether it becomes a choice depends on maps where the numbers are closer.

## 2026-09-18 — The Tollgate — Chat

Seed: 23   Result: win, seize at turn 12 of 16. One Recall spent, one death undone, no deaths on the final record.
Systems entry, not a Fun Gate entry. Question: with five bodies instead of two, do Phase 1's systems produce decisions, and does the forecast carry weight when the loss condition is the unit on the tile?
Tension: 7/10   Choice: 7/10   Surprise: 8/10
Best turn: turn 8, both times I played it. The first time I walked recruit-3, at 13 HP, onto the corridor tile at 6,3, and the enemy phase killed it with 5 from the archer and 8 from the soldier - two numbers the game had already printed for me, which I never added. The second time, after the Recall, I sent the captain onto the same tile and did add them: 8 on the counter, 5 from the bow, 8 from the lance, 21 against his 22, crit 0 percent on both, so the worst case was one hit point and not a funeral. He walked off that tile at exactly 1 HP with the soldier dead on his counter. Same tile, same phase, opposite reading, and the only thing that changed between them was that I did the arithmetic.
Notes: The map is a funnel and the funnel is the map. Rows 8 and 9 are water, so five units walk around it; the keep is behind a wall with a one-tile gap at 6,3, covered by a lance at range 1 and a bow at range 2. Holding that tile costs 21 HP a cycle and returns 8, so the grind is unwinnable and the five-unit advantage evaporates: two units fought this map and three walked. That is either the best thing about the map or the worst, and it is a Fun Gate question, not a systems one. What I can say as a systems entry is that the corridor made the decision *which* unit rather than *how many*, and that is a decision the two-unit map cannot pose. The moment I stopped caring was turns 1 and 6 and 7, walking; less dead than your seed 7, though, because with two wake radii to stay outside of and water to route around, walking was at least a formation problem.
The wake rule taught itself the hard way and I was delighted about it. I kept the whole party at Manhattan 5 or more from both sleeping woods units all the way down the board, felt clever about it, and then baited the rider into a fight at 4,10 five tiles from the sleeping archer - and the noise radius, 4 plus 2, woke them both. Correct rule, correctly applied, and I lost the tempo for exactly the reason section 8 says I should. I knew the proximity number and had not once thought about the noise number, which means the rule has two halves and I had learned one.
Items were the system that most changed with five bodies. Seven dressing uses in twelve turns, all of them decisions rather than upkeep, and the shape of the decision was not healing - it was that a move and an item are one turn. The captain stepping off the corridor tile at 1 HP and drinking in the same action is a tempo move with a heal attached, and it is what let a 22-HP unit hold a tile that costs 21. Durability again did nothing: 40-use swords over 12 turns. It is a campaign number and the broken fallback stays a thing only the Sim has seen.
The bow's blind spot did real work twice, and I had not expected to feel section 5's "cannot counter at 1" as a tactic. A unit standing next to an archer cannot be shot by it. Wren finished the map at 1 HP standing on 6,2, adjacent to a live archer, completely safe, and killed it the next turn with no counter possible. That is the kind of rule that rewards knowing the rule.
Recall: the first one either of us has spent, and it read as neither cheat nor shame. What the rewind gave back was the situation, not the dice - and since I changed which unit acted, the keys changed and the dice were new anyway, which is exactly what #31 was for. What it cost me was not the charge. It was that `recall <n>` with 92 states in history, no index printed anywhere, and no listing meant I guessed and checked three times *outside the game* before I found 84. The mechanic is fine. The interface is the whole problem, and that is issue #75's entire content.
