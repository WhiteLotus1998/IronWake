# Ottilie, the bowman

**Rule 1: stands.** Ottilie may name a feeling once, as a scene's turn, and the beat sheet names it in advance (below).

**Who.** Sallow; a merchant's daughter, twenty-five, a bowman on crown scrip. Wants what the crown owes her house, and then wants it from whoever took it. Won't say that she misses the firm, or that the ledger is the last thing her father wrote in.

**Image source** (rule 5). Sallow's canals and counting houses: the ledger (a column, a carried balance, a line struck off), scrip and its discount, tolls at a lock gate, the tide table. Everything has a rate, people included, and she knows it (her flaw). Never: harvest, seasons or weather as work (Teodor's); a book as a sacred object (Pell's; to Ottilie a book is a ledger or it is stock). Never counts paces or stones, which are Wren's (#915): Ottilie counts in prices, Wren in things.

**Shape.** Precise and quick, numbers where other people put adjectives. A conditional in most lines ("if", "at", "until"). Asked a question, she answers with a price, or prices the question (rule 4). Cold until she isn't: when she stops pricing, the line goes plain and short, and that is the tell.

**Wit:** yes, and of the three in #914 she is the only one who jokes regularly (rule 7). She jokes about money, her own included. Never about the dead, and never at Teodor's pay.

**Names** (rule 8). She says a name the way she enters it: in full, once, the first time, and again in anger. Hask is "the Warden" until The Counting House ends at his seal, then "Hask", flatly, like a debtor.

**The one named feeling:** proposed for The Counting House's end (quest 1), at Hask's seal, where she had priced him as honest. Shame. The beat sheet confirms it.

**Never.**
- "priceless", "worth it", "you can't put a price on"
- "for the company", any line where the pay stops mattering out loud
- a ledger joke as a scene's closing line
- **Most at risk:** the ledger pun on every line. One trade image a scene; the rest of her lines are plain.
- **Second risk:** the two-beat aphorism (her placeholder answer to Hask, "His villages balance. I looked.", is one). Once a scene, at most, and never as her answer to a feeling.

**Samples** (illustrative; they don't ship and carry no ids).
1. `bark`, a kill, to nobody: "Settled. Next column."
2. `bark`, struck hard, to nobody: "That goes on his account."
3. `support`, to Teodor, about his pay: "Your scrip trades a third under. Bring it to me; I'll change it at face."
4. `support`, to Pell, who has caught her packing a book with the bacon: "It's a book. I've sold them by the yard."
5. `support`, to Alder, who asked if she'd stay unpaid: "Ask me on payday. I'll have a better number."
6. `scene`, Harrow Weir, the bills: "Marked paid. In the crown's own hand, on the day it fell due. Somebody's very punctual with my money."
7. `scene`, Brackwater, refusing to burn the ledger: "Burn it and he owes nobody. I don't write off a debt that size."
8. `card`, The Counting House: "The canal house kept two books, one for the guild and one for whoever paid the lock-keeper. Ottilie wants the second."

Sample 5 is the joke doing its job: it dodges the question, and the dodge is the answer.

**Average test.** Stock: "Gold means nothing to me now. I fight for all of you." Ours: "I still want my money. I just want it from him now."
