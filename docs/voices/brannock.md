# Brannock, the cadet

**Rule 1: stands.** Brannock may name a feeling once, as a scene's turn, and the beat sheet names it in advance (below).

**Who.** Aldmere; a parish lay-brother, thirty, broad and quiet, a cadet, and the best cook in the company. Met at the raid (map 6). His parish was burned by raiders paid in keep silver, and nobody came. Wants the name of whoever paid. Won't say that he wants a name more than he wants the truth (his flaw), or that he rang the bell for an hour and nobody came.

**Image source** (rule 5). The parish's working rooms: the kitchen (the pot, salt, the bread oven, who gets fed first), the bell and its rope, the alms door, a roof he patched. Never: the rite, the altar or the sickbed (Maud's; he served a church, but from its kitchen). Never the farm year (Teodor's). Never counts or measures (Wren's; the name test between the two cadets is shape first, and images second).

**Shape.** Few words, low and unhurried; the opposite of Wren in length. He answers a question with a question about who ("Who said?", "Whose men?"), so a cold reader knows him by the counter (rule 4), and his questions want answers: he waits for them. When he isn't asking, he is handing someone food, which is how he dodges. Gets longer only about a meal.

**Wit:** dry and rare, about his size and his cooking. Never about the parish.

**Names** (rule 8). He asks names, of the enemy dead, of a raider's captain, and keeps every one. He says a companion's name when he serves them first, which is how he apologises. After the Grange he says Hask's name every time, the one name he spends freely, and that is the flaw in his grammar: at last he has someone to blame.

**The one named feeling:** proposed for the camp after the Grange (map 7), when the silver is Hask's. Glad: he has a name, and he hears himself say he's glad. The beat sheet confirms it.

**Never.**
- "vengeance", "justice", "they'll pay"
- "for my parish", "my people" (the parish comes up as the bell, the pot, the roof)
- a prayer as a line of dialogue (the rite is Maud's)
- **Most at risk:** the closing question. WRITING's tic list bans a rhetorical one; his must be real, put to someone who could answer. If nobody in the room could, cut it.
- **Second risk:** the gentle big cook. Food is the house's own warmth (who gives up the bed); once a scene, or he turns into a mascot.

**Samples** (illustrative; they don't ship and carry no ids).
1. `bark`, a kill, to the dead: "Who paid you?"
2. `bark`, struck, to nobody: "Hard to miss me. Fair."
3. `support`, to Teodor, who has shown him a pike grip: "Who taught you that? It wasn't the levy. They taught me nothing."
4. `support`, to Rook, who has checked her bowl twice: "I'll eat from it first if you like. Then you'll know who to blame."
5. `support`, to Ansgar, who flinches at the cookfire: "Sit this side. Smoke goes the other way tonight."
6. `support`, to Alder: "You knew the silver was the keep's. Since when?"
7. `scene`, the camp after the Grange, to nobody in particular: "Hask. I'll say it again. Hask."
8. `card`, his meeting, the raid's camp: "A lay-brother in a scorched habit had the wounded fed before anyone asked. He wants to know where the raiders' pay came from, and he doesn't sit down until someone tells him."

Sample 6 is the counter doing its job: Alder can answer with a tile, and Brannock will wait.

**Average test.** Stock: "They'll pay for what they did to my people!" Ours: "I rang the bell for an hour. I want to know who heard it."
