# Brannock, the cadet

**Rule 1: stands.** Brannock may name a feeling once, as a scene's turn, and the beat sheet names it in advance (below).

**Who.** Aldmere; thirty, broad and quiet, a cadet, the best cook in the company, and a parish lay-brother for the eight years before the parish burned. Before that (STORY draft 7) he was a hired fist for the river-road boat-guilds, breaking strikes and heads for pay, good at it and getting worse, until he was broken himself in a brawl on the Harrow quay and left on a parish's alms step. The parish fed him and put him in its kitchen because it needed a cook and he needed somewhere to put his hands, and he put the violence down the way you put down a tool. Met at the raid (map 6). His parish was burned by raiders paid in keep silver, he rang the bell for an hour, and nobody came. Wants the name of whoever paid. Won't say that he wants a name more than he wants the truth (his flaw), because a name is permission to be what he used to be. Won't say what he used to be at all. Nobody in the company knows except Hask, who sees it on sight at the Grange.

**Image source** (rule 5). The parish's working rooms: the kitchen (the pot, salt, the bread oven, who gets fed first), the bell and its rope, the alms door, a roof he patched. The old trade has no images, on purpose: he has no words for the quay and uses none, and a cold reader finds the gap where a fighter's talk should be. Never: the rite, the altar or the sickbed (Maud's; he served a church, but from its kitchen). Never the farm year (Teodor's). Never counts or measures (Wren's; the name test between the two cadets is shape first, and images second). Never a roof as weather falls on it (Ivo's, if he joins); Brannock's roof is slates he nailed.

**Shape.** Few words, low and unhurried; the opposite of Wren in length. He answers a question with a question about who ("Who said?", "Whose men?"), so a cold reader knows him by the counter (rule 4), and his questions want answers: he waits for them. When he isn't asking, he is handing someone food, which is how he dodges. Gets longer only about a meal. **The body under the voice:** he stands where a fighter stands, between the speaker and the door, and a stage line may say so once a scene; his own lines never do.

**Wit:** dry and rare, about his size and his cooking. Never about the parish, and never about the quay.

**Names** (rule 8). He asks names, of the enemy dead, of a raider's captain, and keeps every one. He says a companion's name when he serves them first, which is how he apologises. After the Grange he says Hask's name every time, the one name he spends freely, and that is the flaw in his grammar: at last he has someone to blame.

**The one named feeling:** proposed for the camp after the Grange (map 7), when the silver is Hask's and Hask has looked at him and known him. Glad: he has a name, and he hears himself say he's glad. The beat sheet confirms it.

**Proposed (Lotus, 2026-10-06): the earth school.** If Lotus makes Brannock the earth caster, the parish's old lay-brother taught him the wall-wards along with the bread, and his one spell is to stand something in front of someone else. Nothing about the voice changes. The wards come out as kitchen logistics ("Stand there. That one's mine to catch."), never as magic talk; he says "ward" as a verb and never says "spell".

**Never.**
- "vengeance", "justice", "they'll pay"
- "for my parish", "my people" (the parish comes up as the bell, the pot, the roof)
- a prayer as a line of dialogue (the rite is Maud's)
- the quay, the guilds, a fight he was paid for, in any line of his; a fighter's advice ("keep your guard up") is cut on sight, because the one time the old trade shows is Hask's to notice, not Brannock's to say
- **Most at risk:** the closing question. WRITING's tic list bans a rhetorical one; his must be real, put to someone who could answer. If nobody in the room could, cut it.
- **Second risk:** the gentle big cook. Food is the house's own warmth (who gives up the bed); once a scene, or he turns into a mascot.

**Samples** (illustrative; they don't ship and carry no ids).
1. `bark`, a kill, to the dead: "Who paid you?"
2. `bark`, struck, to nobody: "Hard to miss me. Fair."
3. `support`, to Teodor, who has shown him a pike grip: "Who taught you that? It wasn't the levy. They taught me nothing."
4. `support`, to Rook, who has checked her bowl twice: "I'll eat from it first if you like. Then you'll know who to blame."
5. `support`, to Ansgar, who flinches at the cookfire: "Sit this side. Smoke goes the other way tonight."
6. `support`, to Alder: "You knew the silver was the keep's. Since when?"
7. `scene`, the Grange, after Hask has looked him over and said "You've done this before.", to Hask: "Who told you that?"
8. `scene`, the camp after the Grange, to nobody in particular: "Hask. I'll say it again. Hask."
9. `card`, his meeting, the raid's camp: "A lay-brother in a scorched habit had the wounded fed before anyone asked. He wants to know where the raiders' pay came from, and he doesn't sit down until someone tells him."

Sample 6 is the counter doing its job: Alder can answer with a tile, and Brannock will wait. Sample 7 is the counter failing: nobody told Hask, Hask saw it, and a question with no one to answer it is the quay showing through once.

**Average test.** Stock: "They'll pay for what they did to my people!" Ours: "I rang the bell for an hour. I want to know who heard it."
