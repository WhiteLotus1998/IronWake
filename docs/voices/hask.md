# Hask, the Iron Warden

**Rule 1: stands, and his break is spent on someone else.** Hask never names a feeling of his own; the last line (Lotus's) is where he would have spent it, and he spends it on thanks instead.

**Who.** The seam; thirty years commander of the garrison, never beaten, in his sixties and dying of an old wound. Raised Alder in everything but the word. Wants the prison broken and all three regions sworn, which would end the wars, and he is right that it would. Won't say that the Kin promised he'd live to see it, or that he planned Alder's death on the raid's wall and called it the cleanest one he could find, until the Grange, where he says the second without flinching. The wound is a Sallow pike through him at Saltmarsh Ford twenty years ago; his first sergeant, Teodor's grandfather, carried him off the ford to the lazar house at Sorrel Bend, and got the garrison's oldest lance for it (STORY draft 7). He has paid that house's meal bins for eight years and never signed the book. His plan is the rite's three parts broken one by one: burn the Word, move the Stones, starve the Count. He is the only man alive who can recite all three, and he does, once, at the Grange.

**Image source** (rule 5). The long command: the muster and the roll call, rations and who's short, a march on a wet road, the order of battle, the dead counted by village, the armistice as a schedule he can recite, the rite's three parts in the order the hill does them, the songs about him that don't agree. Horses and tack are shared with Alder on purpose: he taught them (STORY, the girth on map 1), so the overlap is inheritance, and it is the only image he and Alder share. Never: ledgers or prices (Ottilie's; he counts men and meals, not money, and pays tolls back without looking at the coin). Never the survey or a count of things for its own sake (Wren's).

**Shape.** The most patient speaker in the game. Long sentences, plain words, every clause concrete, then a short order. **He predicts:** he speaks about other people's next moves in the future tense, flatly, and is right ("They'll burn the letters inside a week."). He is the one character who answers questions straight and fully, which is its own dodge: he answers so honestly that the listener runs out of objections before reaching the one that matters (rule 4, through candour).

**His own voice and the shard's.** A cold reader must be able to tell which sentences are Hask and which the Kin puts in his mouth.
- **Hask's:** "I", the past tense, numbers of men and years, orders, places by name, and the dead. He is dry and he is specific.
- **The shard's:** "we" and "you'll be", the future made soft, warmth as a promise (warm, safe, fed, kept, no more), and the listener's feeling named for them. Never more than one clause of it in a Hask line, until the keep.
- Both Hask and the Kin spend names freely, so a name never marks the shard in his mouth; the split runs on "we", "you'll be" and the warm words alone.
- The leak gets wider map by map: none on map 1, a word at the Tollgate, a clause at the Grange, and on the keep half his sentences finish in the Kin's grammar. When he falls the leak goes out first (STORY), so his last line is entirely his own.

**Wit:** yes, dry, rationed. About the regions, their treaties and the songs about him. Never about the dead, the oath, or Alder.

**Names** (rule 8). "Alder" freely to Alder, as he always has; it is the one name he never rations, which is how a cold reader knows he raised them. Everyone else by trade or region ("the Sallow bowman", "your chaplain") until they've cost him something. **"Son" or "daughter" is never said until the last line.** He has called Alder by name for twenty years so that the word, when it comes, is new.

**The one named feeling:** none. The last line is the turn, and it names a cost and a thanks, not a feeling.

**The last line** (Lotus's, quoted, never rewritten; `human:` locked when stamped): *"It said we would be safe. It said the world would be at peace... but I didn't stop to think what it would cost. Thank you for stopping me... son."* ("daughter" for a female captain, #648). Its "we" and "safe" are the Kin's words, quoted now by a man who can hear them as someone else's. It is WRITING's one rule-9 exception. **No other Hask line closes on theme.** Every other scene of his ends on an act (the girth, the paid toll, the hands on Alder's face).

**Never.**
- "destiny", "glory", "my child", "trust me", "you'll understand someday"
- "mercy" (he believes it and won't say it; the shard says "kind" for him)
- raising his voice; he has never needed to
- a threat to anyone in the company; he describes what will happen, and that is worse
- **Most at risk:** the villain monologue. The Grange is long; break it with the captain's refusals and his own short orders, and let him stop before he's finished.
- **Second risk:** wisdom. A man who predicts everything wants to say profound things about it; he says the next thing that will happen and who'll do it.

**Samples** (illustrative; they don't ship and carry no ids).
1. `scene`, Starting Alone, checking the girth, to Alder: "Loose. It always was on this one. Two fingers, not three."
2. `scene`, the Tollgate, seeing the lance, to Teodor: "Your grandfather carried that eleven years. He never once cleaned it right."
3. `scene`, the raid's end, holding Alder's face (pinned): "You held it."
4. `scene`, the Grange, his own voice, to Alder (pinned): "I gave you the cleanest death I could find. On a wall, before you had to see what comes after."
5. `scene`, the Grange, the shard showing, to Alder: "Ten names. They'll be warm, Alder, every one. You'll never bury one of them."
6. `scene`, the Grange, predicting, to the company: "Sallow will blame Aldmere by the thaw. Kestrow will send no one. Write it down."
7. `bark`, the keep, he takes the field, to his sworn: "Second rank, forward. We'll be home by dark."
8. `scene`, the Grange, dry, to Pell: "There are four songs about me in Aldmere. Two of them have me hanged."

Sample 5 against sample 4 is the split the sheet exists for: the same man, the same scene, the second line finishing in the Kin's grammar. Sample 7's "we" is both, which on the keep is the point.

**Average test.** Stock: "Join me, and we will build a world without war!" Ours: "I fought for all three of them. Every time, the one I saved sold out the other two within a year."
