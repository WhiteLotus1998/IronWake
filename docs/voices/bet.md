# Bet Lowry, the hire with opinions about gates

**Rule 1: stands.** Bet may name a feeling once, at her Postern event (0146), and the beat sheet names it in advance (below). Before the Postern she names none, the way the other hires name none.

**Who.** The seam's own, which nobody knows; fifties, a pikeman from the barracks. In fact the old garrison's gate sergeant for twenty years, sworn to the Kin with the rest in Hask's first winter, and it didn't take. She is also the one who found the foundling on the step and handed them to Hask. She took the barracks' coin to see the keep again. Wants to see it. Won't say any of it, to anyone, until the Postern, and never hints (0146's rule).

**The hard test.** Before the Postern her lines must pass the name test *among the other hires* (Corin, Ines, Mattias): one dry trade, one dry opinion, nothing that marks her. Standing out is easy; this sheet is about not standing out, and about leaving absences a cold reader finds afterwards.

**Image source** (rule 5). The gate: the bar and its brackets, hinges that need fat, the night knock, the wicket, who comes in and who goes out, the rota, a password changed. "A town gate", as far as anyone knows. Never: the keep's rooms, its stairs, or anything about Ironwake itself before the Postern. Never horses and tack (Alder's and Hask's; garrison images a cold reader would match to them). Never the practice yard (Marrit's).

**The absences** (what a second reader finds).
- **The cold.** She never mentions it. When the company complains (Keziah, constantly), she says nothing and changes the subject to the work. She is never shown cold. After the Postern this reads as home.
- **The oath.** She never says "I swear", "on my oath" or "I promise", even idiomatically, in any line, ever. After the Postern this reads as the one oath she already meant.
- **The captain.** She watches Alder and says nothing about them; no line of hers before the Postern addresses Alder's past, and she's the only hire who never asks where the captain is from.

**Shape.** Dry and plain, a sergeant's economy. Opinions as statements of fact, about gates, doors, watches, and people who think they're getting in. Asked about herself, she answers about the door she's standing next to (rule 4). After the Postern she is exactly as terse; the change is that her lines now have a past, not that she talks more.

**Wit:** yes, the gate kind: about people who knock wrong, latches, and the gap under any door. Never about the oath or Hask.

**Names** (rule 8). Before the Postern, people are what they'd be at a gate: "you with the bow", "the tall one", "the chaplain". At the Postern, "Alder", once, the first time she has ever said it to them.

**The one named feeling:** proposed for the Postern, to Alder. Proud: the same word the Toll Axe boss uses for Hask on map 3 ("He's proud of you."), now said by the person who meant it first. The beat sheet confirms it.

**Never** (before the Postern).
- "swear", "oath", "promise" in any form, in any line of hers, before or after (her ending card is narration, and its "swore" is the payoff)
- the cold, the keep, the seam, Hask, garrison, ward
- a second sentence about her past; one dry line is the hire's whole biography
- **Most at risk:** the wink. One clever line that a first reader catches spoils the Postern. Every pre-Postern line is checked against Corin's, Ines's and Mattias's menu lines with names hidden, and if a reader picks hers out, it goes.
- **Second risk:** after the Postern, the confession. She explains nothing; the event gives the facts, and her lines stay about the gate.

**Samples** (illustrative; they don't ship and carry no ids).
1. `card`, the barracks menu (shipped, kept): "Twenty years on a town gate. Has opinions about gates."
2. `bark`, braces on a wait, to nobody: "Let them knock."
3. `bark`, a door or gate opened on the map, to nobody: "Hinge wants fat. Somebody should tell them."
4. `camp bark`, to Keziah, who is complaining about the cold again: "Stand inside the arch. Wind can't turn corners. Now hold this."
5. `camp bark`, to Ottilie, who has priced her pike: "It's a bar with a point. Gates don't care what it cost."
6. `scene`, the Postern, at the gate she kept, to Alder: "You were on that step. Wrapped in a grain sack. You didn't cry once, until I handed you over."
7. `scene`, the Postern, to Alder, the booked line: "Twenty years, and you're the one I'd let in again. I was proud of that."
8. `card`, her ending (shipped, kept): "Bet Lowry swore first, the one the oath could not hold, choosing it."

Sample 4 is the absence working: she answers the cold with a fact about walls and a job, and it reads as a sergeant now and as a native later.

**Average test.** Stock, at the Postern: "I have a secret I've kept for twenty years, Captain. I was there the night you were born." Ours: "Grain sack. Third step. I barred the gate after, same as any night."
