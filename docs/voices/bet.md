# Bet Lowry, the hire who still counts heads at supper

**Rule 1: stands.** Bet may name a feeling once, at her Cold Kitchen event (0146, 0247), and the beat sheet names it in advance (below). Before the Cold Kitchen she names none, the way the other hires name none.

**Who.** The seam's own, which nobody knows; fifties, a pikeman from the barracks. In fact the old garrison's cook for twenty years, sworn to the Kin with the rest in Hask's first winter, and it didn't take. She is also the one who found the foundling against the outside of the bake-oven wall, under the alder that is the keep yard's one tree, and handed them to Hask. She took the barracks' coin to see the keep again. Wants to see it. Won't say any of it, to anyone, until the Cold Kitchen, and never hints (0146's rule).

**Why it didn't take** (partners only, never in game text; round 366). The Kin's oath feels like warmth. She lit the garrison's fire at four every morning for twenty years and knows exactly what warmth costs. A god offering it for nothing had nothing to sell her.

**The hard test.** Before the Cold Kitchen her lines must pass the name test *among the other hires* (Corin, Ines, Mattias): one dry trade, one dry opinion, nothing that marks her. Standing out is easy; this sheet is about not standing out, and about leaving absences a cold reader finds afterwards.

**Image source** (rule 5). The work: salt, the pot, the oven, a fire that won't draw, wood that's wrong for it, the supper count, who's missing at the count. "A garrison", as far as anyone knows; it names no keep. Never: the keep's rooms, its stairs, or anything about Ironwake itself before the Cold Kitchen. Never horses and tack (Alder's and Hask's; garrison images a cold reader would match to them). Never the practice yard (Marrit's). Never gates or doors as her trade (Lotus, round 366: a near-miss).

**The absences** (what a second reader finds).
- **The cold.** She never mentions it. When the company complains (Keziah, constantly), she says nothing and changes the subject to the work. She is never shown cold. After the Cold Kitchen this reads as home.
- **The oath.** She never says "I swear", "on my oath" or "I promise", even idiomatically, in any line, ever. After the Cold Kitchen this reads as the one oath she already meant.
- **The captain.** She watches Alder and says nothing about them; no line of hers before the Cold Kitchen addresses Alder's past, and she's the only hire who never asks where the captain is from.

**Shape.** Dry and plain, a mess sergeant's economy. Opinions as statements of fact, about fires, pots, rations and people who think they know how to bank a fire. Asked about herself, she answers about the work in front of her (rule 4). After the Cold Kitchen she is exactly as terse; the change is that her lines now have a past, not that she talks more.

**Wit:** yes, the kitchen kind: about wet wood, a fire that won't draw, and people who stir a pot they didn't start. Never about the oath or Hask.

**Names** (rule 8). Before the Cold Kitchen, people are what they'd be at a supper count: "you with the bow", "the tall one", "the chaplain". At the Cold Kitchen, "Alder", once, the first time she has ever said it to them.

**The one named feeling:** proposed for the Cold Kitchen, to Alder. Proud: the same word the Toll Axe boss uses for Hask on map 3 ("He's proud of you."), now said by the person who meant it first. The beat sheet confirms it.

**Never** (before the Cold Kitchen).
- "swear", "oath", "promise" in any form, in any line of hers, before or after (her ending card is narration, and its "swore" is the payoff)
- "fed" or "hungry", in any form, ever: that vocabulary is Kinsbane's (round 366), and the bark rules keep it away from puns
- the cold, the keep, the seam, Hask, garrison, ward (her menu line's "a garrison" is the one exception, and it names none)
- a second sentence about her past; one dry line is the hire's whole biography
- **Most at risk:** the wink. One clever line that a first reader catches spoils the Cold Kitchen. Every pre-Kitchen line is checked against Corin's, Ines's and Mattias's menu lines with names hidden, and if a reader picks hers out, it goes.
- **Second risk:** after the Cold Kitchen, the confession. She explains nothing; the event gives the facts, and her lines stay about the work.

**Samples** (illustrative; they don't ship and carry no ids). Lines 3, 5, 6 and the average test are Chat's offers in round 366.
1. `card`, the barracks menu (shipped): "Cooked for a garrison twenty years. Still counts heads at supper."
2. `bark`, braces on a wait (Banked), to nobody: "Let it burn low. It'll hold till morning."
3. `bark`, at a campfire, to nobody: "That won't draw. Wrong wood."
4. `camp bark`, to Keziah, who is complaining about the cold again: "Stand on the lee side. Smoke goes the other way. Now hold this."
5. `camp bark`, to Ottilie, who has priced her pike: "It's a bar with a point. Gets the same as the pot: cleaned and put back."
6. `scene`, the Cold Kitchen, at the oven she kept, to Alder: "You were against the oven wall. Wrapped in a grain sack. You didn't cry once, until I handed you over."
7. `scene`, the Cold Kitchen, to Alder, the booked line: "Twenty years of suppers, and you're the one I'd count twice. I was proud of that."
8. `card`, her ending (shipped, kept): "Bet Lowry swore first, the one the oath could not hold, choosing it."

Sample 4 is the absence working: she answers the cold with a fact about the fire and a job, and it reads as a cook now and as a native later.

**Average test.** Stock, at the Cold Kitchen: "I have a secret I've kept for twenty years, Captain. I was there the night you were born." Ours: "Grain sack. Back of the oven. I banked the fire after, same as any night."
