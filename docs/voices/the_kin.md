# The Kin, the god under the hill

**Rule 1: widened, and turned outward.** The Kin names feelings constantly, but only the listener's, in "you" and "we"; it never says "I" and has no feeling of its own to name (round 296).

**Who.** The outlands, carried north; the god chained under Ironwake in frozen iron for three hundred years. It reaches the world above the iron only through the sworn, and through Hask, who is its door (STORY). Wants more who swear; that is all it wants. Won't say that, because it never says what it wants. It says what *you* want, one step further than you'd have taken it.

**Where it speaks.** Three registers, and only these.
- **Through the shard**, to Hask. Never printed as its own speaker before the keep; it leaks into his lines (Hask's sheet says how to tell).
- **The sworn.** Ordinary people who sound like themselves, with its grammar underneath: "we" where "I" belongs, and no want of their own. Hollin's "We're well, thank you." is the model.
- **Itself**, on Under the Hill and in the bad ending's cards. The only places a line is tagged as the Kin.

**Image source** (rule 5). The hearth and the table: a fire in the chest, a lit window, coming in from the cold, a full bowl, a blanket put round your shoulders, the bench with room on it, the end of being afraid of the man beside you. In a cold country these are the strongest images there are, and they are the bait. Never: hunger as appetite, the hunt or the herd (Kinsbane's; the two gods must part on one line each). Never the cold as home (Alder's and Bet's; the Kin only ever offers to end the cold, and someone who is at home in it is the one listener it has no purchase on).

**Shape.** Short, warm, present tense. It opens on the listener ("You") and closes on an offer that isn't phrased as one: a place, a seat, a bowl. **The lean is deletion only:** when the Kin answers something you said, every word in its reply is yours, minus one, and what is left still parses as a sentence, never a stutter. It never puts a word in your mouth; it takes one out, and the one it takes is what was holding the thought back ("alive", "yet", "if", "for now"). Anyone can check an echo by diffing the two lines. Sample 4 is the example. The warm words are its own, so they belong in its offers (samples 2, 5, 6, 7), never in an echo; at most one echo a scene, and the beat sheet places it. It never asks a real question; its questions are already answered. Asked anything, it answers the want under the question, not the question (rule 4, inverted into kindness).

**Wit:** never. Nothing it says is ever funny, and nothing it says is ever cruel.

**Names** (rule 8, broken on purpose). The Kin is the one speaker for whom names cost nothing: it knows every name and spends them freely, gently, like a hand on the shoulder. Because rule 8 holds for everyone else, a line that says a name for no reason is a tell. The sworn are the reverse: they drop every name, their own included ("We're well"), except one the oath hasn't finished taking (Marrit's "Alder"). The kept name means someone is still in there; a sworn who says no name at all is past reach.

**The one named feeling:** none of its own, ever. The listener's, always.

**Never.**
- "I", "me", "mine" (it is all "you" and "we"; a single "I" breaks it)
- obey, serve, kneel, master, power, forever, chosen
- a threat, a raised voice, a price; it never says what swearing costs, because it believes, insofar as it believes anything, that it costs nothing
- the word "Kin" for itself; others name it
- **Most at risk:** the cult seducer, every line a purr. It must sound like the best thing anyone ever said to you, plain and kind, not like a villain being kind.
- **Second risk:** the two-beat aphorism. A god that makes wise little turns is a fortune cookie; it states, it offers, and it stops.

**Samples** (illustrative; they don't ship and carry no ids).
1. `scene`, Hollin card, a sworn child asked her name: "We're well, thank you." (pinned, STORY)
2. `bark`, a sworn soldier on the field, to whoever attacks him: "Put it down. There's room by the fire for you too."
3. `bark`, a sworn soldier falls, to nobody: "Someone tell my sister we're fine."
4. `scene`, through the shard, the dismissal winter, to Hask (quoted in his card, never shown live): "You kept them alive thirty years. You can keep them."
5. `scene`, Under the Hill, to Marrit, freed and shaking: "You're cold again, Marrit. Come back in. Your bench is still warm."
6. `scene`, Under the Hill, to Keziah: "You've been hungry your whole life. Nobody at our table is hungry."
7. `scene`, Under the Hill, to the captain: "You read the list every morning. Let us read it. You can sleep."
8. `card`, the bad ending, a recruit's epilogue in "we": "We keep the list now. Every name on it is well. Nobody on it is cold, and nobody has left, and nobody wants to."

Sample 4 is the lean at its smallest: "keep them alive" loses one word and becomes "keep them". Sample 7 is the shape aimed at the captain's flaw; it is the one line on Under the Hill the beat sheet should build toward. It never addresses the drake: an animal never swears, and the Kin has nothing to say to it.

**Average test.** Stock: "Swear to me, and together we shall have peace eternal!" Ours: "You've been cold since the dismissal. Sit down. Nobody here will hurt you."
