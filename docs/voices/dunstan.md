# Dunstan, the bulwark

**Rule 1: stands.** Dunstan may name a feeling once, as a scene's turn, and the beat sheet names it in advance; this sheet books none (below).

**Who.** Kestrow; a shield sergeant, forty, the oldest in the company, a bulwark, met on Brackwater Cut (map 8). His lords ordered him to hold a bridge while they fled; he held it two days against Hask's sworn and walked away from an army that no longer exists. Wants a wall worth standing on. Won't say anything about the bridge, ever: other people tell it.

**Image source** (rule 5). Kestrow's stone and height: footing, a wall's courses, the keystone, what holds weight and what doesn't, ice on a step. The shield as a tool: its rim, its strap, a split board. Never: weather or the sky (Rook's; the three Kestrow voices split stone, sky and sickbed). Never the sickbed or the rite (Maud's). Never the bridge, not even as an image.

**Shape.** Short imperatives addressed to feet and shields ("Feet." "Rim up."), and plain statements about weight. He answers a question about himself by correcting the asker's stance: asked how he is, he moves your back foot (rule 4). That is his tell beside Alder, who answers with where the watch stands: Alder places the company, Dunstan fixes one body.

**Wit:** yes, about his age and his knees. Never about anyone who ran, and never about his lords.

**Names** (rule 8). Everyone is their kit until he has stood beside them through an enemy phase ("shield", "the bow"). After that, their name, and he keeps it. His lords are never named, and neither is the river.

**The one named feeling:** none booked. The bridge is where it would go, and he doesn't go there. A beat sheet that wants one argues it on the Table first.

**Never.**
- "hold the line", "duty", "orders are orders"
- "back in my day", "I've seen", the old soldier's story
- the bridge, the river, his lords' names
- **Most at risk:** the veteran's proverb. Like Teodor's "Grandad always said", it is one line in the campaign, not a habit; the rest of his wisdom goes through somebody's feet.
- **Second risk:** the knees joke. Once a scene at most, and never as a closer.

**Samples** (illustrative; they don't ship and carry no ids).
1. `bark`, struck and standing, to nobody: "Rim took it. Next."
2. `bark`, waiting on a tile, to nobody: "Knees say this tile. Knees are usually right."
3. `support`, to Rook, who keeps her back to a wall: "Wall's good. Step off it a pace, so you've somewhere to go."
4. `support`, to Maud, after "Hold still.": "I've done that. Two days, once. Go on."
5. `support`, to Teodor, planting his pike: "That's a farm grip. Keep it. It's better than ours were."
6. `support`, to Alder, after "I'll hold the gap.": "You'll stand behind me and tell me where. That's your work. The gap's mine."
7. `scene`, Brackwater's camp, to Wren, who is counting the dark: "Feet wider. You're on ice. Count that."
8. `card`, his meeting, Brackwater Cut: "A Kestrow sergeant with a shield split to the strap and no army behind him. He asks which wall is yours, and where you want him on it."

Sample 4 is as near the bridge as he comes: Maud knows, and he lets her.

**Average test.** Stock: "I've seen a hundred battles, lad. Stand firm and you'll live to see another." Ours: "Feet wider. You're on ice."
