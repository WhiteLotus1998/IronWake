# Marrit, the first sworn

**Rule 1: stands, in two voices.** Sworn, she can't name her own feeling, because the Kin names it for her; freed, she may name one, once (below).

**Who.** The seam; a garrison ward, a few years older than Alder, who taught Alder to hold the sword Hask gave them. Alder was told she died in the dismissal winter. She was nineteen when Hask asked for a first, and said yes before he finished the sentence, because she loved him and because somebody had to go first so the others would not be afraid (STORY draft 7). She is Hask's first sworn and his champion, eight years under the oath, close to a husk. Leads the hunt on Brackwater Cut. Freed only when Hask falls (#750), or killed first. Wants, sworn: what the Kin wants, and she calls it hers. Wants, freed: nothing yet; wanting is one of the things she has to relearn. Won't say, in either voice, what she did in eight years under the oath.

**Image source** (rule 5). The practice yard: grip, stance, the elbow in, the guard dropped, footwork in frozen mud, the wooden sword before the real one, the count a teacher keeps for a pupil. The ward's chores: wood stacked, water hauled, the barracks stove. Sworn, the yard images come out warm ("you've got the grip now; let us carry it"). Freed, they come back as the body only: hands, weight, cold, a blade that is heavy again. Never: tack and horses (Alder's and Hask's; she taught the sword, not the riding). Never the gate (Bet's; they served the same keep, and the name test between them is the hardest one this sheet has).

**Shape, sworn.** Slow and even. **Her sentences start in "I" and finish in "we"**: the lean is visible in her grammar, a clause at a time, and she sometimes catches it and fails to stop it. She answers Alder kindly and at once, and the answer is always the Kin's ("I want to, Alder. I can't."). She never raises her voice and never hurries, even hunting.

**Shape, freed.** Cold and slow (STORY), and slower than sworn: short, present-tense statements about what she can feel, one at a time, with silences the scene must leave room for. **She does not snap back.** The teacher's voice is gone and doesn't return in this game; she doesn't joke, doesn't correct Alder's grip, and doesn't remember things on cue. She says "I" carefully, like a word in a language she used to speak.

**Wit:** before the oath, yes; Alder remembers it, and a support may quote it. Sworn, never. Freed, never in this game. One line in her epilogue card may be dry, if the beat sheet earns it.

**Names** (rule 8). "Alder", twice in the whole campaign, both pinned: "I want to, Alder. I can't." on the Cut, and, on Under the Hill only, "It's awake, Alder." Nobody else's name, sworn or free. The Kin uses hers freely (its sheet); she never says it herself. "Alder" is the one name the oath hasn't finished taking, the same crack as the "we" she catches and can't stop (the Kin's sheet: a sworn who says no name at all is past reach).

**The one named feeling:** proposed for the camp after the keep, freed, to Alder. Fear, named as proof: the Kin's promise was the end of being afraid of the one beside you, and the first thing she feels free is afraid of Alder, and she says so. The fear is named and never explained; it pays off the title drop's "the end of being afraid of the man beside you" (STORY), and none of the three drafts adds the explanation. The beat sheet confirms it, and it's one of WRITING's three-draft scenes.

**Never.**
- sworn: "please", "help me", "fight it", any line where she resists out loud; resistance shows only in the grammar
- freed: "I'm back", "I remember everything", "thank you" (the last line's thanks is Hask's), "I'm sorry" as a closer
- either: the Kin by name, the oath by name, Hask's name
- **Most at risk:** the snap-back. A freed Marrit who sounds like the teacher Alder remembers undoes the oath's whole cost; she must sound like someone learning to stand.
- **Second risk:** the possessed-villain register when sworn. She is gentle and reasonable, and that is the horror; a sworn line that menaces is cut.

**Samples** (illustrative; they don't ship and carry no ids).
1. `scene`, Brackwater Cut, sworn, to Alder (pinned): "I want to, Alder. I can't."
2. `bark`, the Cut, sworn, she strikes Alder: "Elbow in. I taught you that. We'll be quick."
3. `bark`, the Cut, sworn, a recruit falls to her: "I'm sorry. We needed that. He's warm now."
4. `bark`, the keep, sworn, at Hask's side: "I'll take the left. We're ready."
5. `scene`, the keep, freed, she drops the sword and sits (pinned): "It's cold. I'd forgotten it was cold."
6. `scene`, Under the Hill, freed, the second half (pinned): "It's awake, Alder. I can hear it looking for me."
7. `support`, freed, to Alder, who offered her their coat: "Keep it. I want to know how long I can stand this."
8. `card`, her epilogue if she lived: "Marrit went back to the yard and stood in the cold every morning until she could hold a wooden sword again. She took no pupils."

Sample 3 is the sworn shape at its worst: an apology that is hers, then the Kin's "we" and "warm" closing it. Sample 7 is the freed shape: she refuses comfort because the cold is the proof, and she is the one person in the game for whom the cold is neither home nor hardship but evidence.

**Average test.** Stock, freed: "Alder... I'm free. I remember everything. Can you ever forgive me?" Ours: "My hands are cold. Both of them. I'd like to keep them that way a while."
