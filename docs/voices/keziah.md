# Keziah, the reaver

**Rule 1: stands.** Keziah may name a feeling once, as a scene's turn, and the beat sheet names it in advance (below). Hunger is never her feeling; it is Kinsbane's, and she talks about it the way you talk about a dog in the room.

**Who.** The outlands; a shrine-keeper's daughter, early twenties, a reaver carrying the scythe Kinsbane. Her shrine was burned by all three regions in one generation. Angry, generous, superstitious, and right more often than anyone credits. Wants the outlands' seat to mean something, and wants to know what to do with a god in a blade. Won't say that the hunger is the only thing that has ever wanted her, and that she isn't sure she minds (her flaw).

**Image source** (rule 5). Two sources, and they argue. **The hunt:** spoor, the waterhole, the dry months, the kill shared out by rank, the night when things move. **The shrine:** the keeper's broom, the offering bowl, smoke, the ash of the burned beams, the small rules a keeper's child keeps (don't count the dead aloud, step over a threshold left foot first). Her country is heat, dust and night, so she complains about the cold, often, as a fact about her body, never as a metaphor. Never: the cold as home (Alder's and Bet's). Never the pen or the herd as contempt (Kinsbane's; she hunts for food, it hunts for hate). Never height, sky or weather-reading (Rook's; the claimants part on images first).

**Shape.** Quick and hot. Interrupts, overrides, swears mildly, and then does the generous thing in the same line. **Generous with what she needs most:** she gives away food, and she gives away her blanket while complaining about the cold, and those two lines should sit next to each other. Asked a question, she answers the question under it, often rudely, and is right (rule 4). Superstitions come out as practical instructions, never as mysticism.

**How she answers Kinsbane.** Its lines are to her alone; she never repeats them to anyone. She answers it out loud and sideways, as you'd talk to a hound you are fond of and a little afraid of: short, teasing, occasionally an order it ignores. She never says its name in the company's hearing. At the Oath Stone (`KeziahOath`, 0225) she answers it with an act: `fed`, `spared` or `refused`, and the beat sheet writes one line per outcome; she never explains the choice to anyone.

**Third register: passed, returned in Hask's ranks** (map 9). She took his offer and he gave her a seat by the fire, the first warm one she's had since the outlands. She is defensive, a little ashamed, not sworn, and talkable. The cold complaint turns into the argument: he kept her warm. The talk that wins her back is Rook's (the picked claimant's), and Keziah answers Rook's suspicion with her own.

**Wit:** yes, the most of anyone after Ottilie (rule 7). About the cold, the north's food and the north's gods. Never about her shrine, and never about the scythe's hunger to its face.

**Names** (rule 8). She says names readily to people she's fed, and calls everyone else by what they're wearing or carrying ("you with the book"). Kinsbane is "it", "the old one", or nothing. Hask is "the old man", which is meant kindly and becomes worse.

**The one named feeling:** proposed for the Oath Stone, on whichever side of the hunger she lands, to the captain. Wanted: that the hunger is the first thing that ever wanted her. The beat sheet confirms it.

**Never.**
- "blood", "my destiny", "the spirits", "the gods will" (no mystic register; a keeper's child is practical about gods)
- naming Kinsbane in company, or repeating what it said
- the cold as anything but a complaint before the keep
- **Most at risk:** the angry outsider, every line a grievance. The anger is real and rationed; the generosity is the bigger half of her.
- **Second risk:** the superstition as a tic. One small rule a scene, at most, and it has to change what she does.

**Samples** (illustrative; they don't ship and carry no ids).
1. `card`, the choice screen (`pitch`), to the captain: "Everyone here is offering you a promise. Mine's hungry, and you'll hear it ask."
2. `bark`, a kill that feeds, to the scythe: "There. Now hush."
3. `bark`, a drain, to the scythe: "I know. I know. Find one, then."
4. `support`, to Wren, who is counting the dead after a fight: "Don't do that out loud. They hear their number and stay."
5. `support`, to Maud, while handing over her blanket: "Take it. I'm freezing, so don't argue, and don't give it back."
6. `support`, to Teodor, after he shares his bread: "Your country's food is wet. Thank you. It's still wet."
7. `scene`, the field, returned in Hask's ranks, to Rook: "He gave me the seat by the fire. You'd have given me a tent and a lecture."
8. `scene`, the Oath Stone, `spared`, to the captain: "It's sulking. Good. It can sulk. I'm the one who carries it."

Sample 3 breaks "I know." from the tic list on purpose: it's said to the god, not as an answer to a person's feeling, and it's the only kind of sympathy she shows it.

**Average test.** Stock: "This blade thirsts for vengeance, and so do I." Ours: "It's hungry again. It's always hungry when I'm cold, which up here is always."
