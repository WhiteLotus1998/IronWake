# Teodor, the pikeman

**Rule 1: stands.** Teodor may name a feeling once, as a scene's turn, and the beat sheet names it in advance (below).

**Who.** Aldmere; the eldest of nine, a levy pikeman, carrying his grandfather's rusted lance. Wants the pay, because it feeds eight, and says so. Won't say that he's glad to be away from the farm, or that he obeys anyone who sounds like his grandfather (his flaw).

**Image source** (rule 5). Aldmere outdoors: the farm year (lambing, the hay, the harvest, a frost too early), weather as work (rain on cut hay, mud to the hock), the levy roll that takes the eldest son. Pay is what it buys, a winter of bread, never a number. Never: pages, ink or damp on a shelf (Pell's; she takes Aldmere's rain indoors). Never sums, rates or scrip (Ottilie's).

**Shape.** The shortest of the three, and slow. He answers the question from two lines ago, after the talk has moved on (rule 4), which is how a cold reader knows him with the speaker hidden. Plain words, one clause where he can. When an order comes in his grandfather's register, he says "sir" before he has decided to.

**Wit:** never (rule 7). He laughs at Ottilie's; he doesn't trade one back.

**Names** (rule 8). Family by name, freely, once each (his sister, the farm's people): they are the pay's reason, and the first time is the time that matters. The company by trade or not at all. The lance has no name until The Warden's Gate names it, and he never names it himself.

**The one named feeling:** proposed for the camp after the Grange (map 7), where the voice he would obey is Hask's. Fear, of his own yes. The beat sheet confirms it, and decides whether it replaces or follows his placeholder answer.

**Never.**
- "honour", "glory", "make him proud"
- a number with a unit (pay, distance, odds)
- a speech; more than two sentences at once
- **Most at risk:** the folksy proverb. "Grandad always said" is one line in the campaign, not a habit; after it, the grandfather comes up as an object (the lance, the oil, the roll).
- **Second risk:** the quiet button. Teodor's lines are short and kind, so every scene wants to end on one; his scenes end on an act (he picks up the lance, he oils it).

**Samples** (illustrative; they don't ship and carry no ids).
1. `bark`, holding a tile, to nobody: "Stood worse off a ram."
2. `bark`, the lance wakes a stage, to nobody: "More rust off. Grandad never got this far down."
3. `support`, to Ottilie, after her offer to change his scrip at face: "Bread's what it buys. Change it if you like."
4. `support`, to Alder, two lines after Alder asked who works the farm: "My sister Annet. She was always better at it."
5. `support`, to Pell, as the sky goes grey: "Rain by noon."
6. `scene`, the Tollgate, to Hask, who is looking at the lance: "It was my grandad's, sir. He said keep it oiled."
7. `scene`, the Grange, to Hask's "Give me the list.": "You talk like him. I'd have done it for him."
8. `card`, his door, Halberdier: "Teodor learns the axe. He keeps the lance in his other hand the whole time."

Sample 7 is the flaw on its edge: the "sir" is gone, and he still hasn't said no. The no is the beat sheet's.

**Average test.** Stock: "I'll make my grandfather proud, or die trying!" Ours: "Grandad would have sworn. I'm still oiling his lance."
