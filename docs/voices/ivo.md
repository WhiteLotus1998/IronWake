# Ivo Caddell, the storm-warden (proposed)

**Status.** Proposed in STORY draft 7 for Lotus's lightning mage (Design Table, 2026-10-06). Nothing ships from this sheet until Lotus takes the character, and the name is a placeholder.

**Rule 1: stands.** Ivo may name a feeling once, as a scene's turn, and the beat sheet names it in advance (below). He is the speaker least likely to, because nothing lands on him.

**Who.** Sallow; forty-four, an adept of the lightning school. Indentured at ten to the storm-wardens' guild, which keeps a man on the roof of every salt house and counting house when the weather comes in off the sea, with a copper rod and the guild's Lore, to catch the lightning and put it in the ground before it finds the warehouse. Then nineteen years on the roof of Vasse and Daughter, Ottilie's family firm, keeping the garrison's salt dry. Struck off by the guild as the firm's man when the firm folded; out of work a year; joins at the camp after Harrow Weir. Wants the pay, and to see the salt go where it was owed. Won't say that Ottilie's father died owing him nineteen years' wages, or that he has never once thrown what he catches.

**Image source** (rule 5). The roof, and the storm as a threat to a building: the copper rod, its strap and its ground-stake, the smell before a strike, the hair standing on his arms, the count between the flash and the sound, slates wet and slates dry, the salt house under him and the measure of salt in it, the sea coming in. He reads weather from under it, as a thing to be caught. Rook reads it from above, as a thing to fly in, and the two never share a word: hers are wind, front, thermal and cloud base; his are charge, count, strike and ground. Never the fen (Ansgar's). Never prices or scrip (Ottilie's; he talks about salt by the measure and never by the price). Never pages (Pell's).

**Shape.** Full, cheerful sentences, the only cheerful voice in the company, and he asks: how a thing works, what it's called, who taught you. He remembers every answer and uses it later. Asked about himself, he asks about your trade (rule 4). Nineteen years alone on a roof made him glad of anyone. **The grounding:** when something should land on him (an insult, a death, the firm), his sentence doesn't change length or shape at all, and the scene has to notice that he didn't flinch, because he never does.

**Wit:** yes, gentle, about roofs, weather, his knees on a ladder and his own age. Never about the firm, and never about the lightning as a weapon.

**Names** (rule 8). He asks everyone's name on the first day and then uses them freely, which is rule 8 broken on purpose, as the Kin breaks it. The difference a cold reader must be able to see: Ivo asks first, every time, and the Kin never asks. Ottilie is "Miss Vasse" until she tells him to stop, and then he stops, once and for good. Hask is "the Warden", flatly, a trade title.

**The one named feeling:** proposed for the Rod (his quest 2), on the keep roof, after the first throw of his life. Sorry: not for the sworn he struck, but for Ottilie's father, dead a year, which is the thing that finally comes through when he stops grounding. The beat sheet confirms it.

**Never.**
- "power", "unleash", "fury", "wrath", "the storm within" (the stock lightning mage; his lightning is a hazard he manages, not a feeling he has)
- a storm as a metaphor for anything; weather is weather
- "burn" (fire's word, Pell's), "thunder" as a verb
- a line that says he is cheerful, or why
- **Most at risk:** the lovable old hand, everyone's uncle. He is kind and he is hollow, and the scenes must show the hollow: a death in the company that he answers with a question about how the dressing is tied.
- **Second risk:** the question as a tic. One question he wants the answer to a scene; the rest is him telling you how a roof works.

**Samples** (illustrative; they don't ship and carry no ids).
1. `bark`, casting, to the target: "One. Two. That's close enough."
2. `bark`, low on HP, to nobody: "Off the roof. Everybody off the roof but me."
3. `support`, to Ottilie, after "Don't call me that.": "No. What did he finish the last line with?"
4. `support`, to Ansgar, who has said he ran: "I stayed. They gave me a certificate. It's at the bottom of the harbour, I expect."
5. `support`, to Pell, who has corrected his hand: "Guild hand. We learned it on slates in the rain. Show me yours."
6. `support`, to Brannock, who has handed him bread first: "Who taught you to bank an oven? That's a roofer's draw, that."
7. `scene`, the Salt House, the salt going out on the carts, to the captain: "Eight years of it. Nineteen if you count the pans. Where's it going?"
8. `scene`, the Rod, the keep roof, after the first throw, to the captain: "He'd have liked to see that. The old man. I'm sorry he didn't."
9. `card`, his joining, the Weir camp: "A grey man came down off the mill roof with a copper rod on his back and asked the Sallow bowman a question about her father. He has not asked to join. He has asked where the company sleeps."

Sample 8 is the booked feeling, and it is the first sentence of his in the campaign that is not about a thing. Sample 3 is rule 4 and the grounding in one line: told off, he agrees and asks the question he came to ask.

**Average test.** Stock: "Feel the wrath of the storm!" Ours: "Count. One, two. It's close. Everybody off the roof but me."
