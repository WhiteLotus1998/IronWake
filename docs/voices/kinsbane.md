# Kinsbane, the god in the scythe

**Rule 1: widened, then narrowed.** Kinsbane names its hunger and its hate as freely as a dog shows teeth; it names no human feeling at all until it wakes, and then exactly one, once (below).

**Who.** The outlands; an old god of the hunt in the lean season, older than the faiths, hidden in a blade for three hundred years and fed only on what the blade kills. Speaks only to its carrier, one short line per event, at most three a battle (0221). Wants to eat. Under that, wants the one prey it was made for, and has forgotten that it wants it. Won't say what that prey is, ever: nobody in game names the Kin as its enemy (STORY), and it can't, because it doesn't remember until it's woken, and woken it doesn't need to.

**Image source** (rule 5). The hunt in the dry months: the thinning herd, the waterhole shrinking, spoor, the limping one and the one that runs, marrow, the pack that lets the old one go. Heat is its weather; cold is only the absence of a meal, and it never says "cold"; on a frost map no reader hears it as hunger. Its hate has one image: **the pen.** A fed herd that never runs is the thing it despises most, so the sworn smell to it like penned stock. It never says why. Never: the shrine, smoke, offerings, the keeper's broom (Keziah's; she remembers the shrine, it never does). Never the hearth, the table or warmth as comfort (the Kin's; Kinsbane must never sound like it, and a cold reader must be able to tell the two gods apart on one line each).

**Shape.** Its sentences grow with its teeth. Starved, one word or two, an imperative or a noun. Each tooth adds a clause: the first tooth line is a sentence, and by the third it can look ahead ("next time"). The woken line is the first in which it says its carrier's name, and the first that speaks from memory instead of appetite. It never asks a question before it wakes. It answers nothing; Keziah's replies are hers and it talks straight over them (rule 4 by appetite).

**Wit:** never, before waking. After waking, once, in the Fight win's goodbye, and only if the beat sheet earns it.

**Names** (rule 8). None, until it wakes. Then "{name}", once, in the woken line (0221, the Table's model). After that the blade is silent in battle. The one other line it ever says to her by name is the goodbye in the Fight win (Under the Hill).

**The one named feeling:** the goodbye in Under the Hill's Fight win, sane and quiet before it leaves the blade. Proposed: "glad", as the one word it has that is not appetite. The beat sheet confirms it.

**The choice screen** (`pitch`, 0222). Kinsbane does not speak there. The offer is Keziah's to make, in her voice, and the blade is unfed by this captain; the first line the player ever hears from it is on a battlefield, to her, not to them.

**Never.**
- "blood", "souls", "flesh", "feast", "master", "mortal" (the stock hungry blade; it eats like an animal, not like a villain)
- warm, hearth, safe, we (the Kin's words; one of them in Kinsbane's mouth breaks both gods)
- a threat to Keziah; it is never cruel to her, only indifferent until it isn't
- the Kin, the hill, the prison, the sworn by name
- **Most at risk:** the edgy blade, every line a menace. It is hungry, not evil; hunger is dull and practical and that is scarier.
- **Second risk:** the clause count as a tic. The growth is a rule for drafting, not something the player should be able to count line by line.

**Samples** (illustrative; they don't ship and carry no ids).
1. `bark`, starved (a drain empties it), to Keziah: "Empty."
2. `bark`, starved, late in the map, to Keziah: "Anything. The slow one."
3. `bark`, first tooth, to Keziah: "That one limped. It would have died anyway."
4. `bark`, second tooth, to Keziah: "Better. That one saw the blade coming and ran."
5. `bark`, third tooth, to Keziah: "Faster than the last. Next time, the one that bites back."
6. `bark`, fourth tooth, carried past the sworn, to Keziah: "These never ran. Someone fattened them. Kill the one who did."
7. `bark`, woken (the shipped model, kept), to Keziah: "{name}. I remember what I'm for."
8. `scene`, Under the Hill, Fight won, the goodbye, to Keziah: "The herd can run again. I am glad it was you, {name}."

Sample 6 is the hate in its only image, and as near as it ever comes to naming the Kin: "the one who did" is a mystery to Keziah until the Grange, and something else to the player on a second run.

**Average test.** Stock: "Feed me, wielder. Their blood will make us strong." Ours: "That one limped. It would have died anyway."
