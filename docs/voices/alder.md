# Alder Fenn, the captain

**Rule 1: narrowed.** Alder never names a feeling, not even once: the captain's feeling is the player's, so the one break the rule allows every other speaker is not Alder's to spend.

**Who.** The seam; a garrison ward and foundling, academy-trained, early twenties (#648: male or female, so this sheet says "they"). Wants the ten names home. Won't say that they were sent away first, or that Hask was their father in everything but the word. The origin pick (#648) is the card, not the voice: wherever the foundling came from, Alder grew up at the keep, and the images below stay the garrison's. Named Alder for the one tree in the keep yard, the alder against the bakehouse wall the foundling was found under, and Fenn for the duty clerk whose turn it was when the roll needed a second name (STORY draft 7). Alder knows the tree. The hands are the Cold Kitchen's, and Alder never mentions either.

**Image source** (rule 5). Garrison and seam: the keep's walls and the rime on them, horses and tack (the girth, the bit, a lame foot), the roll and the list (ink, a name, a line through it). The cold is home and Alder says so, plainly, as a fact about where to sleep. Never: ledgers, scrip or tides (Ottilie's); pages or books (Pell's). Never the sickbed or the rite (Maud's) either, though Alder will say the rite badly if nobody else is left.

**Shape.** Short, and shortest when it matters. Most lines are a choice the player just made, said aloud: a tile, a target, who goes first. Questions about Alder get answered as a question about the company: asked how they are, Alder says where the watch stands (rule 4). Asked for a speech, Alder gives a placement.

**The flaw in the grammar.** Alder won't spend anyone, so they spend themselves. It shows as the subject of the order: Alder's orders put "I" on the dangerous tile and "you" on the safe one. A line that sends someone else into the gap costs Alder their name (below). The flaw is never named by Alder; Maud names it once (STORY, after the raid), and Alder answers with geometry.

**Wit:** never (rule 10). Alder is the straight line other people's jokes land on. A line of Alder's that would get a laugh is cut, even a dry one.

**Names** (rule 8). Alder says a name in three places only: reading the list (the one place names come free), sending that person somewhere Alder would rather stand, and the last time. Marrit's name is said once, on Brackwater, and not again until the keep. Hask is "him" to the company and "sir" to his face until the Grange, and nothing after it.

**The one named feeling:** none (rule 1 narrowed, above).

**Never.**
- "trust me", "together", "my friends", "for the" (no rallying cry, no cause in the dative)
- "brave", "sorry" as a closer, any line that thanks the company for dying
- explaining the plan's reason; Alder says where, never why
- **Most at risk:** the quiet button. Every Alder line is short, so every scene wants to end on one. A scene may end on Alder's act, not Alder's fragment, unless its beat sheet says otherwise (the reseal's "Same name. Different iron." is the one booked).
- **Second risk:** "I'll" on every order. The flaw is in the grammar, so it repeats; no more than one "I'll" a scene, and the rest of the self-first orders go through the verb or the tile ("The gate's mine.").

**Samples** (illustrative; they don't ship and carry no ids).
1. `bark`, Commander's Word, press, to the company: "Forward. The gate's mine."
2. `bark`, Commander's Word, fall back, to the company: "Back to the wall. I'll hold the gap."
3. `bark`, a listed member falls, to nobody: "Pell." (the name, the last time)
4. `bark`, the captain's `talk` that spares the passed claimant: "Go. You're not on my list, and you're not on his."
5. `support`, to Keziah, who has complained about the cold again: "It's colder at the keep. You'll sleep better there."
6. `support`, to Maud, answering "You stood in front again. You know they can stand too.": "The gap was one wide."
7. `scene`, the Grange, to Hask's "Give me the list.": "I read it this morning. Nobody on it is yours."
8. `card`, the first promotion, one line per class, each a choice and not a temper:
   - Vanguard: "I'll take the front."
   - Marshal: "I'll tell them where to stand."
   - Ranger: "I'll stand off and call what I see."

The Vanguard line is the flaw agreeing with itself; the Marshal line is the flaw's price, since it means telling someone else to take the front; the Ranger line steps out of both. None of the three says which is right. Hask reads them at the Grange (STORY, "What he sees in the captain's class") and the reseal's last card reads them again, so each has to survive being quoted back.

**Average test.** Before the keep, the stock captain: "This is where we make our stand. For everyone we've lost!" Ours: "Same as the raid. I'm on the gate."
