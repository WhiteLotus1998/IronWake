# Wren, the cadet

**Rule 1: stands.** Wren may name a feeling once, as a scene's turn, and the beat sheet names it in advance (below).

**Who.** Aldmere; a surveyor's daughter, nineteen, a cadet, met at the Grange (map 7). Her father was hanged for moving border stones he didn't move. Wants his surveys, the originals, which are at the Grange. Won't say that she held the chain for him the season he set those stones, so if one was moved, she should have seen it.

**Image source** (rule 5). The survey: the chain and its links, a stone and its mark, a bearing, paces, the boundary line, the field book with its thumbed corner. The land measured, never worked. She counts things: stones, steps, arrows, the men on a wall. Never: the harvest or weather as work (Teodor's; his Aldmere is worked, hers is measured). Never a price or a rate (Ottilie's; Ottilie counts in prices, Wren in things). Never a page as precious (Pell's; the field book is a tool she writes in with a wet thumb).

**Shape.** The most words of anyone in the company, and the most numbers. Run-on clauses joined by "and", a count where someone else would put an adjective, and she fills a silence with a tally (her cast line: she fills a lot of them). Asked a question she can answer, she answers before it is finished. Asked one she can't, she counts something she can (rule 4). When she is wrong she stops counting, and the next line is short: that is the tell.

**Wit:** yes, one kind: other people's estimates ("You said three."). Never about the hanging, and nobody else gets to joke about it near her.

**Names** (rule 8). A roll is the one place she spends names freely: who's standing, in order, out loud, the way Alder reads the list. Outside a roll, a name is a turn. Her father is "my father", never his name, until the originals are open. Hask is "the man who moved them" until the Grange gives him a name.

**The one named feeling:** proposed for the camp after the Grange (map 7), where the originals show every stone where her father set it. Relief, and she hates that it's the word. The beat sheet confirms it.

**Never.**
- "I think", "maybe", "about" before a number (she counts; she doesn't guess)
- "justice", "clear his name", "he was innocent" (she says where the stones were)
- a number she hasn't counted; when she guesses, she says she's guessing, and it costs her
- **Most at risk:** the count on every line. A tally a line is a tic; one count that matters a scene, and the rest of her lines are just her talking.
- **Second risk:** being right as the punchline. Her flaw is the times she's wrong; a scene that lets her be right every time has lost her.

**Samples** (illustrative; they don't ship and carry no ids).
1. `bark`, a move, to nobody: "Five, six, seven. Seven. Told you it was seven."
2. `bark`, low on HP, to nobody: "Two more hits. I'd rather not count them."
3. `support`, to Ottilie, after "A moved stone pays somebody. Find who.": "I don't care who it paid. I care where it was."
4. `support`, to Pell, after "That isn't a forty, it's a fourteen. Your figure's smudged.": "Then I've been wrong since the Grange. Don't tell anyone. Fourteen."
5. `support`, to Teodor, who carried her off the field: "Four hundred and twelve paces. I counted. You never put me down once."
6. `support`, to Alder, after "Hold here.": "Here's three tiles off the wall. You held two at the Grange. Which one was wrong?"
7. `scene`, the Grange, the originals open, to nobody: "His hand. Every stone where he set it. Every one."
8. `card`, her meeting, the Grange: "A cadet in the records room, a surveyor's chain wound on one arm, counting under her breath. She asks for the Aldmere box by its shelf number."

Sample 4 is the flaw kept light: she is wrong, it's funny, and she still finishes the count.

**Average test.** Stock: "I will clear my father's name, whatever it takes!" Ours: "Forty-one stones on the east line. I want to see which ones they moved."
