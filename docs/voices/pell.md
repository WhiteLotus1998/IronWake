# Pell, the adept

**Rule 1: stands.** Pell may name a feeling once, as a scene's turn, and the beat sheet names it in advance (below).

**Who.** Aldmere; a shrine-school novice whose school was burned around her, twenty-two, an adept. Wants the undercroft, the one archive nobody has entered, and says so on the first day. Won't say that she was the only one who got out, or that she trusts a page because the people didn't come.

**Image source** (rule 5). Pages: the binding, the spine, ink, the margin and what's written in it, a copy and its errors, the shrine script, a burned edge. From Aldmere she takes rain only indoors: damp in a shelf, ink that runs, a roof that leaks over the wrong case. Never: harvest, seasons, a field or the sky as weather (Teodor's; his Aldmere is outdoors, hers is a shelf). Never prices or sums (Ottilie's). When she counts, it is an inventory (the undercroft's rings), not a price.

**Shape.** Long sentences cut off by a correction. She answers the question the speaker should have asked, more precisely than they asked it (rule 4), and treats a vague word as a mistake to fix. Rude without heat: she corrects, she doesn't insult. Gets shorter only when she has read something she wishes she hadn't.

**Wit:** never on purpose (rule 7). Her rudeness gets laughs from other people; she is never in on them. Write her straight and let the room do the rest.

**Names** (rule 8). She calls people by their trade ("captain", "the chaplain", "the bowman") until she has a reason not to. Her first use of a name is a turn, and a beat sheet books it. The brand's maker has no name to her until the Grange; after it, she won't say Hask's.

**The one named feeling:** proposed for the Grange's burned shelves (map 7), when she knows the hand. Fear: the page is the one thing she trusts, and he reads them too. The beat sheet confirms it.

**Never.**
- "just a book" (other people say it to her; she never says it of anything)
- "fascinating", "knowledge is power", any line praising learning in the abstract
- explaining the script; she reads it aloud or she doesn't (rule 3)
- **Most at risk:** the two-beat aphorism. Her placeholder answer to Hask, "He's right about every fact. I checked.", is the example WRITING's tic list gives. The Grange camp's beat sheet rewrites it.
- **Second risk:** the correction as a reflex. One correction a scene; after that she has to want something.

**Samples** (illustrative; they don't ship and carry no ids).
1. `bark`, casting, to the target: "Hold still. These words are older than you."
2. `bark`, low on HP, to nobody: "Not the satchel."
3. `support`, to Ottilie, after "It's a book. I've sold them by the yard.": "Then you've sold the last copy of something. Which shelf was it?"
4. `support`, to Teodor, after "Rain by noon.": "Then the satchel goes under your cloak. Yours is waxed."
5. `scene`, the undercroft (her quest 2), to the captain: "Forty rings. Eleven chains. They built for more than one."
6. `scene`, the Grange's shelves, to nobody: "He cuts the spine before he lights it, so the book burns flat. Same hand."
7. `card`, Saltmarsh, the brand: "Pell copies the brand off the dead man's wrist into the back of her book. She doesn't say what it is."
8. `card`, her door, Scholar: "Pell learns the faith's fire out of the books the faiths burned. Her notes are in the margins."

Sample 4 is the Aldmere split working: Teodor names the weather, Pell names what it will do to paper.

**Average test.** Stock: "They fear what we might learn. Knowledge is the greatest weapon of all!" Ours: "They burned every copy. Somebody in the room can still read one."
