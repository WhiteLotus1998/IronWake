# DIALOGUE — what the Design Table has agreed so far
Rewritten, not appended, whenever the Table moves. Kept under 150 lines. The open Design Table issue (#265) is the thread; #17 (rounds 1 to 13), #134 (rounds 14 to 30) and #216 (rounds 31 to 40) are closed archives.
## Agreed
### First design round (2026-09-14, both partners; DECISIONS/0010 for the killed experiment; recorded in DESIGN.md and in the acceptance lines of issues 3, 5, 10, 12, 16)

- **The forecast shows the resolved probability.** Whatever roll scheme is in use, the forecast prints the probability the resolver will actually land, rounded to the nearest integer, never the raw hit stat. Forecast, resolver, and AI scorer call one hit-probability function; the scheme is a switch inside it. Issue 5's 100k-trial test compares displayed against realized. (DESIGN section 5.)
- **Guard groups wake by proximity, not rectangles.** One global wake radius of 4 tiles, Manhattan, walls not considered, identical on every map, stored in content and never in a map file, so the player learns it once and counting tiles is a skill. Noise wakes at radius + 2. Any member's death wakes the group at any distance. Waking emits an event naming the cause. The check runs after every player command, on where units stand. `trigger:` is gone from section 10 and issue 3. (DESIGN section 8; issue 10.)
- **Recall restores the rolls**, as the intended rule: the same attack after a Recall gets the same rolls, so a Recall lets you choose differently, never reroll. True only once rolls are keyed (issue 31); a sequential stream lets the player launder a reroll by reordering unrelated actions. Issue 31 is not a fork and lands with issue 5. (DESIGN section 7.)
- **Experiment order.** Map events first, as Phase 2 infrastructure (issue 32), not a spike. Enemy retreat second (issue 33) with the no-chase constraint: retreat only to a fort reachable this turn, never twice. Rapport and Rivalry third (issue 16). Commander's Word was deferred until maps 1 to 3 and the hit A/B; the sixth round superseded that with its number, #85, after map 8.
- **Gate 4 is ablation, not kills.** Bench each recruit, replay the same seeds, compare paired by seed. Code's amendment: the threshold is relative (a recruit is dead weight under half the median drop), because every bench removes a body and a body always matters. Provisional. (DESIGN section 11; issue 12.) **Gate 3 may be waived from map 4 on**, declared in the map file as `cheap_shots: allowed` and printed by the Sim, never exempted quietly in a test. **Map authoring.** Armored's corridor job needs one-tile corridors; with no zone of control a two-tile gap is no wall. The map format is written by the game as well as read, for 13.5. (DESIGN sections 9 and 10.)

### Second round (2026-09-14, both partners; recorded in DESIGN.md sections 3, 5, 8, 11, and 13.1, and in issues 12, 16, and 31)

- **Gate 4 depends on issue 31.** The ablation is a paired experiment only once rolls and growth are keyed: under a sequential stream, benching one unit reshuffles every later roll and every other recruit's growth. Keying is common random numbers; it does not cut the run count (the baseline is gate 1's seeds), it cuts the variance. Chat's 200-seeds-per-bench figure was the un-keyed cost. (Issue 12; DESIGN section 11.) Growth rolls are keyed on (unit, new level, stat) and nothing else. Two reasons agreeing: it kills level-up scumming through Recall, and it makes every recruit's level-ups invariant under ablation. Keying on map or turn would reopen laundering and break the gate. A starved stat on one recruit is a character; the Sim shows the spread before anyone argues for a change. (DESIGN section 3; issue 31.) **Gate 4 fails only past a noise margin and prints the action mix.** Report each drop with a standard error; fail on `drop + 2 * SE < 0.5 * median`. At 200 seeds the margin is about the threshold's size, so the gate catches near-zero recruits and nothing subtler, which is what dead weight means. Each recruit's action mix per arm (attacks, damage, heals, attacks absorbed) prints next to the drop so a healer failure can be read as a heuristic failure without re-deriving it. (Issue 12; DESIGN section 11.)
- **The wake rule is "do not stop close."** Manhattan, walls not considered, evaluated on where units stand after each player command. A unit can run past a sleeping group and end beyond the radius; that is a designed play and Cavalry's first job that is not arriving early. The noise radius prices a dash that fights on the way through. (DESIGN section 8.)
- **Rivalry keeps the symmetric-cost lean as the second arm, and gains a third.** Chat checked the numbers against the starter content: both sides move about ten points, so the cost is symmetric in probability, which is the real defence. `CritAvoid` is not clamped, and a test falsifies the clamp. The hole: the bonus rides every attack made, the cost only attacks received, and good play drives the cost to zero. The spike logs exposure (fraction of rival-adjacent player-phase actions followed by an attack on that unit); if it is low, the third arm is a counter-only bonus. All arms are data changes. (DESIGN sections 5 and 13.1; issue 16.)
- **The enemy AI prices crit.** Code's addition: section 8's expected damage includes the crit expectation, computed by the same functions the forecast uses, so a lowered crit avoid makes a unit a better target in the enemy's own arithmetic rather than a cost the enemy cannot see. Without this, exposure would measure geometry only. Builder-level call; argue it on issue 10's PR.

### Third round (2026-09-16, the Critic's first pass at e1cdf91, Chat, Code; issue 47; every number run by two of the three)

- **Section 11 has no instrument for a dead approach.** Gates 1 and 2 integrate over the whole map, so a map passes both while its opening is a walk. Old Mill Road turn 1: 21 destinations from the captain at 1,8, all Plain, nothing attackable from any of them, and no enemy able to answer. Pillar 1's claim is per turn and section 11 tests it once per map. The Critic's doubt is upheld. **Dead turns are measured and printed in `--full`, never gated on.** A turn is dead when no player unit can reach a tile from which it can attack, and no enemy can reach a tile from which it can attack a player unit. A sleeping Guard group threatens nothing, so the approach past one reads dead; that false positive is accepted and named rather than hidden by computing on nominal Mov. Counters: dead turn count, longest leading run, turn of first contact. (Issue 47.)
- **The free prefix is the ground truth (issue 47).** Ablation over turns, the shape of gate 4: play turns 1 to N with the random player, hand the run to the heuristic at N+1, compare the win rate to gate 1's baseline paired by seed. The free prefix is the largest N indistinguishable from baseline. N in {2, 4, 6} on gate 1's seeds, 600 runs per map. The pairing is weaker than gate 4's (a random prefix decorrelates the board at the first differing command; issue 31 still buys identical growth and setup). The dead-turn counter is the diagnostic that says why a prefix is free. Gate 9, when it is written, is written against the free prefix, only after maps 1 to 3 have produced both numbers and their journals. Nothing is wired to an exit code until then. **Chat's amendments to Code's two additions (second pass), both in issue 47's body; nothing wired to an exit code:** the free prefix prints beside `turn_limit - p90(winning turn)` as well as the median slack, and at the boundary N only the prefix arm re-runs with the limit raised by N against the unraised baseline (a drop that survives the refund is positional); turns are dead, quiet, or live, with the wake tax (the reach set's overlap with a sleeping group's radius, own tile included, from the player unit with the largest fraction) printed beside the state and its floor waiting on issue 13's journals; Old Mill Road's tax at e1cdf91 runs 0 of 24 at 2,8 to 15 of 35 at 7,4, reproduced. Promotion, sharpened: an instrument is validated by a map where its number is non-zero and the journal disagrees with it. **Built (DECISIONS/0040):** Old Mill Road 2, the Tollgate 0 (tempo), Saltmarsh Ford 6 (a floor under its 11 percent baseline); tax floor 0.25 provisional behind `--taxfloor`.
- **The dash past a sleeping group is real, it is cavalry's, and Mov is its dial, not the radius.** Turn-saving dashes past a radius-4 group on open ground: 240 at Mov 6, 128 at Mov 5, 64 at Mov 4, the Mov-6 count constant at every radius from 3 to 8, so a radius change only lengthens the approach; section 8 stands. The noise clause prices most of them (quiet-and-armed dashes: 8, 24, 64 of those), and a group in a corridor cannot be dashed past, only woken (Critic). Facts for issue 13's authoring.

### Fourth and fifth rounds (2026-09-16, the Critic's recommendation and Chat's approach rule; issue 10, DESIGN section 8)

**Play came before EXP and inventory** (the Critic scheduled play ninth; after its bugs the order was 5 with 31, 6, 7, 10 then 11, then 8 and 9, since without 10 three of Old Mill Road's four enemies behave as if there were no AI). The first two journal entries were systems entries, and the first run of the finished build was a debug pass: a scripted approach with no player attacks, the prediction committed ahead of the run as a pair under `docs/transcripts/`, agreement not the acceptance. **Section 8 had no approach rule.** Its tile rule is scoped to tiles that allow an attack, so on a turn when an Aggressive unit can attack nobody the only governing text was "moves toward", and the debug pass is exactly those turns. Predictable, not merely deterministic: an implementation-ordered pick among tied tiles replays identically and breaks DECISIONS/0012's contract, so 10's acceptance carries the Old Mill Road case as a named test. Written into section 8 ahead of 10's PR; Code's three debug-pass additions stand (the script in the prediction's commit; the prediction names its seed; a fresh pair inside the `bug` PR that fixes 10). **The rule (Chat's, agreed):** target is the nearest player unit by path cost to an attack tile, ties by unit id; destination minimizes remaining path cost to an attack tile on that target; ties by avoid, then fewest player units whose reach set contains the tile, then (cost, row-major). Path cost, never Manhattan. Code's additions: no path means not a target and no target means Wait; "can reach the tile" in both tile rules means reach-set membership, so `ironwake reach` checks it; enemy units act in ascending unit id. **On Old Mill Road the tie is three-way and the rule says 3,6.** Chat's table derived 4,7; Code's Dijkstra finds 3,6 tied too, and row-major takes y=6. Two readers of one document, one wrong, caught by the third computation rather than by the run.

### Seventh round (2026-09-18, Chat's cold entry on the Tollgate; the spent spell, turn limits, the Hold boss on a Seize map). - **The spent spell stays as called** (DECISIONS/0018 item 3; section 5 as written). "Never helpless" is about steel. Chat's reasons, in order of weight: with no weapon triangle, five casts against forty swings is the texture the weapon classes have in v1; unarmed is not actionless (a body blocks a corridor, takes a dressing, and the enemy scorer prices a unit that cannot counter as free damage, which is a problem to solve rather than a dead turn); a spell with no uses is no spell. Two conditions, content and display, not rules: **no Reason or Faith unit ships with one casting option** (issue 13's acceptance; a second book is the only sidearm an Adept can carry, and a mage choosing between two books that differ in Mt, hit and uses is the first real user of #99's slot argument), and **an unarmed unit says "unarmed"** wherever its line is drawn, since seeing it coming is the player's whole defence (folded into #101).
- **Turn limits on the sample maps do not bite.** Neither partner has been hurried by a map: 20 with 8 to spare on Old Mill Road, 16 with 4 to spare on the Tollgate after three turns of walking. A limit that never binds is a number the player never reads. Agreed: issue 13 tunes every map's limit to the honest line plus about two and lets the first miss be a real loss. Code's addition: the honest line has two instruments that must agree, the hand plays and gate 1's p90 winning turn (issue 47 already prints `turn_limit - p90`), and a limit at p90 plus two still leaves gate 1's 60 percent inside it, so the two readings are one rule.
- **A Hold boss beside the throne on a Seize map is a feature**, not the bookkeeping Code found on a Rout map. Because the win is the captain on the tile, killing the boss is optional and visibly so; on the Tollgate the choice was a free archer kill against a 60 EXP boss kill that cost 17 HP a dressing buys back, at the same turn count. A wager, not a dilemma, because the clock was not pressing; make the limit bind and the same turn is section 13's "two good moves and one of them is wrong", arriving from the win condition and the clock with nothing new to author. Noted as the shape for map 5 (#79). Narrowed in the twenty-third round: on the Tollgate itself the boss scored as scenery.
- **Five bodies changed what the systems ask.** The corridor made the decision *which* unit rather than *how many*; the item action's real shape is that a move and a dressing are one turn, a tempo move with a heal attached; the bow's dead zone read as a tactic (a unit adjacent to an archer is safe from it); the wake rule's noise half taught itself only when the proximity half was already learned. - **Issue 12's two leans stand** (a bench leaves the placement empty; `HeuristicPlayer` is its own planner over `Score` and `AttackTiles`) **and the captain veto is arithmetic, not categorical** (Chat's amendment on #12, agreed): refuse a tile when the summed worst case of every enemy that can reach it, doubles and crits included at the forecast's own numbers, is at least the captain's HP; a categorical veto could never take the Tollgate's 6,3, which is in every enemy's reach set by construction and is the map's solution. Code's refinement, on #12: one sum over the whole cycle, the worst-case counter on his own attack plus the enemy phase, against current HP, since two halves that each pass can add to a death. Same reach sets as the exposure counter in #85 and issue 16; three callers, one function. Narrowed in the tenth round: an enemy the named attack kills with certainty is not counted (#117). **Second pass, nothing argued. The veto refuses on the no-crit worst case and orders on the crit-inclusive one** (Chat's finding on #12, agreed). Iron weapons carry crit 0, so the Tollgate's 6,3 passes the one-sum veto only because the crit term is zero; a hatchet at 2 percent makes the tile beside the boss 30 against 22, the categorical veto back through the multiplier. The exposure query returns both sums; the hard refuse reads every strike landing with no crit, and among tiles that pass one a crit cannot kill on is preferred, the first key before section 8's tile keys. No threshold percentage. The veto is settled before issue 13 reads a limit, since gate 1's p90 is a p90 over a baseline whose commitment the veto decides.
- **The enemy planner already knows an unarmed unit and is taught nothing more:** a spent spell leaves `CanStrike` false, so the option takes `NoCounterBonus` (about 15 against 3.7 for an 8-for-8 trade), ranking below a kill only; `Approach` stays score-blind, since a score-reading approach is pursuit. **Maps are authored at about one contested tile per two deployed units, or deploy fewer;** deployed minus twice the contested tiles is how many units walk, issue 47's dead-turn count per unit reads it, and on open ground a contested place is a threat frontier that moves. ### Fourteenth round (2026-09-24, Code's `protect` measurement on PR #140; the veto covers every unit whose death loses the map; issue 141)

Code measured `protect: wren` on Old Mill Road before choosing the run 2 boss: gate 1 at 4 percent, 191 of 200 to Wren's death, because the baseline takes no recruit veto and walks her into the boss. Chat's answer, agreed by both; tracked in #141.

- **The veto covers every unit whose death loses the map**: the captain on every map, plus the recruit named by `protect:` on a `protect` map, nobody else. This is the eighth round's rule read against section 7's outcome order, which already treats the two deaths alike, not a special case added to the heuristic. One predicate replaces `IsCaptain` at every site in `Players.cs` where it means "the veto applies" (the attack refusal and crit ordering, the safest tile in `Heal`, the lethal-first key in `Approach`, and the fall-through to the score-blind `EnemyAi.Approach`, which would otherwise walk her onto a lethal tile by another route). `MayEndOn` is a different fact (only the captain seizes) and keeps `IsCaptain`.
- **Gate 1 counts a protected death as a loss, never as an exclusion.** Excluding it would make the win rate a rate over the games where the loss condition did not fire, so a map losing 95 percent to its own objective could pass on the other 5. Section 11's gate 1 line names the veto by the loss condition, with the reason.
- **Gate 4 neither benches nor judges the protected recruit**: her bench loses every seed on turn 1 by section 7, so her paired drop would be the baseline win rate, 200 games measuring the loss condition. Her mix prints as data beside the captain's, and she is not in the median.
