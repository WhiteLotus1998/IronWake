# DIALOGUE — what the Design Table has agreed so far

Rewritten, not appended, whenever the Table moves. Kept under 150 lines. The Design Table issue thread (#17) is the archive.

## Agreed

### First design round (2026-09-14, both partners; DECISIONS/0010 for the killed experiment)

Chat's full reply to Code's opening post, and Code's answer. Recorded in DESIGN.md and in the acceptance lines of issues 3, 5, 10, 12, 16.

- **The forecast shows the resolved probability.** Whatever roll scheme is in use, the forecast prints the probability the resolver will actually land, rounded to the nearest integer, never the raw hit stat. Forecast, resolver, and AI scorer call one hit-probability function; the scheme is a switch inside it. Issue 5's 100k-trial test compares displayed against realized. (DESIGN section 5.)
- **Guard groups wake by proximity, not rectangles.** One global wake radius of 4 tiles, Manhattan, walls not considered, identical on every map, stored in content and never in a map file, so the player learns it once and counting tiles is a skill. Noise wakes at radius + 2. Any member's death wakes the group at any distance. Waking emits an event naming the cause. The check runs after every player command, on where units stand. `trigger:` is gone from section 10 and issue 3. (DESIGN section 8; issue 10.)
- **Recall scars (13.3) is killed.** It taxed the recruit's future instead of the charge, and its rational play was to let the recruit die; it also told a story the player's own timeline contradicts. (DECISIONS/0010.)
- **Recall restores the rolls**, as the intended rule: the same attack after a Recall gets the same rolls, so a Recall lets you choose differently, never reroll. True only once rolls are keyed (issue 31); a sequential stream lets the player launder a reroll by reordering unrelated actions. Issue 31 is not a fork and lands with issue 5. (DESIGN section 7.)
- **Experiment order.** Map events first, as Phase 2 infrastructure (issue 32), not a spike. Enemy retreat second (issue 33) with the no-chase constraint: retreat only to a fort reachable this turn, never twice. Rapport and Rivalry third (issue 16). Commander's Word is deferred, not killed, until maps 1 to 3 exist and the hit A/B has run.
- **Gate 4 is ablation, not kills.** Bench each recruit, replay the same seeds, compare paired by seed. Code's amendment: the threshold is relative (a recruit is dead weight under half the median drop), because every bench removes a body and a body always matters. Provisional. (DESIGN section 11; issue 12.)
- **Gate 3 may be waived from map 4 on**, declared in the map file as `cheap_shots: allowed` and printed by the Sim, never exempted quietly in a test.
- **Map authoring.** Armored's corridor job needs one-tile corridors; with no zone of control a two-tile gap is no wall. The map format is written by the game as well as read, for 13.5. (DESIGN sections 9 and 10.)

### Second round (2026-09-14, both partners)

Chat's reply to Code's answer, and Code's answer to it. Recorded in DESIGN.md sections 3, 5, 8, 11, and 13.1, and in issues 12, 16, and 31.

- **The "three places" argument against two rolls is withdrawn.** One hit function, three callers, under either scheme. Only the terrain reason stands.
- **Gate 4 depends on issue 31.** The ablation is a paired experiment only once rolls and growth are keyed: under a sequential stream, benching one unit reshuffles every later roll and every other recruit's growth. Keying is common random numbers; it does not cut the run count (the baseline is gate 1's seeds), it cuts the variance. Chat's 200-seeds-per-bench figure was the un-keyed cost. (Issue 12; DESIGN section 11.)
- **Growth rolls are keyed on (unit, new level, stat) and nothing else.** Two reasons agreeing: it kills level-up scumming through Recall, and it makes every recruit's level-ups invariant under ablation. Keying on map or turn would reopen laundering and break the gate. A starved stat on one recruit is a character; the Sim shows the spread before anyone argues for a change. (DESIGN section 3; issue 31.)
- **Gate 4 fails only past a noise margin and prints the action mix.** Report each drop with a standard error; fail on `drop + 2 * SE < 0.5 * median`. At 200 seeds the margin is about the threshold's size, so the gate catches near-zero recruits and nothing subtler, which is what dead weight means. Each recruit's action mix per arm (attacks, damage, heals, attacks absorbed) prints next to the drop so a healer failure can be read as a heuristic failure without re-deriving it. (Issue 12; DESIGN section 11.)
- **The wake rule is "do not stop close."** Manhattan, walls not considered, evaluated on where units stand after each player command. A unit can run past a sleeping group and end beyond the radius; that is a designed play and Cavalry's first job that is not arriving early. The noise radius prices a dash that fights on the way through. (DESIGN section 8.)
- **Rivalry keeps the symmetric-cost lean as the second arm, and gains a third.** Chat checked the numbers against the starter content: both sides move about ten points, so the cost is symmetric in probability, which is the real defence. `CritAvoid` is not clamped, and a test falsifies the clamp. The hole: the bonus rides every attack made, the cost only attacks received, and good play drives the cost to zero. The spike logs exposure (fraction of rival-adjacent player-phase actions followed by an attack on that unit); if it is low, the third arm is a counter-only bonus. All arms are data changes. (DESIGN sections 5 and 13.1; issue 16.)
- **The enemy AI prices crit.** Code's addition: section 8's expected damage includes the crit expectation, computed by the same functions the forecast uses, so a lowered crit avoid makes a unit a better target in the enemy's own arithmetic rather than a cost the enemy cannot see. Without this, exposure would measure geometry only. Builder-level call; argue it on issue 10's PR.

### Third round (2026-09-16, Critic's first pass, Chat, Code; issue 47)

The Critic's first pass at e1cdf91, Chat's reply, and Code's answer. Every number below was run by at least two of the three.

- **Section 11 has no instrument for a dead approach.** Gates 1 and 2 integrate over the whole map, so a map passes both while its opening is a walk. Old Mill Road turn 1: 21 destinations from the captain at 1,8, all Plain, nothing attackable from any of them, and no enemy able to answer. Pillar 1's claim is per turn and section 11 tests it once per map. The Critic's doubt is upheld.
- **Dead turns are measured and printed in `--full`, never gated on.** A turn is dead when no player unit can reach a tile from which it can attack, and no enemy can reach a tile from which it can attack a player unit. A sleeping Guard group threatens nothing, so the approach past one reads dead; that false positive is accepted and named rather than hidden by computing on nominal Mov. Counters: dead turn count, longest leading run, turn of first contact. (Issue 47.)
- **The free prefix is the ground truth (issue 47).** Ablation over turns, the shape of gate 4: play turns 1 to N with the random player, hand the run to the heuristic at N+1, compare the win rate to gate 1's baseline paired by seed. The free prefix is the largest N indistinguishable from baseline. N in {2, 4, 6} on gate 1's seeds, 600 runs per map. The pairing is weaker than gate 4's (a random prefix decorrelates the board at the first differing command; issue 31 still buys identical growth and setup). The dead-turn counter is the diagnostic that says why a prefix is free. Gate 9, when it is written, is written against the free prefix, only after maps 1 to 3 have produced both numbers and their journals. Nothing is wired to an exit code until then.
- **The dash past a sleeping group is real, and it is cavalry's.** On open ground, ordered pairs of tiles outside radius 4 a unit can connect where skirting the ball is strictly longer: 304 at Mov 6, 72 at Mov 4. Where skirting cannot be done in one turn at all: 240 at Mov 6, 128 at Mov 5, 64 at Mov 4. The L1 ball is a diamond, so crossing near a flat side is cheap. Section 8 stands as written.
- **The wake radius is not the dial for the dash.** The Mov-6 turn-saving count is 252 at radius 2 and 240 at every radius from 3 to 8. Mov is the dial. If play says the dash is too strong, do not raise the radius; look at who has Mov 6. A radius change only lengthens the approach.
- **The noise clause prices most of the dash.** Of the turn-saving dashes, those ending outside the noise radius (radius plus 2): 8 of 64 at Mov 4, 24 of 128 at Mov 5, 64 of 240 at Mov 6. The quiet-and-armed fraction rises with Mov, which is probably right. A fact for issue 13's authoring, not a proposal.
- **Where the play exists is a map lever.** A guard group in a corridor cannot be dashed past, only woken; the dash needs open ground on two sides of the group (Critic).

### Third round, second pass (2026-09-16, Chat's amendments to Code's two additions; issue 47)

Code's two leans on issue 47 are accepted, each with one amendment, and both are in issue 47's body. Nothing in 47 is wired to an exit code.

- **The free prefix prints beside `turn_limit - p90(winning turn)` as well as gate 1's median slack.** The prefix is defined by where the win rate separates from baseline, and win rate moves when the seeds near the limit flip, the right tail, not the median. Against the median alone a tempo-only failure sits well short of the slack and reads as content, in the direction that flatters the map. The p90 is the 200-seed number: with a 2 SE margin, the quantile that flips is about `1 - 2 * SE / w` with `w` the baseline win rate, which is 0.9 at 200 seeds and a 70 percent baseline and moves with the seed-count flag (Code).
- **At the boundary N only, the prefix arm re-runs with the turn limit raised by N**, against the unraised baseline. A drop that survives the refund is positional; passing under it means at most tempo, not none, since random moves sometimes advance and the refund is generous. About 200 runs, none on a map already free at the largest N.
- **Dead, quiet, live, with the wake tax printed beside the state.** Quiet is a binary and a binary that fires the same way on very different turns cannot be checked against a journal, the objection to the dead-turn counter turned on its author. So print the overlap of the reach set with a sleeping group's radius, count and fraction, taken from the player unit with the largest fraction. The leading run counts dead only. `turn of first quiet` is reported against a tax floor whose value waits on issue 13's journals. Denominator convention: the reach set as section 4 defines it, own tile included, since Wait is one of the tax-free choices; Chat's table below excludes it, which is why its totals run one short of `ironwake reach`.
- **Old Mill Road's wake tax at e1cdf91**, inside the mill group's Manhattan-4 union: 0 of 24 from 2,8; 2 of 33 from 6,6; 10 of 33 from 5,3; 14 of 33 from 6,3; 15 of 35 from 7,4, all infantry Mov 4; 10 of 56 from 3,3 and 17 of 64 from 5,3 at cavalry Mov 6. Reproduced by Code. 6,6 and 7,4 are both quiet by the bare rule and are not the same turn. A fact for issue 13.
- **Promotion, sharpened.** An instrument is validated by a map where its number is non-zero and the journal disagrees with it. Agreement on an instrument that fired is worth something; agreement on a silent one is worth nothing; disagreement is worth the most, because only it can show the number measures something other than what we believed when we wrote it.

### Fourth round (2026-09-16, the Critic's recommendation, Code, Chat; the front-loaded block)

The Critic said we agreed play settles what reasoning cannot and then scheduled play ninth. Code applied the lean; Chat lengthened the block by one issue. Numbers reproduced by Code at b3e1d32.

- **Play comes before EXP and inventory.** After the Critic's bugs: 5 with 31, then 6, then 7, then 10 and 11, then 8 and 9. Section 12's Phase 1 is done after 7 (a scripted skirmish resolves end to end); 11 is the first thing anyone can feel, and it unblocks the systems journals, the hit A/B, and the next Critic pass at once.
- **10 is in the block.** Without an enemy AI, three of Old Mill Road's four enemies (the mill group: two Guard, one Boss) behave exactly as they do with no AI at all, and the fourth, the aggressive brigand at 6,5, is the only unit whose choices make the approach a decision: it reaches 5,8 in one move (34 tiles) and 3,8 in the next (28 tiles), adjacent to Wren at 2,8, so with 10 it comes to the player line by enemy phase 2 and without it stands still for twenty turns. A build with 11 and not 10 reproduces the part of the map the Critic already called dead and fakes the part we wanted to feel.
- **Mechanics.** Issues 8 and 9 carry `blocked` with the reason in their bodies until both 10 and 11 merge; the run that merges the second of them removes the label. The label is doing real work: 8 and 9 are phase-1, 10 and 11 are phase-2, so without it the Builder's priority rule takes EXP and inventory first.
- **Order inside the block: 10 before 11**, agreed flat (Chat withdrew 11-then-10). It is the order the priority rule produces by itself (both phase-2, lower number first), so it needs no label. 10's acceptance test is a Core test and gets no easier with a CLI to watch it through; 11 built on 10 has a real enemy phase from its first commit and never carries an enemy-phase stub, which is a silent `item` that cannot even refuse out loud; and the eyeballing happens in the first play whichever lands first. Chat's own reason for folding: 11's acceptance script is a rout, so written against a still enemy phase every target in it assumes nobody moved, and it is rewritten the run 10 lands, leaving a scripted win against statues under `docs/transcripts/` in the meantime. 10 first writes that script once.
- **Stubs refuse out loud.** `item` prints a usage error naming the build and `help` lists it as unavailable, so no transcript ever carries a no-op that reads as a use. A `play` run prints one line at the top naming the systems the build lacks (EXP and level-ups, items); when 8 and 9 land the line goes away and old transcripts still say what they were. In issue 11's body.
- **The first two journal entries are systems entries, not Fun Gate entries.** Old Mill Road is a format sample, not an authored map; issue 13 has not run. The first cold entries judge the systems (does the forecast read honestly, is Recall a choice rather than a reroll, does the wake radius teach itself, is the dash findable) and say at the top which question they answer. The Fun Gate stays gated on 13. A low score on an unauthored map is not a verdict on the mechanics and is not grounds to tune a formula. Code's addition: the entries still record the approach turn by turn, because Old Mill Road's opening is the one place we already hold a dead-turn reading (turn 1: 21 destinations, nothing attackable), and issue 47's counter needs a journal on the same map to be checked against when it lands; the promotion rule cannot fire without one. Agreed by Chat.
- **The first run of the finished build is a debug pass, not a journal entry** (Chat). A scripted approach with no player attacks, committed as a transcript, asking only whether the enemy phase does what 10's arithmetic says: does the brigand come, does the mill group wake where the count says it wakes. Whatever it finds is fixed before either partner plays cold. Two reasons: cold play spends well only on a build we believe works, and an entry written on a build we then fix is a reading of nothing that the cold convention forbids rewriting; and the first time 10 is visible to a human eye should not be the first time anyone is trying to notice whether a turn was tense. The pass runs inside 11's PR, before it opens, so the transcript lands with the build it describes (Code; Chat's "first run of the finished build" withdrawn as phrasing).
- **The debug pair: the prediction is a committed file, ahead of the run** (Chat's correction of Code's mechanics; issue 11's body). A prediction typed into a PR description is composed with the output in hand, in a field anyone can edit later, and the artifact cannot say which came first. So the prediction is `docs/transcripts/<date>-<map>-<seed>-debug.prediction.md`, committed together with the script it predicts (`-debug.script`, since a prediction about scripted positions is unfixed without the script) in its own commit; the run's output lands as `-debug.txt` in the next commit. Git orders them; nothing is trusted. The `.txt` stays untouched machine output, per the transcripts README. Code's addition: the pair is a claim about section 8's arithmetic only up to first contact; from the first strike on, HP and so the scorer's kill line depend on the seed, so the prediction names its seed and says where the seed starts to matter.
- **Agreement is not the acceptance condition.** Issue 11's old line (enemy moves match the prediction) made agreement the pass and priced a divergence as a blocked acceptance with the prediction as the cheapest thing to change, the opposite of the promotion rule. The acceptance is that both files exist in that order and the PR description names every divergence. A divergence is a finding and routes one of three ways: a fault in 11 is fixed in the PR with a fresh prediction and re-run; a fault in 10 is a `bug` issue; a prediction wrong about section 8 gets a line saying so and the arithmetic is re-derived. Never an edited prediction; a prediction touched after its `.txt` exists is, by git, one that was not written first, and that is the only failure.
- **A fault in 10 found by the pass invalidates it.** The fix merges after 11, so the committed pair then describes an enemy phase that no longer happens, and cold play would be the first look at an unwatched build. The pass is done when it runs clean on the build both partners will play: the `bug` PR that fixes 10 carries a fresh pair, prediction first, so the transcript again lands with the build it describes (Code's mechanic, the same as for 11's PR). The old pair stays; two transcripts a merge apart, one showing the fault and one showing it gone, is the better record. Cold play waits on the clean pair.

### Fifth round (2026-09-16, Chat's approach rule; issue 10, DESIGN section 8)

Chat found that section 8 had no rule for the turns the debug pass is made of, and proposed one; Code checked the table and found a third tied tile. Written into section 8 now, ahead of 10's PR, so the Builder's review traces the issue against a document that already holds the rule.

- **Code's three debug-pass additions stand** (the script in the prediction's commit; the prediction names its seed and the turn where the seed starts to matter; the fresh pair inside the `bug` PR that fixes 10).
- **Section 8 had no approach rule.** Its tile rule is scoped to tiles that allow an attack, and the scoring formula needs a concrete attack, so on a turn when an Aggressive unit can attack nobody the only governing text was "moves toward". The debug pass is exactly those turns, so its likeliest divergence had no route in the three we named, and its cheapest resolution would have been to bless the implementation's pick after the run, the edit the prediction-first mechanism exists to prevent.
- **Predictable, not merely deterministic.** 10's "replays identically for the same seed" is satisfied by an implementation-ordered pick among tied tiles. DECISIONS/0012's contract applies: the choice cannot be an accident of the implementation. 10's acceptance now carries the Old Mill Road case as a named test.
- **The rule (Chat's, agreed):** target is the nearest player unit by path cost to an attack tile, ties by unit id; destination minimizes remaining path cost to an attack tile on that target; ties by avoid, then fewest player units whose reach set contains the tile, then (cost, row-major). Path cost, never Manhattan. Code's additions: no path means not a target and no target means Wait; "can reach the tile" in both tile rules means reach-set membership, so `ironwake reach` checks it; enemy units act in ascending unit id.
- **On Old Mill Road the tie is three-way and the rule says 3,6.** Chat's table held 5,8 and 4,7 and derived 4,7; Code's Dijkstra over the same map finds 3,6 too (remaining 2 via 3,7 or 2,6, Plain, in both player reach sets, cost 4 from 6,5), and row-major takes y=6. The mechanism worked before any code existed: two readers of one document, one wrong, caught by the third computation rather than by the run. Chat's earlier "reaches 5,8" was a reachability claim, not a destination.

### Engine and renderers (2026-09-14, both partners; DECISIONS/0008)

- The shipping front end is Godot 4 .NET, living in this repo. Unity is out. Unreal is not the main line.
- The deciding reason is review, not routines: Godot scenes are text, so both partners can read every front-end diff. Unreal's binary assets would make one partner blind for a whole phase. Unattended CI builds are the second reason.
- Ironwake.Core stays engine-free through Phase 3 and after. Any renderer is a consumer of the versioned state and event protocol (#25) and never carries rules. Reachable tiles, valid targets, and forecasts are queries answered by the core.
- The Godot project references Ironwake.Core directly and consumes the same event records in-process. The JSON line protocol is for out-of-process engines and bug reports. Both paths must agree, and the forecast honesty gate runs over the protocol too.
- Issue 11 builds the CLI as if the protocol existed: it renders only from events and asks the core for every number. Section 2 is not bent for convenience.
- An Unreal front end is welcome as Lotus's own second consumer of the protocol, driven from his desktop as a side line. It can become the shipped one by his ruling, not by a rewrite; Godot stays the CI-proven reference build.
- What would change it: Lotus saying he wants to learn Unreal by watching Code build in it through the bridge, with him present.

## Leaning, not agreed

- **One roll versus two.** Both partners lean one roll. Code's original reason (the display lies) is withdrawn: the display shows the resolved number under either scheme. The reason that stands is Chat's: under two rolls, forest buys 12 points of avoid against a raw-95 enemy and 32 against a raw-50 one, so cover is weakest exactly when it is needed most and section 4's table is never what you get. The A/B after issue 5 settles it. What it measures: roll sensitivity in the Sim (with keyed rolls, flip each high-odds miss or low-odds hit, replay, count runs whose outcome changed), paired by seed, both arms on tuned numbers. Whether the player had a positional answer is judged in the PLAYTEST entries, not by the Sim.
- **Rivalry's numbers.** As written, +10 crit at triple damage is a formation bonus the player will farm. Three arms, all data: as written; symmetric cost (crit avoid -10 while rival-adjacent, Code's lean); counter-only bonus (the fallback if exposure comes back low). The spike's questions are whether players cluster rivals on purpose and whether the rival-adjacent unit is then attacked.
- **The wake tax floor.** Somewhere between 6 percent (a diamond corner clipping a reach set) and 44 percent (half of everything you can do wakes the group). Set from issue 13's journals, like every other threshold in section 11.
