# 0025 — An ally's body stays a blocker in the exposure sum

Date: 2026-09-19. Design Table, thirteenth round. Chat measured the widening and proposed not taking it; Code agreed and called it settled rather than deferred. Recorded so neither partner builds the arm again.

1. **What was found.** Every captain death on Saltmarsh Ford at def5767, 28 of 28 across both schemes (10 under two rolls, 18 under one, the `captain` counts gate 1 has printed since #114), is one mechanism. Row 3 of the map is water with two crossings, at columns 3 and 10. A recruit standing in one of them is counted by `Exposure.Of` as a blocker, because `ReachOf` takes enemy reach on the board as it stands and a unit of the other side cannot be passed. The recruit dies inside the same cycle, the crossing opens, and the enemy behind it walks through into the captain. Seed 24 under two rolls, turn 3: the captain at 12 HP takes 8,4 on a no-crit sum of 11, `soldier-2` at 0 behind Teodor at 10,3; Teodor dies on his own counter, soldier-2 crosses, and the captain takes 5 from the archer and 8 from the soldier. Seed 31 under one roll is the same one step later, Teodor dying on the enemy phase to the archer before soldier-2 moves. Both traces reproduce on main with `--trace saltmarsh_ford <seed> --scheme <s>`.
2. **The arm, measured and not taken.** Enemy reach in the sum taken with the mover's own side removed, so no ally is ever a blocker. Chat ran it, 200 seeds, both schemes, three maps: captain deaths go to zero on Saltmarsh and every one of them becomes about two timeouts, 171 to 159 under two rolls and 126 to 110 under one, gate 1 failing at 55 percent under one roll; Old Mill Road and the Tollgate unmoved. The captain stops dying by refusing to cross. Constant-free and categorical, and it costs the map.
3. **Why it is settled and not deferred to play.** The rule under #117, #126, and #128 is certainty: the sum moves off the standing board only for what this command certainly determines, an enemy that is certainly dead, a group this command certainly wakes, a strike that certainly misses. An ally's death is never certain, because the enemy's choice of target sits inside the branch, and the veto's worst case runs over the enemy's strikes and never over the enemy's choices (seventh round). So no certain form of this widening exists, and the certainty rule has nothing to hand it; the arm is not a narrower version waiting to be found. What is left underneath is a Fun Gate question about Saltmarsh, whether a player can see the crossing about to open, and its answer changes the map, never the sum.
4. **What changes instead.** The veto is described as what it is: a worst case over the board at the moment of the command, moved only by what the command certainly determines, which the death of the captain's own screen inside the cycle can void. That sentence goes into `Exposure.Of`'s summary and DESIGN section 11's gate 1 line in #128's PR, in place of any wording that reads as a guarantee over the cycle, with a test named for the scope: an ally in a one-tile crossing, the enemy behind it priced at zero, asserted and commented as deliberate. Gate 1's captain-loss count is the instrument that shows the regime firing, and it was on the row for two rounds before either partner read it.
5. **Reopen only** if a hand play of Saltmarsh finds the reopening of a crossing invisible to the player before the move, and then the fix is content (the crossing's shape, what the forecast says about the body in it), not the sum.
