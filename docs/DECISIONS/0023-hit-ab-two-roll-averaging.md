# 0023 — The hit A/B: two-roll averaging

Date: 2026-09-19. Chat ran both arms on the real cast at 93c4dec (`--full --all --scheme two` against `--scheme one`, the same 200 seeds); Code reproduced every row and every content number. Agreed on the Design Table, ninth round.

1. **Two-roll averaging is the hit scheme.** It was already the default (DECISIONS/0013), so nothing in the build changes. Gate 1: Old Mill Road 129/200 against 120/200 (one standard error, nothing), Saltmarsh Ford 171/200 against 126/200 (five), the Tollgate 199/200 on both. The deciding number is gate 4's median paired drop as a share of the baseline's own wins, which is what the median recruit was necessary for: 95 percent under two rolls against 77 under one on Old Mill Road, 82 against 59 on Saltmarsh Ford. Under two rolls who you brought explains more of the outcome; under one roll the dice do. That is round one's criterion (how often the outcome turns on a roll the player had no positional answer to), and it points the opposite way from the lean both partners held.
2. **The obvious explanation is wrong and is recorded so it is not re-derived.** Two rolls do not favour the player because the player picks high-odds attacks. On plain ground the cast's raw hit against the enemies has a median of 92 and the enemies' against the cast a median of 86, so both sides live at the top of the band and both gain from averaging; one roll widens the player's edge (6 points against 2.6). The cost of one roll is that both sides miss more, fights take more strikes, and only the player is on a clock: Saltmarsh Ford's winning p90 moves 12 to 16 against a limit of 18. Whether the extra losses are timeouts or funerals is unreadable from gate 1's row, which reports winning turns only; #114 prints the loss breakdown.
3. **The terrain reason for one roll is withdrawn as overstated.** Two-roll averaging makes forest's +20 worth less than printed only above raw 85 (11.0 points from 96 to 76) and more below it (19.0 from 86 to 66, 23.8 from 80 to 60, 33.3 from 68 to 48); a fort's +30 breaks even at about raw 90. The cast's median matchup is raw 86, so at today's numbers cover delivers about what it says and under-delivers only where the honest answer was never cover. True as arithmetic, overstated as a design cost.
4. **The "three places" reason was retired by the build.** Forecast, resolver, and scorer call `Combat.Forecast`; the scheme is a switch inside the one hit function; gate 5 draws its scheme per combat, so both arms are proven honest by the same gate.
5. **`--scheme` stays in the build, and the Fun Gate is the reopen condition** (issue 15). Everything above is measured through the heuristic, a crude player, and says nothing about feel. If either partner's journal on maps 1 to 3 says the turns read as arithmetic rather than risk, one roll is the first dial and the flag makes it a one-word experiment. The flip instrument the old leaning entry named (flip each high-odds miss, replay, count changed outcomes) is not filed; the ablation share answered its question from the other side, and it is the first thing to build if the question reopens.
6. **Content findings for issue 13's second run, not formula changes:** the enemy raw-hit band against the cast comes down a few points rather than up (57 of 99 matchups sit above forest's break-even); Dunstan, the corridor unit, is the easiest unit in the cast to hit by both weapon kinds (raw 96 from `archer`, 94 from `hexer`), armor working against steel and a Res 1 hole against magic. In issue 13's body.
