## C: the margin

- **Who wants what.** Wren has filled her field book, so she has started tallying in the margins of whatever paper is nearest, and the nearest paper is one of Pell's books. She wants paper. Pell wants her book back with nothing added, and she wants Wren to understand why that matters, without having to say why.
- **The collision.** Pell finds a column of strokes down the margin of a copy she has carried since the school. Wren defends the tally: it's a count of the wall at the Grange, and it was right. Pell corrects it as a copyist reads it: the third group is four strokes and no cross. Wren is right (round 360): the fifth stroke is over the page, so she has written on two of Pell's pages, not one. Her first wrong number in front of Pell is B's. Wren, counting what Pell carries instead of answering, totals Pell's blank pages and their weight, out loud and to the ounce: a lot of paper to carry with nothing on it. That's her estimate joke, turned on Pell's satchel.
- **Left unsaid.** Why the field book is full: Wren has been copying her father's figures out of the Grange's originals since they opened, and the book filled. And why Pell carries blank quires at all: a school that burned teaches you to carry the paper you'll need.
- **Different by the end.** Pell takes a loose blank quire out of the satchel, from exactly the weight Wren just totalled, and hands it over without a word. The joke pays, and the scene ends on the quire (rule 9). It's the warmth, shown as an act and never said. No book is cut: Pell choosing a person over a page is her arc's turn, and C doesn't spend it.
- **Records touched.** Wren met (map 7). Both deployed through the support's climb (#77). No branch condition.
- **Average test.** Stock: an argument about respecting books that ends in mutual understanding and a hug of a line. Ours: a fight about margins that ends with Pell paying out the very paper Wren was mocking her for carrying.
- **Risks.** Wren's count on every line (her voice sheet's top risk), so one count in C, the satchel. Pell's correction as reflex: one correction in C, the tally.

## B: fourteen

- **Who wants what.** Pell has been copying Wren's field-book figures into a fair copy, since she can't stand the smudges. She wants the copy exact. Wren wants to be right, and on her father's east line especially.
- **The collision.** Pell reads a figure as fourteen where Wren has carried it as forty since the Grange. Wren is wrong (her voice sheet's flaw, kept light). And the fourteen moves a bearing on the east line, which changes which stone was the moved one. The comedy is Wren's refusal and then her recount, out loud, of something she can count, while Pell waits. The weight is that the joke and the stake are the same number.
- **Left unsaid.** Wren's secret: she held the chain for her father the season he set those stones, so if one was moved, she should have seen it. B gets close and doesn't say it. Her line goes short (her tell for being wrong), and Pell, who doesn't read people, misses the tell and corrects the next figure. The short line is the whole tell: nobody looks at the field book meaningfully, and nothing winks.
- **Different by the end.** Wren lets Pell write the corrected figure in Pell's own hand in the field book, and doesn't take the book back. For the first time Wren trusts somebody else's count. It ends on the field book in Pell's lap.
- **Records touched.** Wren met. The Grange's originals opened (map 7, the story's fact). No branch condition.
- **Average test.** Stock: "You're right... I was wrong." Ours: Wren never says she was wrong. She says the corrected number, once, and keeps counting.
- **Note.** Wren's voice-sheet sample 4 ("Then I've been wrong since the Grange...") is illustrative and does not ship. The prose writes this moment fresh, under the average test.

## A: the first stone (rare)

- **Who wants what.** The corrected bearing from B points at the oldest stone on the east line, one older than any Aldmere survey and too big for anyone to have moved. By A the Grange's originals have already proven her father's line as he set it (map 7), so that is not the want. Wren's want is her secret: she held the chain the season he set those stones, so if one was moved then, she should have seen it. A line run from the stone nobody could move, through her father's stones as he set them, settles it. If it runs true, the moved ones were moved after that season, not during it, and she didn't miss it. Pell wants to read the oldest stone's mark: it's cut in the old copyist's hand the shrine schools used for deeds and land grants. That's her trade, not the rite's script.
- **The collision.** They need each other's trade, and each has to admit it. Wren can find the stone and can't read it. Pell can read it and has never stood in a field long enough to find anything. The comedy is Pell outdoors: the wet, the distances, Wren counting her paces for her.
- **Left unsaid, then said.** The want forces the secret out: Wren can't ask Pell to check the line without saying why it matters, so she says it plainly, as a fact: she held the chain that season. Pell doesn't trade a past for it. She answers in her own shape (rule 4), the question Wren should have asked, more precisely: something with the force of "Which end of the chain were you holding?" It turns a confession back into survey, and from Pell that is the kindness, though she doesn't know it. Her own "won't say" stays hers, for her own story.
- **The turn.** Pell reads the mark aloud, as she reads anything, with no lecture on the hand (her sheet). The line runs true through the stones as set. Then she says "Wren" for the first time (rule 8, booked here). The scene ends on Pell writing the reading into the margin of the field book, the one margin she has ever written in on purpose (rule 9): the flaw breaking, which C held back for this.
- **Records touched.** Wren met; the support at A. No conditional line. Wren alive at the reseal is the ending's fact, not this scene's: the stones go into the keep's wall there (STORY), and this A is the reason the ending's line about the stones lands.
- **Average test.** Stock: two characters bond by sharing their tragic pasts. Ours: one says a fact, the other answers it with a surveying question, and the bond is that each writes in the other's book.
- **Risk.** A is the pair's heart and the Sim says most runs never see it. So C and B each stand alone as comedy with weight; nothing in them needs A to make sense. B shows the shape of the secret to players who stop there.
